Fed Poised for First Rate Hike Since 2023

  • Fed widely expected to raise rates 25 basis points (0.25%) today to 3.75%-4.00%, marking first hike since mid-2023.
  • CME FedWatch tool showed 92-93% probability of a hike priced in ahead of the 2 p.m. ET decision.
  • Decision comes despite President Trump's public pressure for rate cuts, raising Fed independence concerns.
  • New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh opposes forward guidance (signaling future rate direction), so FOMC statement will focus only on today's move, followed by a 2:30 p.m. press conference.
  • Strong jobs data and inflation stuck above the 2% target provide the economic rationale, though critics warn of stagflation risk (slow growth plus high inflation) since the hike doesn't address root causes like oil prices and geopolitical shocks.
  • 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to its highest level since 2007 on hike expectations, with some forecasts seeing yields reach 4.5%.
  • Historically, S&P 500 has averaged negative returns in the four months after a Fed hike before turning positive; 12-month average return post-hike is 6.7% (median 10.7%) across six tightening cycles since 1994, suggesting the hike alone is unlikely to derail the bull market.
  • Stock futures/markets have been choppy into the decision: S&P 500, Dow, and Nasdaq each fell 0.5-0.8% in the run-up, while futures rose after two prior down days; Bank of America separately warns stocks look 'overdue' for a pullback.

Jev: New 'System One' AI model class that can't hallucinate

  • Built by Diogo Almeida (ChatGPT co-creator) under TypeSafe/Jev, this model only selects from preset answer options instead of generating free text, eliminating hallucinations (false or fabricated outputs presented as fact) by design.
  • Priced at $42 per billion input tokens with free output — reported as 20-400x cheaper than comparable LLM (large language model) workflows and 238x cheaper than Claude Fable 5.1.
  • Responds in 70-500 milliseconds, roughly 20-200x faster than typical LLM workflows.
  • Trained with a technique called RLCD (Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions), giving it confidence scores that software can actually trust.
  • Intended for background decision-making inside software — fraud checks, ticket routing, record scoring — not conversation or general text generation; one report says it's currently code-only output.
  • Now in early access for developers; Almeida frames it as 'more like a database than a coworker,' suggesting it could become standard infrastructure inside apps rather than a chatbot competitor.

The Rundown AI Cites Jevons paradox (the idea that making something cheaper increases total usage) as the reason this could spread widely if cost/speed claims hold up.

TLDR AI Frames Jev as the first entrant in a new 'System One Models' category, named for fast/structured (vs. deliberative) decision-making, achieving LLM-level accuracy at 100x speed.

Superhuman (Zain Kahn) Notes the current version is limited strictly to code-only output, restricting use to software/technical tasks for now.

Chinese researchers' roadmap for recursive AI self-improvement
