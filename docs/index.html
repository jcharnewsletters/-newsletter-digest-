South Korea Moves to End Its 7-Year ICO Ban

  • South Korea implemented a broad ICO (Initial Coin Offering—a fundraising method where companies sell new cryptocurrencies to investors) ban in 2017 after high-profile fraud cases; the government now plans to lift restrictions and create a regulated framework.
  • New regulations will likely include investor protections, disclosure requirements, and registration processes for projects seeking domestic token fundraising.
  • The reversal reflects growing recognition that blanket bans push legitimate projects overseas while hindering domestic blockchain innovation.
  • South Korea's move could influence other Asian markets to adopt clearer ICO frameworks rather than outright prohibitions, signaling a broader shift from early regulatory overreach toward more nuanced, risk-based crypto oversight.

CFTC Allows Crypto to Count Toward Derivatives Platform Capital Requirements

  • CFTC staff advisory clarifies that bitcoin, ether, and payment stablecoins (cryptocurrencies pegged to stable assets like the US dollar, used for transfers) can count toward the one-year operating cost coverage component of financial resources requirements for derivatives platforms.
  • Firms must still maintain separate cash or highly liquid securities to satisfy liquidity requirements—crypto cannot fully replace traditional capital reserves.
  • The advisory provides regulatory clarity on native asset holdings without requiring a full rulemaking cycle.

Ethereum Foundation Doubles Security Team for Post-Quantum Upgrade

  • EF doubled its protocol security team, citing AI as a greater near-term risk than quantum computing since AI models now find production bugs faster than humans can review them.
  • Targeting December 2028 (community goal) with a 2029 internal deadline and January review, to replace vulnerable elliptic curve encryption with hash-based signatures (a quantum-resistant cryptographic method).
  • Three planned changes: hash-based validator keys for consensus, a migration path to hash-based wallet signatures, and hash-based blob commitments; EF warns the upgrade is harder than the Merge and will require updates to BLS tooling, wallets, multisigs, and staking pools.
