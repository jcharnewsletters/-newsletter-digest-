1. 30-year Treasury yields at 2007 highs squeeze bitcoin's value case

  • The 30-year U.S. Treasury yield (interest rate on government bonds maturing in 30 years) is at its highest since 2007, matching the setup of multi-decade uptrends from the 1960s–1990s that preceded major crashes.
  • Rising risk-free rates (guaranteed returns on government debt) compete directly with stocks and risk assets for capital — the same dynamic that culminated in Black Monday 1987, a 22.6% single-day Dow decline.
  • Bitcoin has no earnings or cash flow to justify its price against 30-year real yields (returns above inflation) near 20-year highs, unlike equities that can grow earnings to offset higher opportunity cost.
  • Fidelity's Jurrien Timmer says $500,000–$1M bitcoin targets look stretched in this environment; BTC's implied volatility index bounced off historical support, a bearish signal alongside spot weakness.

CMC Spotlight 1

1. Vitalik adds privacy and quantum safety to Ethereum's roadmap

  • Buterin proposed encrypted transactions that hide sender, recipient, and amount while preserving consensus-layer security, targeting mainnet in 2–3 years.
  • Privacy design leans on zero-knowledge proofs (cryptography that proves something is true without revealing the underlying data) so validators can verify without seeing details.
  • Post-quantum cryptography (encryption designed to withstand quantum computers, which could break today's elliptic-curve signatures) will be added via migration pathways rather than immediately retiring current schemes.
  • Positions Ethereum as the first major Layer 1 (base blockchain) to formally address quantum risk in its long-term architecture.
  • Rollout requires coordination across wallets, apps, and stakers (users locking ETH to validate transactions for rewards), with possible short-term network activity spikes during transitions.

Meta releases Muse Glimmer, an open-weight agentic model, alongside Zuckerberg's superintelligence manifesto

  • Muse Glimmer is a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model (Meta publishes the model files so anyone can download, run and modify it locally instead of calling Meta's servers), released under the permissive Apache 2.0 license.
  • It is optimized for on-device agents and coding, and beats comparable small models such as Gemma4 and Qwen3.6 on agentic, coding and reasoning benchmarks.
  • Quantized versions (quantization stores the model's numbers at lower precision to shrink memory use while keeping most capability) run on a single consumer GPU with 18GB of memory.
  • Zuckerberg published a ~6,500-word essay ('The Future is for Everyone') arguing superintelligence should belong to everyone and that concentrated AI power is 'inherently problematic'.
  • Meta frames local models as less data leaving your machine, offline capability, and customization without vendor permission — and as the U.S. answer to China's open-model dominance.
  1. The Rundown AI: Meta says weights for the stronger Muse Spark 1.2 will be released 'soon,' which would make it the top open-source rival to China's ecosystem.
  2. The Neuron: Critic Aaron Scher warns that distributing autonomous systems widely creates its own safety problems.
  3. Staying Ahead: Describes Muse Glimmer as a lightweight open-source image model that runs on personal computers.

1. AI deployment is not AI adoption

  • Anthropic put Claude Cowork in front of thousands of employees and productivity barely moved.
  • Organizations split three ways: power users chaining skills daily, casual users, and a silent majority who never engage.

Lenny's Newsletter 1

1. Three founder archetypes for making people care about your startup

  • Problem, Insight, and Vision founders map to three emotional connections — identification, trust, and inspiration — each needing a distinct communication strategy.
  • The Problem founder (solving personal pain) should use vivid scene-based vignettes, e.g. "crying in my car during lunch," instead of "existing options weren't working"; Sara Blakely and Dave Gilboa are the models.
  • Problem-founder pitfalls: slipping into CEO-speak about market gaps, or diluting impact by oversharing unrelated personal details.
  • The Insight founder builds trust by giving away industry analysis generously so audiences feel smarter — explain reasoning, not just conclusions; Aaron Levie and Patrick Collison exemplify it.
  • Insight-founder pitfalls: hot takes with no reasoning, false certainty, or making people feel stupid; make only predictions you're confident in and admit uncertainty.
  • The Vision founder challenges the status quo with a concretely described radical future, needing repetition and evidence to avoid seeming naive.
  • Most founders' block is "what to say," not "whether to write" — knowing your why for starting the company is the foundation.

3. How to run agents overnight without producing slop

  • Agents produce slop from missing context — they can't inherit unwritten tribal knowledge from standups and code reviews, so they guess at machine speed.
  • The Meta Harness system treats documentation as machine-readable code: a git-tracked project record of what's true now, what you intend to build, and what happened.
  • Break work into epics (outcomes), specs (how engineering gets there), and tickets (one unit an agent can finish), each with acceptance criteria that tell the agent when to stop.
  • Add a validation loop (a second model checking output against acceptance criteria) before human review; prep tickets before bed — the prepared ticket, not the overnight timing, is the trick.
