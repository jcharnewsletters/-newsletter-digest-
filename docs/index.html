PG&E and Edison Shares Plunge on California Wildfire Liability Bill

  • PG&E shares fell 17-20% (sources differ) after developments around California's wildfire liability legislation (Senate Bill 492).
  • The bill includes executive compensation limits for utilities causing wildfires, fast-pay victim compensation programs, and a ban on private equity firms purchasing wildfire claims.
  • Utility-caused equipment has sparked at least seven California wildfires in two decades, including the 2018 Camp Fire (85 deaths) and 2025 Eaton Fire (19 deaths); PG&E filed for bankruptcy in 2019 over wildfire liabilities.
  • Wildfire-related charges add an estimated $20-$40 monthly (about 14%-19%) to PG&E customer bills, and the long-term solvency of the state's Wildfire Fund remains unresolved.
  • Analysts downgraded utility stocks to Neutral, citing insufficient investor protections and open-ended liability exposure that complicates financing and capital planning.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Bill requires two-thirds legislative approval and takes effect upon governor's signature; framed as a compromise between Gov. Newsom's original proposal and legislative pushback, with a vote scheduled for Tuesday.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Reports that lawmakers failed to pass the wildfire liability reform, contradicting Seeking Alpha's framing of an upcoming vote, and cites a 17% stock decline rather than 20%.

Runway's Solaris: AI-Generated Video Replaces Website Code

  • Solaris pairs Runway's Gen-4.5 video model with an LLM (large language model, an AI trained to understand and generate text/decisions) to render interactive web interfaces frame-by-frame as live video instead of using traditional HTML/CSS code.
  • Runway calls this an 'Interface World Model'—it reads each user click or drag, decides what happens next, and streams the result in real time with no intermediate code representation.
  • In head-to-head evaluator tests (250 evaluators, per The Neuron), Solaris beat Claude Opus 5-coded interfaces in 61% of instruction-following comparisons and 71% of natural in-scene behavior comparisons.
  • Demo use cases include virtual try-ons, building salads by dragging ingredients, and interactive physics simulations.
  • Current limitations: text legibility issues, session drift over long interactions, occasional hallucinated but convincing-looking screens, and new reliability challenges since there are no pre-built buttons/screens (e.g., a button must behave identically five clicks later).
  • Product is in early access, request-only.

Superhuman (Zain Kahn) ByteDance's competing video model Seedance 2.5 (recently added to Runway) generated 7M+ views for its announcement video due to its visual realism.

TLDR AI Solaris could enable dynamic agent-training environments by removing the need for intermediate code representations.
