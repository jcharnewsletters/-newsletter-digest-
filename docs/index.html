July CPI inflation report lands today — the week's key Fed catalyst

  • The July Consumer Price Index (CPI — the government's main gauge of what households pay for goods and services) is out at 8:30 a.m. ET.
  • Consensus looks for a 0.1% monthly rise and headline inflation cooling to 3.4% year over year.
  • A soft print would ease pressure on the Federal Reserve and, per Yahoo Finance, could rule out a September rate hike; a sticky print pushes bond yields up and pressures stock valuations.
  • Traders are split between 'sticky' (persistent) inflation that delays cuts and cooling prices that justify aggressive reductions, so S&P 500 and bond markets are unusually sensitive to a surprise.
  • A Producer Price Index (PPI — wholesale prices charged by producers) report follows Thursday.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Argues the report may pass without major volatility yet still reveal why interest rates are headed even higher.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Notes investors have been hedging (buying downside protection) into the release, with inflation still above the Fed's 2% target.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg, Investopedia Pre-Market

AI earnings from CoreWeave and Super Micro revive the AI trade

  • CoreWeave (a cloud provider that rents out GPU computing power for AI) more than doubled quarterly revenue to $2.58 billion and disclosed a $104 billion revenue backlog.
  • CoreWeave added a fresh $21 billion Meta contract on top of an existing $14 billion commitment running through 2032; the stock jumped about 13% after hours.
  • Super Micro Computer surged on strong guidance, with both names riding AI data-center demand.
  • S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures rose 0.3% and 0.7% after two down days, as tech beats lifted sentiment.
  • Micron and TSMC echoed the message that demand for AI-related memory and chips remains astronomical; South Korean shares rallied on Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: About one-third of tech companies have raised full-year guidance and CoreWeave narrowed losses with 'expanding operating leverage' (profits improving faster than revenue), prompting the question of whether tech earnings ended the selloff.
  2. Investopedia Pre-Market: Cautions the tech sector stays volatile despite the beats, reflecting doubts about valuations and growth sustainability.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg, Investopedia Pre-Market

Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps oil traders guessing

  • Trump claims the US has 'total control' over the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint between Iran and Oman that carries roughly 20% of global oil shipments.
  • Iran says the strait stays closed until its conditions are met, while US Energy Secretary Wright claims Persian Gulf oil exports are near pre-war levels.
  • Oil prices wavered as traders doubted a near-term restoration of flows, with Washington–Tehran talks deadlocked.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Data centers push into US neighborhoods

  • The share of US home sales within 5 miles of a large data center doubled since 2018 to 1.5% of 2024 sales, with no measurable hit to home values yet.
  • 53% of Americans oppose nearby data center construction over electricity and water use, noise and aesthetics; New York, Texas and other states have enacted temporary moratoriums.
  • Wall Street Breakfast notes data center growth is also driving job creation and rising home values in tech-heavy counties.

Covered by: Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha)

Market breadth widens beyond mega-cap tech

  • The equal-weight S&P 500 (which gives each company the same weight rather than sizing by market value) is outperforming the market-cap weighted index, showing gains spread across segments.
  • Investopedia flags sector rotation as tech retreats from highs and other sectors gain ground, raising the question of whether the market is structurally stronger.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Investopedia Pre-Market

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 2

1. New York City targets Amazon's subcontracting model

  • The Delivery Protection Act, from Council member Tiffany Cabán and backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani, would force last-mile delivery operators to directly employ workers instead of using third-party subcontractors.
  • It creates a licensing system for warehouses and distribution facilities, sets minimum safety and training standards, and makes companies liable for worker protections at their own sites.
  • Amazon says it would have to relocate delivery operations outside the city; Amazon and allies spent over $5 million campaigning against the bill.
  • If passed it could be a template for other cities regulating Amazon, the second-largest US private employer, and would likely face legal challenges like NYC's blocked pied-à-terre tax.

2. Other corporate and policy moves

  • Oracle is planning another round of layoffs this month while spending billions on AI infrastructure.
  • Google's Gemini app passed 1 billion users as competition with ChatGPT intensifies.
  • The Pentagon opened the Golden Dome portal to broaden bids on the $185 billion program; Northrop unveiled its Raid Hunter air defense system and Lockheed pushed hypersonic missiles.
  • Senator Sanders called for freezing AI development; Oracle's Ellison threatened to pull Paramount out of California absent a settlement; CME will launch index-based hockey futures in September.

CoinDesk 3

1. XRP hovers at $1 with record leverage ahead of CPI

  • XRP briefly touched 99 cents Tuesday — its lowest since November 2024 — before bouncing to $1.02, where the recovery stalled.
  • Open interest in XRP futures (total value of outstanding leveraged bets) hit 2.67 billion XRP ($2.73 billion), the highest since October.
  • July CPI (Consumer Price Index, the main US inflation gauge) is forecast at 0.1% monthly growth and 3.4% year-on-year; a hot print could push XRP below $1 toward 92-cent support.
  • Options markets price only a 1.3% post-CPI move for bitcoin, the kind of complacency that often precedes surprises.

2. Hacker drains $200,000 from XRP bridge via fake deposits

  • Attacker manipulated the bridge's relayer code (software that verifies and relays transfers between blockchains) to log deposits that never delivered XRP to the reserve wallet.
  • The bridge issued valid receipt tokens for the empty deposits, and 17 of 28 relayers approved payouts because their records showed legitimate deposits.
  • The exploit ran 97 minutes on August 9, caused by relayer code processing bridge memos without verifying the destination address.
  • Operator tx halted the bridge, patched the flaw and hired forensics specialists; ChangeNOW froze part of the stolen funds as they moved through multiple addresses.

3. Fidelity files to add staking and quarterly dividends to $898M ether ETF

  • Fidelity's Ethereum Fund (FETH) could stake up to 100% of its ether (locking tokens to help validate transactions in exchange for rewards), keeping some liquid for redemptions.
  • The fund would keep 85% of gross staking rewards after paying node operators Blockdaemon, Figment and Galaxy, with net rewards covering expenses then paid out quarterly in cash.
  • An IRS safe harbor from November 2025 permits crypto trusts to stake without losing the grantor-trust tax status ETFs rely on; Grayscale and 21Shares already added staking and BlackRock launched a separate product.
  • The change turns ether ETFs into dividend-paying products, a structural edge over bitcoin (which cannot be staked) while ether underperforms.
CMC Spotlight 1

1. SEC to propose a token safe harbor

  • The SEC plans a framework setting objective conditions tokens must meet to avoid being classified as securities (investments subject to strict registration rules).
  • Qualifying tokens could operate without registration while still meeting investor-protection standards.
  • It marks a shift from defining crypto's boundaries through enforcement actions toward proactive rule-making.
  • Backers argue clear rules could reverse the exodus of projects that delayed launches or moved offshore, and may become a template for regulators abroad.
TLDR Crypto 6

1. Vitalik Buterin's Ethereum 2026 'Strawmap' adds seven priorities

  • Quantum safety becomes a first-class priority, backed by leanSPHINCS signatures and post-quantum aggregation against future quantum-computer attacks.
  • Native rollups arrive via maturing SNARKs (cryptographic proofs that verify a computation without revealing its data), plus blob and gas futures for dynamic pricing of blockchain resources.
  • zkzk frames would expose non-EVM instruction sets (leanISA, RISC-V) at protocol level, demoting the EVM (Ethereum's standard computation engine) from enshrined feature to intermediate representation.
  • Privacy gets protocol-level treatment (keyed nonces, FOCIL, lean privacy pools, wormholes) and recursive STARKs span execution, consensus and data layers.
  • State expiry is dropped for specialized state types tuned to token transfers, swaps and privacy; VDFs (verifiable delay functions) and many EVM tweaks are deprioritized.

2. EIP-8363 staking taper could destabilize onchain credit markets

  • The proposal tapers consensus rewards to zero net issuance (no new ETH minted for validation) at 60.25M ETH staked, about 50% of supply, cutting validator income roughly 50%.
  • MEV (maximal extractable value — profits validators earn by ordering or including transactions strategically) is untouched, so it would become a larger share of validator revenue.
  • LST looping (borrowing stablecoins against liquid staking tokens to re-stake and amplify yield) drives most ETH lending yield, yet the proposal models no downstream effect on borrowers repaying WETH debt or deposit APYs.
  • LSTs are ~40% of onchain TVL (total value locked), $2.6B of stablecoin debt is backed by ETH/LSTs, and $11.4B of ETF and treasury inflows hold 7.8M ETH.
  • Post-Merge net ETH issuance is already 0.30%/year versus Bitcoin 0.82%, gold 1.7% and M2 money supply 5.5%; authors urge modeling MEV burn first.

3. Mastercard closes $1.8B BVNK deal as on-chain growth plateaus

  • BVNK brings $23B in trailing twelve-month settlement volume to Mastercard.
  • On-chain flow growth slowed from 2.6x year-over-year in Q1 to 1.6x in Q2.
  • Ethereum handles 62% of the volume while Layer 2 chains (networks built atop Ethereum for cheaper transactions) remain immaterial for payment settlement.

4. Robinhood Chain leads L2 revenue but barely moves the needle

  • Launched July 1, Robinhood Chain led all tracked Layer 2s with $3.6M revenue in its first month.
  • Annualized chain revenue is only ~$54.8M ($43.2M protocol, $10.5M USDG yield on a $333.1M market cap, plus a Lighter perps share) — about 14% of Robinhood's $400M annualized crypto revenue.
  • Robinhood's Q2 2026 crypto revenue fell 38% year-over-year to $100M, an all-time-low 8% of total revenue, with retail crypto volume at its weakest since Q3 2024.
  • Memecoins are 51% of onchain spot volume versus 5% for real-world assets; Morpho, integrated into the main app, became the chain's 3rd-largest TVL deployment in a month.
  • Lighter's wallet-only perpetuals integration drew just 0.2% of its volume despite $25M in LIT incentives, showing distribution beats incentives.

5. Bitcoin recovery hinges on seller exhaustion, not new demand

  • Bitcoin ETFs saw net $5B of outflows over 52 weeks, with front-end implied volatility at multi-year lows and futures basis unable to clear T-bill yield parity.
  • On-chain metrics sit in deep-value territory: MVRV (market value to realized value — how far price sits above holders' average purchase price) is at accumulation levels and spot turnover is at all-time lows.
  • The DAT (digital asset trust) supply overhang is unwinding as treasury companies slow or reverse BTC buying, while long-term holders absorb supply.
  • BTC remains pinned below its 200-week exponential moving average; large asset manager allocations are the main upside catalyst.
  • Suggested playbook: dollar-cost-average (invest fixed amounts on a schedule) into spot or buy cheap implied volatility via options.

6. Crypto payment card volume hits $759M in July

  • Volume rose 2.5x year-over-year from $306M, across nearly 9M transactions averaging $86 each, up from under $1M when tracking began in October 2023.
  • Settlement concentrates on Optimism (29%), Solana (19%) and Base (19%).
  • USDC handles 58% of card spend and USDT 26% (from 48% and 7% a year ago), while euro stablecoin EURe collapsed from 88% in early 2024 to ~2%.
  • Cards convert stablecoins to local fiat at checkout over Visa rails, giving holders dollar access without a bank account, though volumes remain far below legacy card networks.
  • USDT supply shrank $4B in 60 days, including $870M in the past 11 days, hinting at tightening stablecoin liquidity.
