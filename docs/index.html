Binance Hacked for $6.3 Billion in Largest Crypto Heist Ever

  • Hackers stole $6.3 billion from Binance, the largest cryptocurrency exchange heist in history.
  • Attackers reportedly gained access via a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop and executed authorized transfers across 10 different blockchains.
  • Blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs attributes the attack to TraderTraitor, North Korea's elite hacking unit, with 'high confidence'.
  • Binance froze roughly $3 billion of the stolen funds on-chain and suspended withdrawals while conducting a security overhaul.
  • Binance pledged to cover all user losses from operational reserves, future earnings, and emergency funds, asserting 'user funds are safe'.
  • Experts say most stolen tokens are unlikely to be recovered, though frozen stablecoins (cryptocurrencies pegged to stable assets like the US dollar) could be clawed back if issuers cooperate.
  • Bitcoin fell as much as 5% to around $77,000 (its lowest since September) before paring losses to under 2%; Ethereum and Ripple also sold off.
  • Industry fears the hack could trigger a broader confidence crisis in crypto similar to the FTX and Mt. Gox collapses, though some analysts believe the impact could be short-lived if Binance restores operations smoothly.
  • Regulatory scrutiny is expected to intensify, potentially affecting crypto market access and custody standards, especially after the midterm elections.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Links to TRM Labs' detailed technical report and notes potential for congressional scrutiny after the midterms.

Bloomberg Cites Ballet, a digital-asset hardware wallet firm, saying stolen tokens will likely be impossible to recover.

Big Tech Earnings: Strong Results Met With Steep Sell-Offs

  • Amazon grew net sales 12% to $201.9B with AWS (Amazon Web Services, its cloud computing division) up 39%\u2014the fastest growth in five years\u2014and record operating margin of 13.9%, yet shares fell 4-15% after hours on concerns over $220B in planned capital expenditures (capex).
  • Intel's revenue climbed 21% to $16.5B with data-center/AI sales up 78% to $7.3B, but weak Q4 guidance ($15-16B revenue, gross margin falling to 38% from 44.1%) sent shares plunging.
  • Alphabet (Google's parent) revenue grew 25% to $127.6B but the stock cratered as much as 12%, its worst day since its 2004 IPO, partly because AI search tools are cannibalizing traditional ad revenue and free cash flow turned negative (-$9.06B) after $51.35B in capex.
  • Caterpillar fell 10.8% despite 17% sales growth and a record $78.1B order backlog, simply for reiterating (not raising) prior guidance.
  • The common thread: investors have already priced in massive AI infrastructure spending, so they're now punishing any sign that returns on that spending may be slow, costly, or uncertain.
  • Broader market effect: Nasdaq futures/index fell roughly 1.4-2.4%, S&P 500 dropped about 0.3-1%, as the tech-led sell-off dragged down smaller semiconductor and software names too.
  • Microsoft, by contrast, won analyst praise and raised $25 billion in corporate bonds to fund its own AI infrastructure buildout, while its stock held up better than peers.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Frames this as a repeating pattern across four major companies (Alphabet, Amazon, Intel, Caterpillar) all tumbling despite revenue beats, and ties it to macro data: Q3 GDP accelerated to 3.4% annualized (from 2.2%), core PCE inflation at 3.1% YoY, and jobless claims fell to 194,000.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief Highlights that Google's AI assistant directly competes with its own search ads, creating a cannibalization problem unique among the 'hyperscalers' (large cloud infrastructure companies); also notes YouTube now gets over one-third of its ad revenue from living-room TV viewing and leads U.S. streaming watch time, with Instagram launching 90-minute video uploads to challenge it.

Investopedia Pre-Market Frames the earnings misses mainly as a warning sign for a 'challenging day ahead for tech-heavy portfolios' in pre-market trading.
