▸ Lenny's Newsletter 4
01

Why the permanent-underclass fear from AI is overstated

  • Anish Acharya (a16z) argues history shows workers adapt and new roles emerge when technology disrupts industries, rather than becoming permanently displaced
  • The car industry replaced horses but generated millions of new jobs in manufacturing, infrastructure, and services—AI is expected to follow the same pattern
  • Transition costs are real; policy should focus on retraining and support during job shifts rather than assuming permanent job loss
02

Companies restructuring into 'loops'

  • Every job function (sales, customer support, etc.) is becoming a repeatable loop: input data, AI processes it, human reviews output, and the loop tightens with each iteration
  • This has two effects: individual productivity multiplies as humans leverage AI agents, and roles compress/consolidate as loops handle more work with fewer people
  • Winning companies identify which loops to build and layer them into their DNA rather than resisting automation
03

The '/loop, make me happier' consumer AI opportunity

  • Most current consumer AI apps (e.g., ChatGPT) are transactional and stateless—no memory or personalization loop keeps users coming back
  • The winning formula: AI learns user preferences → delivers more personalized output → user engages more → AI improves further, creating a habit-forming feedback loop
  • Examples of apps with strong retention loops: Spotify (learns music taste) and social feeds (algorithm shows preferred content), unlike current AI chat tools
04

Distribution as the new moat in AI

  • Technology parity is fleeting in the AI era because open-source models and accessible APIs (application programming interfaces, ways for software to talk to each other) quickly level the playing field
  • Companies with direct user relationships, network effects, and brand loyalty will outcompete rivals with better technology but weaker distribution
  • Airbnb's moat wasn't its algorithm but direct access to hosts/guests and brand trust—the same builder/user relationship pattern is expected to matter more than model quality in AI
