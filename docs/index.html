Boom in leveraged and derivatives-based ETFs

  • Assets in derivatives-based ETFs (exchange-traded funds that use financial contracts to amplify or alter returns) rose over 450% since October 2022, versus 250% growth in standard equity ETFs.
  • Issuers launched 3,363 new ETFs over the period, with more than 30% of 2026 launches using leverage (borrowed exposure that multiplies gains and losses), reflecting aggressive issuer marketing rather than proven demand.
  • Leveraged and inverse ETFs are also closing at a record pace, as Wall Street culls underperforming products while pushing new ones — a sign of herd-like risk appetite.
  • Historically such product booms are risk-appetite indicators rather than market-top signals; leveraged ETFs debuted in 2006, over a year before the 2007 peak, and markets kept rising.
  • Most leveraged ETFs reset daily, creating 'volatility drag' — losses from daily rebalancing when prices swing up and then reverse — which can leave holders worse off than the underlying asset's actual performance, especially in choppy markets.
  • A new European ETP (exchange-traded product) offers exposure to Anthropic's pre-IPO valuation without owning actual shares, instead using notes and swaps tied to Binance perpetual futures, adding complexity and counterparty risk.

Platform Strategy Split: Open APIs vs. Locked Proprietary

  • Shopify extended direct backend API access to agents from any provider (Town, Hermes, Instinct, Dots), following an open 'headless' model that decouples frontend from backend systems.

Gemini 4 Argon: Frontier Benchmarks, Unclear Purpose

  • Google released Argon on a new pretrain; it achieves state-of-the-art results on knowledge-work benchmarks (Vals Index, Harvey's Legal Agent) and leads Fable 5.1 by 13 points, with a 50% API discount bringing benchmark cost to $1.99 per task through November 30.

Banking and Third-Party AI Agents: The Liability Problem

  • JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned that customers using external agents face liability transfer\u2014the bank won't indemnify (legally protect) faulty third-party agents the way it does fraud.
  • JPMorgan blocks agents like Muse and Instinct from logging in with banking credentials; read-only budgeting via open banking APIs is permitted, but transaction settlement and bill-payment are blocked.
  • An alternative approach gaining traction: scoped permissions (limited access rights), hard spending limits, and kill switches rather than outright lockout.
  • The core tension: agents need authenticated account access to be useful, but banks face liability if an agent hallucinates (produces false information) and empties an account\u2014no clear industry settlement yet.

ChatGPT Habits Freeze Fast
