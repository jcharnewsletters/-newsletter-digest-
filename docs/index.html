Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Faces Board Investigation Over Alleged Chinese Intelligence Ties

  • Huang admitted Sunday to an affair with Alice Huang (also known as Huang Le), a Megaspeed executive identified by U.S. officials as connected to Chinese intelligence.
  • Megaspeed, a Singapore-based computer facility operator, purchased over $4.8 billion in Nvidia chips across two years; Nvidia kept selling to them even after a Commerce Department investigation began.
  • Nvidia's board formed an independent director committee with outside counsel to investigate potential security and governance risks, including classified-information access and conflict-of-interest concerns.
  • Huang remains CEO during the probe; no wrongdoing has been confirmed, and he's seen as unlikely to fully depart, though a shift to executive chairman (a senior role retaining strategic influence without day-to-day CEO duties) is possible given investor and political support.
  • Donald Trump called Huang a "fine person" and said he should remain CEO.
  • Nvidia shares fell in premarket/Friday trading on the news, while the broader Nasdaq and S&P 500 futures still advanced, suggesting markets are compartmentalizing the risk rather than broadly selling off tech.
  • The episode adds scrutiny to semiconductor industry security practices and export-control enforcement amid U.S.-China tensions, with possible implications for Nvidia's government contracts.
