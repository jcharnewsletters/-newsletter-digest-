Claude's AI-Driven Biology Breakthrough: Novel Enzyme System 'ART'

  • Anthropic deployed roughly 950 Claude agents in parallel, searching DNA databases for about 21-24 hours using 210 million tokens to hunt for reverse transcriptases (RTs—molecular copy machines that convert RNA into DNA).
  • The search flagged 3,500 candidates, narrowed to 20, then to one: an enzyme with unusual repeating DNA patterns reminiscent of how CRISPR (a gene-editing tool that cuts and copies precise DNA segments) was first noticed.
  • Lab tests confirmed the system—named ART—produces small RNAs and appears to combine cutting, copying, and pasting DNA functions, but its exact biological role and applications remain unknown pending further research.
  • CEO Dario Amodei said the work was mostly Claude's, with humans choosing the research direction and running wet-lab validation; he compared the result to publishable PhD-level biology research.
  • This marks a milestone for AI-driven science, with models going from failing basic math in 2023 to tackling open biology questions in 2024-2026.

The Rundown AI Claude now leads 26% of Anthropic's AI research tasks end-to-end (up from under 1% in February), and the company runs ~30K AI agents with only 1 in 47,000 actions blocked; full autonomy in production remains at zero.

The Neuron Provides a step-by-step account of the discovery process, noting Claude didn't invent a new tool but flagged something unusual enough to warrant lab testing—humans still chose the search area and validated results.
