Nasdaq plunges into correction as tech/AI selloff wipes trillions in value, futures attempt rebound Friday

  • Nasdaq Composite fell approximately 5.2%-5.5% Thursday, its worst day since the March 2020 COVID crash, erasing between $2.3 trillion and $2.5 trillion in market value.
  • Decline pushed Nasdaq into correction territory (a 10% drop from recent highs), reversing Wednesday's post-midterm relief rally.
  • S&P 500 fell 4.35%-4.4% Thursday; chip and AI infrastructure stocks led losses, with the PHLX Semiconductor Index down about 12%.
  • Qualcomm's disappointing profit forecast triggered contagion: Qualcomm and Nvidia each dropped over 9%, AMD and Micron posted double-digit declines (Micron down as much as 17%), and SanDisk fell 22%.
  • Selloff driven by fears that the Democratic midterm sweep will slow AI data center permitting, funding, and power approvals.
  • Retail investors abandoned their usual 'buy the dip' strategy, dumping $338M net from single stocks Thursday—the third-largest liquidation day on record—while volatility-target funds forced $42B-$48B in systematic rebalancing.
  • VIX (a market volatility/fear gauge) surged about 70% to 28.18, its biggest one-day percentage jump since December 2024, even larger than the move during the March 2020 crash.
  • Asian markets fell in tandem: Japan's Nikkei entered a bear market (down 20% from peak) below 60,000, South Korea's Kospi dropped 6% below 5,000, and Taiwan's TAIEX fell 4.5%, with TSMC (40%+ of the index) down 5%.
  • Nvidia faces comparisons to Cisco's dot-com-era overexpansion, with analysts questioning unlimited AI growth plans amid power and capital constraints; Anthropic's $1.5 trillion IPO valuation is also drawing investor skepticism.
  • Hedge funds suffered widespread losses as crowded trades unwound, with Goldman Sachs' trading desk describing Thursday's panic selling as the 'exact opposite' of Wednesday's rally-driven activity.
  • Dutch bank ING sold roughly $500 million in data center loans, reflecting bank concern that the new regulatory environment could slow AI infrastructure buildout.
  • Friday morning showed tentative stabilization: Nasdaq futures rose about 1%-1.5% on a semiconductor rebound, though Dow futures fell and S&P futures were only modestly positive, with hyperscalers (large, diversified cloud providers like Google, Microsoft, and Amazon) holding up better than chipmakers due to diversified revenue.

CNBC Morning Squawk Framed the premarket bounce as a sign investors are finding selective opportunities in beaten-down chip and cloud names despite broader uncertainty.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Treasury yields rose 2 basis points to 4.86%, Bitcoin fell 5.3% to $76,031, crude oil rose 0.5% to $90.95, and gold declined 0.8% to $4,080 amid the selloff.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Historical data shows the S&P 500 traded higher one month after each of the five biggest prior VIX percentage spikes, suggesting the panic could be short-lived.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief Called it the worst post-midterm trading day since 1896, and noted the selloff creates conflicting narratives—hurting chip suppliers while potentially helping big tech's margins by curbing capex.

Bloomberg Paraguay's Ueno Bank collapse (tied to a $25M loan slice backing the OpenAI/SoftBank/Oracle Stargate data center project) forced President Santiago Peña's resignation, with Oracle citing force majeure to developers.

Investopedia Pre-Market Highlighted investor focus shifting toward portfolio protection strategies and rules-based downturn playbooks given concentrated AI exposure.

Data center backlash mirrors Tesla's EV subsidy playbook

  • After the $7,500 EV tax credit ended, Tesla used its scale advantage to outlast smaller rivals and consolidate market share.
  • Meta president Dina Powell McCormick said incumbent data center operators face fewer community obstacles than new entrants due to established track records.
  • Democrats' election wins have strengthened local opposition to new data center projects, creating barriers to entry that parallel the EV subsidy removal's effect on auto competition.
  • The parallel suggests hyperscalers could remain attractive investments even amid a broader data center backlash if they can block new competitors.

  • Senator Elizabeth Warren, incoming chair of the Senate Banking Committee, signals more assertive congressional oversight of the Fed.
