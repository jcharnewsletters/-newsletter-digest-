Nvidia's Q3 Earnings Beat Expectations Amid Leadership Turmoil

  • Revenue jumped 103% year-over-year to $115.8 billion, nearly $7 billion above analyst estimates; adjusted EPS hit $2.68 vs $2.48 expected.
  • Guidance for next quarter raised to $130 billion, exceeding the $125 billion consensus, with zero China data-center revenue assumed.
  • Company announced a $150 billion stock buyback and committed $32-390 billion to suppliers/neoclouds (cloud computing providers specializing in AI workloads) through 2028-2032, obligations that must be paid regardless of future AI spending trends.
  • Gross margin guidance fell to 71-72% from 74.9%, signaling rising costs; shares rose in after-hours/pre-market trading but remain down about 10.5% since Jensen Huang's resignation announcement.
  • SEC (Securities and Exchange Commission) is probing Nvidia following Huang's resignation amid a scandal involving an alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy; Huang has agreed to testify before Congress/Senate on December 1-2.
  • Interim CEO Colette Kress raised the sales forecast and expressed confidence in the business continuing without Huang; Nvidia stopped shipping licensed Hopper AI chips to China despite US approval, citing compliance concerns.
  • Trump administration is advancing a ban on Chinese data-center components, escalating US-China tech competition.
  • Semiconductor peers also rose on the results, and Nasdaq/S&P 500 futures climbed as investors rotated back into tech stocks.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) The $390 billion supplier commitment is up from $279 billion just three months earlier, with more than half due in fiscal 2028 and roughly three-quarters due by fiscal 2029 — a major financial obligation inherited by whoever leads next.

Cascading Tech Outages Raise Infrastructure and AI-Patching Concerns

  • Major outages hit Microsoft (Outlook/Teams, 36 hours), FAA (nationwide flight grounding, 4+ hours, pausing Juniper router installations made by HPE), JPMorgan, United Airlines, Apple, Workday, Costco, and Equinor (Europe's largest natural gas supplier).
  • Bank executives and the NSA (National Security Agency) flagged AI-related software vulnerabilities; Trump publicly downplayed the security concerns.
  • Pattern suggests AI systems are automatically deploying software patches without sufficient human oversight, creating cascading failures across critical infrastructure.
  • Underlying structural risks include aging, underfunded systems (e.g., FAA's outdated copper cable switches, air traffic control understaffing) and AI code-generation tools shifting the bottleneck to reviewing rather than writing code, raising risk of untested code reaching production.
  • Costco's system failure triggered panic buying amid 'apocalyptic' fears among shoppers.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief AI tools are also expanding cybersecurity risk by helping hackers find zero-day bugs (previously unknown software vulnerabilities) faster and scale social engineering attacks, with the Binance hack cited as an example.

Bloomberg Surveys show a majority of Americans now believe future technology poses an existential threat, with anxiety amplified by the outages, though Secretary of State Marco Rubio disputed a direct AI connection.
