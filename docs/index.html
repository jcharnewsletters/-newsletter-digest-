Nvidia's High-Stakes Earnings: First Without Jensen Huang Amid CEO Reinstatement Battle

  • Nvidia reports quarterly earnings today, its first earnings call in 33 years without founder Jensen Huang, who was removed as CEO with Colette Kress serving as interim chief.
  • Analysts expect revenue of $109.1 billion, a 91% year-over-year jump, but the stock has fallen for six consecutive weeks (worst losing streak since 2012), losing roughly $2 trillion in market cap and down 16% year-to-date versus the S&P 500's 8% gain.
  • Huang's departure stemmed from HR violations (subordinate affairs) and a federal investigation into his ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset connected to AI chip export controls.
  • More than 188,000 people signed a petition for Huang's reinstatement; major shareholders Altimeter Capital and Atreides Management back his return, and an internal Nvidia director letter warned of 'irreplaceable talent' departures.
  • The reinstatement push faces opposition from Congress and Cabinet officials over national security concerns; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent accused Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick of lax China enforcement, while President Trump said he'd welcome Huang's return.
  • Roughly $279 billion in Nvidia deals reportedly hinge on customer and partner trust in stable leadership.
  • Washington tightened AI chip export rules, requiring licenses for advanced semiconductor sales to five countries (Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Brazil), sending AMD and Broadcom down 4-6% and triggering a selloff in Chinese AI stocks.
  • Investor focus centers on forward guidance, gross margins (a measure of pricing power and manufacturing efficiency), and data center chip demand amid intensifying AI-chip competition.
