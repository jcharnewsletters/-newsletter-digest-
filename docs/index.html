September CPI inflation comes in hotter than expected, shaking rate-cut hopes

  • September CPI rose 3.7% year-over-year (highest since 2023) with core CPI (excludes volatile food/energy prices) at 2.6%.
  • Inflation divergence: energy/gas prices surging sharply while grocery costs stayed stable, complicating the overall picture.
  • Main drivers—gas prices and AI data center buildouts—are largely outside the Fed's (Federal Reserve, the U.S. central bank) control via interest rates.
  • CME Fed futures market odds of an October rate hike jumped from 22% to 54% after the data release.
  • Real wage growth stalled at about 3%, below the inflation rate, squeezing household purchasing power.
  • S&P 500 fell 1.1%, snapping a record 52-day streak without a 1% decline, though the index remains up 12%+ year-to-date.
  • Fed officials signaled the 2% inflation target may take years rather than quarters to achieve, implying rates stay higher for longer.
  • Higher-for-longer rates pressure stock valuations (future earnings are worth less at higher discount rates) and could further cool mortgage and real estate activity.
