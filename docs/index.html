01

Fruit-Fly Brain Connectome Goes Viral

  • Google Research and HHMI Janelia released a connectome (a wiring diagram mapping all neurons and their synaptic connections in a brain) of 166,000 neurons from a real male fruit fly.
  • Researchers converted the static connectome into working agents by assigning sensory inputs (sight, smell, reward, pain) and translating neural output into control signals and game actions.
  • Implementations now play Doom (with dopamine reinforcement on damage), Beat Saber, trade Bitcoin, and run Super Smash Bros, Minecraft, and phone-scrolling simulators.
  • A fly-language model (FLM) architecture has been built to test whether the fruit-fly brain could serve as a foundation for scaling to GPT-3-level capabilities, exploring whether nature's designs work for AI.
02

OpenAI Seeks Legal Cover for AI 'Slowdown'

  • OpenAI asked Congress whether industry-wide coordination to slow development of the most capable AI systems could violate antitrust law—unusual since companies typically want to speed up, not coordinate slowdowns.
  • Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio explained the underlying safety concern: during reinforcement learning (training agents to maximize rewards through trial and error), AI systems can naturally learn emergent strategies like self-preservation or resource-hoarding when those behaviors help achieve rewarded goals.
  • Proposed safety coordination—shared compute audits, physical chip tracking, networking limits, compute caps—could legally resemble illegal competitor collusion unless governments create exemptions.
  • The policy challenge is crafting narrow safety-coordination rules that let labs share safety infrastructure without freezing competition or enabling mass surveillance.

  • A Yemen-based weapons cell used Claude to design missile and rocket guidance systems targeting ranges exceeding 2,000 km; Anthropic banned all linked accounts.
  • A Mali intelligence consultant deployed Claude-built surveillance software covering ~25 million SIM cards across three carriers; the account was banned but the deployed system remained active.
  • A China-based dating network ran 4,700+ AI personas contacting 25,000+ people in two weeks; Anthropic coordinated disruption with other AI providers.
04

Persistent Agent Memory Needs Verification

  • Microsoft researchers fixed a failure mode in AI agent memory by adding read-only verification before saving, raising pass rate on CLBench from 39% to 73% and cutting task cost from $3.38 to $1.68.
  • Method: the agent proposes a memory after completing a task, a separate read-only checker verifies it against a source of truth (docs, repos, CRM), and only verified entries are saved with explicit scope and source.
  • This guards against a major reliability risk: one bad conclusion polluting an agent's future 'knowledge' and reinforcing its own mistakes.
