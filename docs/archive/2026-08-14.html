S&P 500 hits record high as volatility collapses

  • S&P 500 closed at a record, clearing 7,800 for the first time Thursday, with futures steady Friday.
  • VIX (Wall Street's 'fear gauge,' measuring expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days) finished at 14.6, roughly 30% below its 10-year average.
  • The index has moved an average of just 0.38% over the last five sessions and hasn't fallen 1% since July 29.
  • Correlations between individual stocks have broken down — niche AI names swinging double digits in opposite directions mechanically cancel out and suppress index-level moves.
  • Tail risk: the calm 'works right up until stocks start moving the same direction.'
  • When the VIX last sat at these levels in January, the S&P 500 traded 13% below Thursday's close, implying significant valuation expansion.
  1. Investopedia Pre-Market: Semiconductor (computer chip) stocks are nearing conditions for a sustained bull market (an extended period of rising prices).
  2. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Bulls lean on historic earnings, but macro backdrop is tough: CPI 3.4% y/y, wholesale prices 4.7%, 30-year Treasury yield above 5.2%.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Investopedia Pre-Market, Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha)

Cooler wholesale inflation strengthens the case for a Fed pause/cut

  • July producer price index (PPI, which tracks wholesale prices businesses receive) rose 4.7% year-over-year, down sharply from the prior month.
  • Opening Bell reads 4.7% as beating a 4.9% consensus (prior 5.5%); Yahoo Finance reads it as slightly above a 4.6% forecast but improved from June's 5.1%.
  • Follows a cooler consumer inflation (CPI) print earlier in the week, taking near-term rate hikes off the table.
  • Capital Economics' chief economist says the combined reports make a September rate hike less likely; Opening Bell frames it as raising odds of a September rate cut.
  • Fed officials remain split: hawks want to press toward the 2% target, doves see mixed data as reason to wait.
  • Friday's calendar: retail sales (+0.3% expected) and University of Michigan sentiment (54.1 expected, down from 55.2).
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Initial jobless claims rose to 209,000, a two-month high above the 202,000 consensus, hinting at labor-market softening.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Long-end Treasury yields hit multi-decade highs

  • The US Treasury sold 30-year bonds at the highest yield since 2001 — levels not seen in about 25 years.
  • Elevated long-term rates raise financing costs across corporate America and consumer credit, even as inflation moderates.
  • Yield strength persists despite the cooling-inflation narrative, reflecting fiscal and term-premium concerns.
  1. Bloomberg: Fitch kept the US at AA+ but warned that fiscal deficits — with debt more than double the median for similarly rated nations — leave the country vulnerable to economic shocks.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Silver Lake in talks to take Workday private in ~$43B software buyout

  • Silver Lake is negotiating to acquire HR and finance software firm Workday (WDAY) at roughly a $43 billion valuation, among the largest software buyouts ever.
  • Workday shares jumped 19.5% on the report.
  • Part of a broader private-equity push into mature public companies, betting on operational improvements and a stabilizing cost of capital.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Nelson Peltz's Trian, already holding 16% of Wendy's, is assembling a consortium with Flynn Group to take the chain private; a bid is expected within weeks and WEN rose ~12%.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha)

Reddit jumps on S&P 500 inclusion

  • Reddit (RDDT) surged double digits after confirmation it will join the S&P 500 index.
  • Sui joins the S&P MidCap 400 in the same reshuffle.
  • Index inclusion forces passive funds tracking the S&P 500 to buy the stock, a mechanical demand boost.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 2

1. CEO-worker pay gap becomes a political flashpoint

  • AFL-CIO data shows average S&P 500 CEO pay rose 21% to $22.8 million in 2025 versus 3.7% wage growth for workers — a 312-to-1 ratio.
  • Elon Musk's Tesla compensation was reportedly 2.5 million times median employee pay, potentially resetting the benchmark for executive packages.
  • Most of the gap comes from stock options and restricted stock (equity awards that pay out as the share price rises), which boards defend as tying pay to shareholder returns.
  • Widening gaps are fueling democratic socialism on the left and private-sector union support on the right.

2. Other corporate moves

  • Charter Communications received final state approval for its $34.5 billion merger with Cox Communications.
  • Apple partnered with Alibaba on a custom AI model for the Chinese market.
  • Elon Musk became the world's first trillionaire following the SpaceX IPO, which jumped 11% in its debut.
  • Miami, Nashville, and Houston now lead US buyer's markets as housing demand shifts geographically.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 4

1. Sit-down restaurants crush fast casual for the first time in a decade

  • Over 60 trading days, Darden, Texas Roadhouse, and Cheesecake Factory are 62 percentage points ahead of Chipotle, Wingstop, and Shake Shack — the widest gap in ten years.
  • Fast-casual names generated minimal returns over three months despite higher expected earnings growth.
  • Winners are gaining on same-store sales (revenue per existing location) and repeat traffic rather than new-store expansion, suggesting selective, inflation-conscious consumers.

2. DeepSeek's 4x price hike restores AI pricing power

  • Chinese AI lab DeepSeek raised prices roughly 4x, moving from deep-discount territory to near-competitor levels.
  • The hike eases fears of commodification (a race-to-the-bottom on AI service pricing) that threatened OpenAI and Anthropic IPO valuations.
  • AI return-on-investment and contract margins were the quarter's dominant investment theme, with Google and Microsoft under scrutiny on monetization versus infrastructure spend.

3. Bipartisan backlash to data centers and surveillance cameras becomes a credit risk

  • Republicans and Democrats are jointly opposing data center expansion, citing land use, power consumption, and construction disruption.
  • Flock Safety's license-plate camera network faces scrutiny after the Washington Post reported at least 50 police officers charged or accused of misusing plate readers to stalk people and ex-partners.
  • Flock announced new safeguards Thursday; Wall Street is now treating community resistance as a material credit risk for data center and surveillance firms.

4. Household cost pressures

  • Car insurance premiums are climbing in more than half of US states.
  • Mortgage rates fell for the first time in six weeks.
Bloomberg 4

1. OpenAI annualized revenue tops $40 billion

  • Annualized revenue run rate surged past $40 billion, roughly double its end-of-2025 pace, per people familiar.
  • Growth is driven by subscription sales and a nascent advertising business, strengthening IPO (initial public offering — a company's first sale of shares to the public) plans.
  • Signals successful expansion beyond ChatGPT into enterprise and consumer monetization.

2. Oil rises on Iran pressure

  • Crude gained after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent signaled increased economic pressure on Iran.
  • Tankers are going dark (switching off transponders) in key shipping straits to avoid detection.

3. Trump's 'golden age' claim clashes with voter sentiment

  • Only 39% approve of Trump's job performance per RealClearPolitics, with midterms less than three months away.
  • Data is mixed: unemployment is low and consumer spending strong, but inflation is back above 3% annually.
  • Some Republicans want fresh proposals such as further tax cuts rather than campaigning on the existing record.

4. US slaps up to 100% tariffs on imported drones

  • New levies reach 100% on imported drones and components to cut reliance on foreign suppliers.
  • The announcement didn't name China, but risks deepening two-way decoupling from the world's dominant drone manufacturer.
  • Aim is to build domestic drone manufacturing capacity.
