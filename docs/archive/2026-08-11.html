Zuckerberg's 6,500-word AI manifesto pivots Meta back to open source

  • Meta CEO published a 6,500-word manifesto arguing superintelligence (AI that outperforms humans across most intellectual tasks) should be widely distributed rather than controlled by elite labs or governments.
  • Plan: personalized AI agents for billions of people working 24/7 on relationships, health, career, finances and hobbies, delivered free or via compute auctions.
  • Strategy rests on open-source models plus 'distillation' (training smaller, cheaper models on the outputs of larger ones) to undercut proprietary rivals like OpenAI.
  • Zuckerberg dismisses job-loss fears as 'doom discourse' that would concentrate power.
  • Critics note the stance conveniently serves Meta: recovering lost ground in the AI race, harvesting data for ad targeting, and monetizing its data-center compute.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Timing follows legal setbacks including a $567M New Mexico child-harms settlement and a stalled social media addiction trial, giving Meta a PR incentive to change the subject.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Notes the reversal from Meta's post-2016 advocacy of centralized infrastructure and content filtering, plus its new open-source model Muse Glimmer.
  3. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Frames it as Facebook's old 'liberation' narrative recycled — private platforms sold as public utilities — while Meta launches ChatGPT and Claude alternatives and sells compute.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)

AI funding bonanza: Nvidia's $500B package, Intel's upsized share sale

  • Nvidia is assembling a roughly $500 billion AI infrastructure financing package with Goldman Sachs, Blackstone and four other Wall Street firms — an unprecedented capital commitment for one company.
  • Intel raised $20 billion in a share sale priced at $95, about 33% more than targeted, to fund its foundry (contract chip-manufacturing) buildout; earlier reports pegged the deal at $15 billion.
  • The scale of demand signals sustained investor appetite for AI supply-chain names, and a shift toward private capital markets for funding AI expansion.
  • Concentration risk grows if a handful of suppliers control global compute capacity.
  1. Bloomberg: Anthropic secured $9.1B from Bitcoin miner Riot Platforms for extra AI data-center capacity, showing unconventional funders entering the space.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Some institutions are exploring using physical Nvidia chips as loan collateral — treating GPUs as a tangible asset class like gold or equipment.
  3. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Ties Intel's raise to U.S. government support for domestic chip production.

Covered by: Bloomberg, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, CNBC Morning Squawk

Chip supply and demand: TSMC's 45% revenue jump, memory crunch to 2028

  • Taiwan Semiconductor reported a 45% revenue jump, sparking rallies in Applied Materials, Lam Research and ASML as validation of the AI capex cycle.
  • JPMorgan projects a memory chip shortage lasting until 2028 as AI server demand outruns expansion of HBM (high-bandwidth memory, stacked chips that feed data to AI accelerators) and DRAM capacity.
  • Elevated memory prices are expected to keep benefiting Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Frames the shortage as roughly two more years of tight supply.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Corporate profits growing fastest since 2021, lifting Wall Street targets

  • Q2 earnings delivered the fastest profit growth since 2021 — and outside post-recession rebounds — with gains broader than just AI winners.
  • S&P 500 rose 2.4% over the past month; JPMorgan raised its year-end S&P 500 target to 8,000, about 3% upside.
  • Q2 profits grew 32% excluding Alphabet's and Amazon's investment gains; small-caps are up 23%, widening market breadth.
  • Forward valuation compressed from 22.2x to 20x earnings even as prices rose, and strong outlooks held up despite the Iran conflict and sticky inflation.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): S&P 500 is up 117% over 3.8 years since October 2022; 7 of 10 major bull markets since 1957 survived past year three, all positive in year four with a median 13.2% gain, and none died from age alone — Fed rate hikes killed the 1957 and 1962 rallies.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Credits Creative Planning's Charlie Bilello and notes FOMO reinforcing buying pressure.
  3. Investopedia Pre-Market: Futures pointed slightly higher ahead of Tuesday-evening AI earnings from Lumentum (Nvidia-backed optical components) and Supermicro (AI server hardware), both expected to swing sharply.

Covered by: Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Investopedia Pre-Market, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)

Inflation and oil revive rate-hike talk

  • WTI crude rose 1.9% to $83.70 as Trump hardened his stance toward Iran and demanded reparations for past attacks, lifting bond yields and inflation worries.
  • Headline PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred price gauge) sits at 3.7% with core near 3.3%; Fed governor Beth Hammack said several rate hikes may be needed to reach the 2% target.
  • CME futures show roughly 50-50 odds of a September rate hike despite August payrolls falling 23,000 and 100,000+ jobs revised away from prior months.
  1. CNBC Morning Squawk: Says three key economic reports this week could reset Fed expectations and stir equity and bond volatility, while oil's pullback from recent highs pressures energy stocks and offers rebalancing opportunities.
  2. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Argues monetary tightening, not calendar age, is the real threat to the current bull market.

Covered by: Bloomberg, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), CNBC Morning Squawk

Berkshire Hathaway deploys cash again under Greg Abel

  • New CEO Greg Abel put $10 billion into Alphabet stock plus $4.5 billion of buybacks, Berkshire's first significant stock purchases in about four years.
  • Michael Burry ('The Big Short' investor) says Berkshire is unattractive without Buffett's discipline and questioned the cash-pile strategy.
  • Berkshire shares are up 6% year to date versus a 13% market gain, suggesting investors remain unconvinced.

Covered by: Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)
