July jobs report is the day's main event — and the Fed's next signal

  • Consensus clusters around 88,000 new nonfarm payrolls (jobs added outside farming) for July, up from June's downward-revised 57,000 but well below May's 172,000; CNBC's cited consensus was higher at 185,000.
  • Unemployment rate expected roughly steady (4.0%–4.2%); average hourly earnings seen +0.3% month-over-month and +3.5% year-over-year, unchanged from June and not clearly inflationary.
  • Healthcare is expected to supply about two-thirds of July job growth — economists call reliance on one industry unsustainable.
  • Leisure and hospitality shed 61,000 jobs in June despite the summer/World Cup season, a red flag for broader demand.
  • Labor force participation (share of adults working or looking for work) fell to 61.5% in June, lowest since 1976 excluding the pandemic, driven by an unexplained drop among 25–34 year-olds.
  • Data feeds directly into the September Fed meeting: a weak print raises rate-cut odds, while strong payrolls or hot wages support 'higher for longer.'
  • Stock futures were roughly flat-to-higher into the print (Dow flat, S&P +0.1%, Nasdaq +0.4%) with major indexes on track for their best week in months.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Tech accounts for roughly one-third of 2026 announced job cuts with AI often cited as the cause; ADP private payrolls missed Thursday while JOLTS showed hiring improving as openings fell.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Sees modest broadening into 2026 with construction, retail and transportation gaining, and notes manufacturing PMI (factory activity survey) at a four-year high.
  3. Bloomberg: Frames the print as a market-roiling risk alongside high oil prices, as talks to reopen the Strait of Hormuz stalled.

Alphabet returns to the bond market as AI capex balloons

  • Alphabet's bond sale (reportedly ~$25 billion) drew $115 billion in orders despite a recent investor sell-off — a standout versus tepid demand for recent Meta and Amazon deals (Amazon raised $12.5B for a Texas data center).
  • AI capital spending has consumed hyperscaler free cash flow; Alphabet is now running negative cash flow and borrowing to fund expansion.
  • Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta are on track to spend 103% of operating cash flow on capex (capital expenditures — spending on data centers, chips and infrastructure) by end-2026, up from 64% in 2025, meaning external funding is required.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Every capex dollar lands on suppliers' books, making chipmakers, power producers, cooling specialists and utilities direct beneficiaries.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

1. Corporate and regulatory moves

  • American Airlines is scrapping free upgrades for elite frequent flyers.

  • Amazon will deliver GLP-1 weight-loss medications (drugs like semaglutide that mimic an appetite-regulating gut hormone) to Medicare recipients for $50/month, challenging traditional pharmacy distribution.

3. Tech hardware and manufacturing

  • OpenAI's first device, a smart speaker, will be priced above $300.
  • Tesla is building 'Terafab,' a large chip-manufacturing complex, a potential tailwind for industrial suppliers.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) 2

1. Wall Street reprices the hyperscalers

  • Microsoft has jumped 24% since earnings, adding roughly $700 billion in market cap; Amazon gained 25% and Alphabet rose 5.1% in five days.
  • Microsoft revenue grew 18% with Azure up 43%; Alphabet revenue up 24% with Google Cloud up 82%; AWS grew 37%, its fastest in five years.
  • Before earnings these names had been punished for capex — Microsoft down 21%, Alphabet and Amazon roughly flat.
  • Interactive Brokers' Steve Sosnick likened marking up multi-trillion-dollar companies 25% in three days to 'a meme stock,' while conceding 'the demand is there and the profits and the margins are there.'

2. HubSpot plunges on guidance cut

  • Shares fell 24% after the company added only 7,000 new customers versus 9,000–10,000 expected, a warning sign for software adoption.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 2

1. SpaceX retail investors bought the lockup-expiration dip

  • Retail traders bought $22.7 million of SpaceX shares in the first hour of Wednesday's session as the lockup (post-IPO period barring insider selling) expired.
  • There has not been a single day of net retail selling since the June 12 debut, despite the stock falling post-IPO.
  • Wall Street price targets run as high as $300/share, a rare alignment of institutional and retail bullishness.

2. Ford's sub-$30,000 electric pickup, the Fathom

  • Priced from $28,350 using a simplified universal platform, finally hitting Ford's sub-$30,000 EV goal.
  • Pre-orders open in early 2027 with deliveries several months later; the truck format targets US buyer preferences and avoids Tesla's brand baggage.
  • Automakers have written down billions as EV demand lagged and shifted toward plug-in hybrids.
Bloomberg 5

1. First US–Japan joint yen intervention since 1998

  • The two governments jointly bought yen (currency intervention — selling dollars to prop up the yen), but the yen has already surrendered most of its gains amid speculation of further action.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blindsided the ECB, informing it only after US dollars had been sold.

2. Sports streaming fragmentation draws antitrust scrutiny

  • The NFL is expected to collect about $12.5 billion a year from four networks plus Amazon, YouTube and Netflix, far more than other leagues.
  • The Justice Department opened a probe into whether NFL broadcast deals inflate consumer costs; a potential $14 billion Sunday Ticket antitrust verdict is on appeal.
  • FCC Chairman Brendan Carr floated letting broadcasters collectively negotiate media deals, like NFL teams do, to cut programming costs.

3. Trump executive orders on birthright citizenship and vaccines

  • New orders target babies born to women deemed to be doing 'birth tourism' or to certain foreigners, and face near-certain legal challenge as with his prior attempt.
  • The administration is weighing an executive order linking vaccines and autism as soon as next week, reviving a topic officials had downplayed ahead of midterms.

4. Colombia's incoming president inherits a mess

  • Abelardo de la Espriella won by the narrowest margin, campaigning on Argentine-style fiscal austerity (deep spending cuts), El Salvador's hardline security model, and closeness to Trump.
  • He faces a widening budget deficit, accelerating inflation, high interest rates and record coca production strengthening armed rebel groups.

5. Odd lot

  • A $10 Dunkin' gift card tied to activating the Paze digital wallet has set off an obsessive consumer hunt for 'free money' rebates.
Investopedia Pre-Market 1

1. Retirement savings gaps

  • 401(k) enrollment (employer-sponsored retirement accounts funded with pre-tax pay) is rising, but many new participants pick contribution rates too low to build adequate savings.
  • Federal Reserve data shows wide net-worth disparities among Americans aged 55–64, underscoring uneven retirement readiness.
  • Most retirees lean on Social Security rather than IRAs (self-directed tax-advantaged retirement accounts), signaling underuse of personal savings vehicles.
  • Few Americans receive the maximum Social Security check, largely due to claiming timing and earnings history.
