New Jersey Takes Kalshi to the Supreme Court

  • New Jersey filed a complaint at the U.S. Supreme Court against Kalshi, a prediction market platform offering contracts tied to real-world events like election outcomes and economic data releases.
  • The state argues Kalshi operates without required state authorization and violates gambling laws by allowing users to place bets on event outcomes.
  • Kalshi contends it operates under federal Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) authority, which regulates derivatives (financial contracts whose value depends on underlying assets or events), claiming state gambling laws don't apply.
  • The dispute reflects a fundamental tension over whether prediction markets fall under federal derivatives regulation or state gambling oversight.
  • Multiple states have challenged the CFTC's approval of Kalshi's contracts, arguing the platform resembles illegal betting rather than legitimate financial instruments.
  • Resolution could determine whether prediction markets can expand nationally or remain restricted to limited jurisdictions.

AI productivity requires the right design, not just cost reduction

  • Token count alone is a poor efficiency metric; the goal is using sufficient context to complete tasks well rather than minimizing API calls at the expense of quality.
  • One-shot prompt templates frequently fail; the most effective architectures use closed-loop 'taste flywheels' (iterative feedback mechanisms) to refine outputs.
  • AI raises the ceiling of work quality, so users should push for higher-quality outputs rather than settling for pre-AI baselines just because AI enables faster work.

  • A Michigan judge barred Kalshi's prediction market platform from operating sports betting, calling it a betting operation "masquerading as an investment opportunity" and threatening $500,000 daily fines for violations.
  • Regulatory definitions of which crypto trading products count as derivatives versus gambling or securities remain contested and inconsistent across jurisdictions.
