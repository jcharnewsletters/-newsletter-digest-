Tesla's Cybercab Event in Austin

  • Tesla holds Cybercab event in Austin, unveiling its purpose-built autonomous taxi (self-driving vehicle designed for ride-hailing rather than personal ownership) with no steering wheel or pedals, powered by a 163 kW (219 horsepower) front motor.
  • Bull case requires 25-50 Cybercabs deployed for paid retail rides within days/weeks; fewer than 5-10 vehicles deployed would be seen as insufficient by Morgan Stanley analysts and could disappoint investors.
  • Bear case is a retail demonstration only with no commercial fleet deployment, which would likely cause the stock to decline post-event.
  • Tesla has logged 380,000 unsupervised robotaxi miles across six U.S. cities as of July, versus Waymo's 200 million autonomous miles—a significant technology gap.
  • Cybercab is the only U.S. purpose-built robotaxi besides Amazon-owned Zoox.

Google Wins Antitrust Case, Keeps Ad Tech Business Intact

  • A federal judge (Leonie Brinkema) ruled Google does not have to divest (sell off) its ad tech business despite finding the company monopolized online ad markets.
  • The court instead imposed behavioral remedies—mandated changes to business practices—rather than a structural breakup.
  • This marks the third consecutive court rejection of antitrust arguments to break up a major Big Tech firm, highlighting judicial reluctance toward structural remedies despite findings of market abuse.
