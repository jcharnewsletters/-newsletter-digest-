Fed Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole speech stokes rate hike expectations

  • Warsh said inflation remains 'too high' and must reach the Fed's 2% target 'clearly and at sufficient speed,' framing price stability as the central bank's core job.
  • He indicated financial conditions are not currently restrictive, suggesting room for policy moves without harming growth.
  • Called for a 'quieter Fed,' hinting at less aggressive regulatory intervention going forward.
  • Despite pledging to avoid forward guidance, former Fed vice chair Alan Blinder interpreted the speech as signaling a likely September rate hike.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Speech occurred alongside updated Q2 GDP data showing 1.5% annualized growth, a moderate but slowing pace of economic expansion.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Warsh flagged that Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) index readings—a key inflation gauge tracking prices consumers pay for goods and services—remain concerning, and recent summer improvements don't reflect a real trend change.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 3

1. Nvidia's blockbuster earnings lift markets

  • Q2 revenue hit $96.2B, up 106% year-over-year, beating expectations on strong AI infrastructure demand.
  • Company guided for 70% revenue growth next fiscal year, citing broad-based demand across AI projects and data centers.
  • Strong guidance reassured investors that the AI investment cycle remains robust and secular in nature.

2. Social Security solvency crisis looms

  • Trust fund is projected to run dry by 2031, triggering an automatic 22% cut to benefits unless Congress intervenes.
  • Current retirees this decade are projected to collect more than 3x what they contributed, favoring Baby Boomers disproportionately.
  • Younger generations face higher payroll taxes but lower eventual benefits absent policy reform.

3. Market winners and losers this week

  • Software/cloud stocks surged: Salesforce +22%, CrowdStrike +14%, ServiceNow +13%, on strong AI productivity narratives.
  • Small-caps underperformed, with the Russell 2000 down 1.5% versus modest gains in the S&P 500 (+0.5%) and Nasdaq (+0.9%).
  • Commodities weakened: crude oil fell 4.2% to $83.44/bbl and gold dropped 3.8% to $4,504/oz, as the energy sector fell 2% amid rotation into tech.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 3

1. Retail earnings reveal pressure on consumer wallets and margins

  • Walmart posted its slowest same-store sales growth since late 2020, with weaker customer traffic and transaction sizes; CFO cited the psychological impact of $4 gasoline prices on spending decisions.
  • Dollar Tree beat earnings expectations but shares fell 4% as guidance disappointed, with a significant chunk of projected earnings coming from court-ordered tariff refunds, raising skepticism about core operating performance.
  • Value retailers like Target and Dollar General benefited as budget-strained consumers traded down to lower-priced options.
  • Beauty category showed standout strength, bucking broader retail sector weakness.

2. PayPal acquisition collapses

  • Stripe and Advent Capital abandoned their combined $50 billion bid to acquire PayPal, sending PayPal shares lower.
  • The collapse removes a potential strategic exit option for the struggling payments company.

3. Other notable items

  • Goldman Sachs reports oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz (a key shipping chokepoint for global oil) at two-thirds of pre-war levels, signaling ongoing supply constraints.
  • Andreessen Horowitz closed a $1.1 billion fund dedicated to AI infrastructure investment.
