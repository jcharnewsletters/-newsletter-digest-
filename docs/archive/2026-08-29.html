OpenAI Cuts Cursor Over Trust, Not Technology

  • OpenAI will end model access for coding tool Cursor on November 12, after Cursor's parent company Anysphere was acquired by SpaceX.

  • Cursor must now rely more heavily on Anthropic's Claude model and its own in-house Composer model to stay viable.

South Korea's Free AI Initiative

  • South Korea will roll out free, unlimited AI services to all citizens by late 2026, delivered through three consortia: SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao.
  • 512 Nvidia B200 chips (high-performance AI accelerator chips used to train and run AI models) will be distributed during 2026 to power the effort.
  • Starting in 2027, the government will cover part of the operating costs, with companies funding the rest.
  • Rules require at least 50% of model compute to come from independent Korean-made foundation models (large general-purpose AI models), plus 30% from other domestic developers, to strengthen the local AI industry.

Meta's Robot Workforce Trials in Data Centers

  • Meta is testing robots from vendors Watney Robotics, Kinova, and ABB for data center tasks like cable swapping, server reboots, rack moving, and equipment inspection.
  • The robots currently need continuous human supervision because they struggle with tangled cables, battery life, navigation obstacles, and inspection accuracy.
  • One worker estimated that successful cable-swapping robots could eventually replace up to 80% of certain job functions, though Meta has not released official productivity forecasts.

Prediction Market Regulation Split Heads to Supreme Court

  • The Ninth Circuit federal appeals court (covering nine Western U.S. states) ruled that prediction markets like Kalshi can be regulated by states as gambling.
  • This contradicts an earlier Third Circuit ruling that favored federal financial regulation over prediction markets, creating a circuit split (when two federal appeals courts reach opposing conclusions on the same legal question).
  • Such splits typically prompt Supreme Court intervention to set a uniform national rule.
  • The ruling exposes Kalshi and similar platforms to potential state-level bans or tighter regulation in California, Arizona, and six other Ninth Circuit states.
