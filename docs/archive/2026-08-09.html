Lenny's Newsletter 2

1. Cursor's talent chief: run every hire like an executive search

  • Adam Ward, Head of Talent at Cursor (the AI coding editor startup), argues the traditional recruiting funnel produces mediocre hires because it depends on job postings and inbound applications.
  • He calls it the 'funnel of doom' — a standard process that filters candidates through increasingly narrow criteria, but only sees people who apply.
  • Top talent lives in a 'tale of two cities' market: elite candidates are actively courted by multiple companies and rarely apply through job boards.
  • Step 1 — Scoping: define the exact skills, experience, and qualities needed before any sourcing begins.
  • Step 2 — Mapping: research and list specific named individuals who match the scope, sourced via referrals and professional networks.
  • Step 3 — Relentless pursuit: engage candidates repeatedly over time, treating recruiting as an ongoing relationship rather than a transaction.
  • Ward says caring about candidates as people, not just skill sets, is the single biggest unfair advantage in hiring.

2. The 'forward deployed engineer' becomes a real career path

  • Forward deployed engineers (technical staff embedded inside a customer's or partner's organization to build tailored solutions) are emerging as a distinct and growing talent pool.
  • The role bridges engineering and customer success, requiring both deep technical skill and strong interpersonal ability.
There's An AI For That (TAAFT) 5

1. AI beats 458 of 526 human teams in a 16-day programming contest

  • An AI system worked autonomously for 16 days on a contest project and finished faster than 458 of the 526 human teams entered.
  • It marks the first opening of the contest's 'Max tier' difficulty level; the model weights (the trained parameters that define the AI) ship next week.
  • The result signals AI now exceeds median human performance on complex, multi-day technical work.

2. ChatGPT drops message caps for free users

  • Free accounts now get unlimited text messages, replacing the previous daily cap.
  • A new 'Think' button routes harder questions to reasoning mode (where the model works through steps before answering).
  • Paid tiers add a slider to control how deeply the model thinks, plus a model variant that gives critical feedback.

3. Google study: 86% of AI use happens outside work

  • Analysis of 15 million chats found 86% of AI usage occurs outside work contexts, undercutting enterprise automation narratives.
  • At work, AI touches only about one-fifth of tasks, and under 10% of work interactions actually automate anything.
  • Adoption is running far behind industry forecasts for workplace productivity gains.

4. Google runs Gemma translation offline on a Raspberry Pi

  • Google deployed its Gemma language model on a Raspberry Pi (a low-cost, credit-card-sized single-board computer) with no internet connection required.
  • Translation happens in real time through a terminal interface, with all computation running locally on the device.
  • Works underground, on planes, or anywhere connectivity is poor, and response speed matches local processing rather than cloud latency.

5. MIT publishes 13 free AI courses

  • Curriculum spans AI fundamentals, machine learning, computer vision, and algorithms, all free to access.
