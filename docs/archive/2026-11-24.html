Nvidia Board Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, Sparking Record-Breaking Rally

  • Nvidia's board removed Huang around Nov. 12-17 citing personal conduct issues, then reversed course on Nov. 23 and reinstated him, with board chair Harvey Jones calling him 'the right person to lead'.
  • Stock surged 31% on the reinstatement news, adding roughly $952 billion to $1 trillion in market value in a single day—the largest one-day market-cap gain for any stock on record, more than double Microsoft's prior $450 billion record.
  • President Trump said he personally called into the board meeting and 'convinced' directors to reinstate Huang, framing him as a 'national treasure' essential to national security and the American economy.
  • The rally helped drive the S&P 500 up 4.7% and Nasdaq Composite up 6.5%—the market's best day since April 2025—recovering much of the roughly $1.2 trillion in Nvidia value lost over the prior week.
  • Huang still faces Congressional testimony next month and an ongoing external investigation into foreign intelligence and export-control allegations; Sen. Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement 'reckless' and demanded answers.
  • Analysts criticized the board's flip-flopping as a governance failure ('clown-like' response), and Bloomberg reported the board did not consult senior leadership before reversing its decision.
  • Markets pulled back the next day as enthusiasm faded: Nvidia and other AI stocks led declines, with futures down (Dow -0.5%, S&P 500 -0.9% to -1%, Nasdaq -1.7% to -2%), and Monday's rally is now seen as one of the strongest single days in market history, prompting talk of consolidation.

Block Size and Technical Scaling Constraints

  • Under Ethereum's Glamsterdam upgrade's 60M gas limit, skipped EIP-7702 authorizations (transaction pre-approvals stored on-chain) represent the worst-case block-size attack, packing 17.0 bytes per 1k gas and producing 1.81 MiB execution layer blocks — about 2x what calldata (transaction input data) achieves
  • A structural conflict exists with FOCIL (EIP-7805): a transaction satisfying gas constraints may breach the 8 MiB execution layer byte cap when added to a block, leaving builders unable to meet attesters' (validators providing inclusion guarantees) expectations despite valid gas usage
  • Author proposes pricing every payload byte in gas via EIP-8131 and EIP-8279 plus deposit-request metering with a capped gas limit to bound worst-case envelope size, giving the consensus layer a deterministic maximum: max_envelope_size = C + ceil(1.25 × gas_limit / 64)

AI Coding Agents Skip Approved Company Code Without Context

  • A DesignVerse study tested two popular coding agents on 15 private codebases (15-20 years old, 1M+ lines of code) across aviation, banking, defense, and other sectors.
  • Agents used each organization's own approved code in only 55-59% of tasks versus 92% with DesignVerse's own setup; otherwise they invented components or pulled public code despite internal alternatives existing.
  • Root cause: having access to a codebase isn't the same as knowing which files are the 'blessed' approved ones among a million lines, so agents guess.
  • Caveat: DesignVerse sells a competing solution and tested its own setup against typical agent configurations, so treat results as a warning rather than an independent benchmark.

Factory Engineering: A New Role Definition
