Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's First Jackson Hole Speech

  • Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole speech today (10 a.m. ET) at the Kansas City Fed's Economic Symposium, an annual gathering of central bankers and economists.
  • Warsh has committed to avoiding forward guidance (pre-announcing future policy moves), so investors should not expect specific signals on interest rate decisions.
  • Expected focus: 'big picture' topics like AI and productivity, long-term Treasury yields, inflation persistence, labor market conditions, and the Fed-Treasury relationship, rather than near-term rate guidance.
  • Trump appointed Warsh to lower rates, but the Fed has held rates unchanged for two consecutive meetings despite three colleagues voting for a hike in July.
  • Inflation remains above the Fed's 2% target; bond yields have climbed to multi-year highs as markets price in at least one more rate hike this year.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent and Warsh are publicly diverging on rate strategy, a rare split that opens room for more hawkish positioning inside the Fed.
  • Kansas City Fed's Jeff Schmid called current rates 'very accommodating' and noted the Fed is missing its 2% inflation target, a hawkish signal ahead of Warsh's remarks.
  • Five task forces formed after Warsh's first meeting are reviewing Fed monetary policy practices, with final reports due by year-end; limited progress updates expected today.
  • Ten-year Treasury yield ticked up 1 basis point (0.01 percentage point) to 4.69% as markets awaited the speech.
  • Market history: stocks have risen 7 of 10 times following past Jackson Hole speeches, but the average same-day return is actually slightly negative (-0.09%), suggesting caution.
  • If Warsh signals inflation-fighting as his top priority, it could flatten the yield curve (narrow the gap between short- and long-term bond rates), lift risk appetite, and boost the dollar.
  1. CNBC Morning Squawk: Frames Warsh's remarks as a credibility signal that shapes market expectations for future Fed chair decisions generally.
  2. Investopedia Pre-Market: Notes stock futures were flat and markets are in a holding pattern after big gains the prior day, awaiting the speech for policy clues.
  3. Bloomberg: Frames the speech as a 'yield tightrope' where the right message could flatten yields and boost the dollar, but markets are wavering with yields barely moving pre-speech.
  4. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Highlights escalating geopolitical and fiscal backdrop (Iran tensions, rising government debt) as context testing whether Warsh stays non-committal.

Alphabet's Steep Market Value Decline Amid AI Ad Concerns

  • Alphabet has lost $692 billion in market value since its recent peak, the largest decline among the 'Mag 7' (the seven largest mega-cap tech stocks).
  • Investors are questioning whether Alphabet's advertising dominance can survive disruption from AI-driven search and content tools.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Contrasts Alphabet's decline with Nvidia, which added in three months of 2024 profit an amount equal to its entire 2022 annual earnings, highlighting valuation disparities in tech.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Notes Marvell's stock also fell despite strong earnings, suggesting broader chip-sector unease beyond Alphabet alone.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Venezuela Weighs Leaving OPEC as U.S. Pursues Oil Field Stake

  • The U.S. is moving closer to securing an ownership stake (equity) in Venezuelan oil fields.
  • Venezuela is considering exiting OPEC (the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, a cartel coordinating oil production among member nations) after more than 60 years of membership.
  • This reflects a major political realignment in Caracas following Trump's actions against Nicolás Maduro's government and the U.S. taking control of Venezuela's oil sales.
  1. Bloomberg: Notes losing Venezuela would be a historic blow to OPEC's cohesion, marking one of the cartel's most significant membership losses since its founding.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

  • HP shares fell despite hitting record quarterly revenue because PC shipments dropped 16%, signaling weakness in a key business segment.

  • Kansas City Fed official Schmid warned that payment innovation (new ways for consumers to move money, reducing reliance on traditional bank deposits) will be 'very disruptive' to bank deposit flows.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 3

1. Nvidia's Delayed Rally and Ecosystem Investment Strategy

  • Nvidia stock jumped 8.7% a full day after earnings as investors digested strong Q2 results and bullish guidance backed by actual customer orders.
  • CFO Nicole Kress disclosed Nvidia earns revenue twice: once from hardware sales and again from a share of rental income from customers using its chips.
  • Nvidia's China business shows a gap in forward guidance, representing a potential bullish catalyst if trade restrictions ease.
  • Nvidia defends its investments in AI labs and startups as building an ecosystem where it wins across multiple scenarios, rejecting criticism of 'circular financing' (companies investing in each other to inflate mutual revenue).

2. AI-Powered Mortgage Refinancing Could Disrupt Bond Markets

  • Lenders using AI claim near-instant refinancing: Rocket Mortgage targets 10 minutes, Better.com claims 2 minutes, versus traditional weeks-long processes.
  • Morgan Stanley estimates two-thirds of borrowers forgo refinancing savings because the process is too burdensome.
  • Faster refinancing could destabilize mortgage-backed securities (bundled home loans sold to investors) if buyers demand higher yields to offset increased prepayment risk.
  • Regulatory and manual-process hurdles may make ultra-fast AI timelines aspirational rather than immediately achievable.

3. Earnings Roundup: Software Strength, Chip Sector Mixed Signals

  • Software and chip stocks broadly rallied on earnings, offsetting weakness elsewhere; Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff declared 'this is not the SaaSpocalypse' after beating expectations and raising guidance.
  • Three Fed officials warned about inflation ahead of Jackson Hole, adding pressure to Warsh's upcoming speech.
  • Market focus is also on University of Michigan inflation expectations and sentiment data due Friday.
Bloomberg 3

1. Nepal's Himalayan Flash Flood Disaster

  • At least 477 people have died and over 1,500 remain missing after flash floods triggered by a glacial collapse in a remote valley between Nepal and China.
  • Debris has dammed rivers and created unstable lakes, raising fears of further catastrophic downstream flooding.
  • Recovery costs will reach 'at least a few billion dollars' for Nepal, which is still rebuilding from a 2015 earthquake.
  • Chinese President Xi Jinping ordered faster glacier surveys across the Tibetan Plateau to prevent future disasters, as accelerating glacier melt from global warming raises regional hazard risks.

2. Trump-Canada Trade War Turns Personal

  • The US-Canada trade conflict has devolved into personal attacks, with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick blaming Bank of Canada Governor Mark Carney, who responded by invoking Canada's Indigenous heritage.
  • Harvard economist Ken Rogoff calls the dispute a 'bar brawl' but expects both sides to eventually back down.
  • Merchandise like Lake Ontario hats is selling rapidly amid the cultural pushback against US trade threats.

3. Radiant World Fraud Ensnares Glencore and Other Firms

  • Blue-chip firms including commodities giant Glencore lost substantial sums lending money and reputation to obscure iron ore trader Radiant World.
  • The case illustrates how sophisticated frauds can ensnare even major, sophisticated financial institutions.

  • Tariff refunds contributed significantly to record-setting corporate profits in the recent quarter.

2. Caretaking Costs Strain the 'Sandwich Generation'

  • The 'sandwich generation' (people financially supporting both aging parents and children) faces mounting caretaking expenses, particularly in rural areas.
