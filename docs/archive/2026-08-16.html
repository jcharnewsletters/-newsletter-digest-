Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 5

1. Retail earnings dominate the week ahead

  • Home Depot reports Tuesday, a read on home improvement demand amid high borrowing costs and weak housing activity.
  • Target reports Wednesday, with focus on discretionary spending and second-half outlook.
  • Walmart reports Thursday having beaten estimates in 15 of the past 16 quarters; results should move Costco, Kroger and Albertsons.
  • TJX, Lowe's and Ross Stores also report Wednesday–Thursday, alongside Analog Devices and Estee Lauder.

2. Fed July minutes and manufacturing data

  • July Federal Reserve meeting minutes land Wednesday, with investors seeking detail on the rate debate after three hawkish dissents from Lorie Logan, Beth Hammack and Neel Kashkari (hawkish = favoring higher rates to fight inflation).
  • Empire State manufacturing index Monday and Philadelphia Fed manufacturing report Thursday give regional U.S. factory readings.
  • Global PMI (Purchasing Managers Index — a survey of business activity in manufacturing and services) reports Friday show international conditions.

3. Asian tech and equipment earnings

  • Baidu reports Tuesday and Alibaba Thursday, testing Chinese e-commerce and tech health amid regulatory pressure.
  • Deere reports Thursday, reflecting farm equipment demand.
  • Results come amid questions on China's recovery and tariff impacts on U.S. importers.

4. Index change and sports betting catalysts

  • Reddit joins the S&P 500 Tuesday, replacing AvalonBay Communities and triggering passive index fund buying.
  • Premier League season kicks off Friday, a revenue driver for DraftKings, Flutter Entertainment and MGM Resorts' BetMGM.

5. BridgeBio Pharma beats Q2

  • BridgeBio topped Q2 estimates by $24 million, with Attruby sales up 311% year-over-year to $222.4 million.
  • Company is on track for over $1 billion in combined 2026 sales.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 2

1. Wendy's financial and operational crisis

  • Six straight quarters of same-store sales declines; U.S. same-restaurant sales fell 7% in Q2 2026 versus a 2.3% decline a year earlier.
  • Adjusted operating profit fell 13.2% year-over-year and 289 U.S. locations closed in the first half of 2026, out of roughly 5,700.
  • Balance sheet strained: $2.7 billion in long-term debt against $341 million in cash; the stock is down 65% over five years.
  • A dividend cut freed capital, but Bernstein notes net leverage at 5.0x sits at the top of the company's target range.
  • Billionaire Nelson Peltz is reportedly near a buyout deal for the second time this year, though CEO Bob Wright has yet to stabilize sales.

2. Why the brand may not be fixable

  • No signature sandwich to rival the Whopper or Big Mac; Wendy's is better known for baked potatoes than fries.
  • Pricing seen as too expensive as Burger King and McDonald's push higher quality and aggressive value menus.
  • No meaningful rewards program versus Chipotle, McDonald's or Starbucks, and the mobile app is an afterthought.
  • 'Fresh beef' and the square burger no longer differentiate; breakfast has 'failed mightily' and may be dropped in 2027.
  • Many locations haven't been updated since 1987, delivery orders arrive sloppy, and GLP-1 medications (appetite-suppressing weight-loss drugs) threaten fast food demand broadly.
