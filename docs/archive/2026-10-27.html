Anthropic files for mega IPO, revealing huge revenue growth but mixed profitability picture

  • S-1 filing (IPO registration document disclosing financials) shows $16.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, up from $4.6 billion for all of 2025 and $41 million in 2023.
  • Adjusted (non-GAAP) operating margin turned positive at 10% in H1 2026, swinging from a $1.8 billion loss to a $1.6 billion profit on that basis.
  • On a GAAP basis the company posted an $80.7 billion net loss, but this is largely a paper loss from revaluing convertible notes; true operating loss was $693 million.
  • Compute costs as a share of revenue fell sharply to 48% in Q2 2026 from 251% in Q1 2025, showing improving infrastructure efficiency.
  • High-value customers (paying ≥$100k/year) grew to 11,900 by September 30 from 1,500 at end of 2025; top five customers make up ~25% of revenue with no single customer over 10%.
  • Anthropic holds roughly $430-637 billion in long-term compute/cloud commitments through 2036 with Amazon (~$110-114.5B), Google (~$114.5B), Microsoft (~$44.9B), and SpaceX (up to $120.5B), with about $430 billion being fixed minimum payments.
  • Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are lead underwriters; Broadcom CEO Hock Tan personally helped arrange a $63 billion debt deal to support the IPO.
  • Seven Anthropic co-founders are each expected to hold stakes worth over $13 billion post-IPO.
  • Filing flags major risks: inadequate insurance industry capacity for catastrophic AI incidents, customer contracts that can be cancelled without notice, and uncertainty over whether 'commodity' (cheap/open-source) or 'frontier' (cutting-edge) AI models will dominate pricing power.
  • Revenue per employee is $6.6 million, second only to Nvidia's $7.2 million among major tech firms, suggesting an efficient business model relative to headcount.

Big Tech's AI spending spree raises profitability and ROI concerns

  • Meta's free cash flow turned negative for the first time in over a decade as AI spending surged, even as AI boosted ad revenue.
  • Meta is raising its stock offering to as much as $95-98 billion (up from $22.5 billion), split between a $35 billion underwritten offering and a $60 billion at-the-market program, to fund AI infrastructure—one of the largest equity deals in history.
  • Gartner estimates more than $3.5 trillion in AI infrastructure spending risks impairment (write-downs from assets losing value) by 2030 if current spending pace continues; providers are expected to spend nearly $1 trillion this year and $6 trillion more from 2027-2030.
  • Analysts (Saxo, Schwab, Seeking Alpha's Julian Lin) warn Wall Street is losing patience with AI costs, noting most current spending funds model training rather than guaranteed revenue-generating applications.
  • Apollo's Torsten Slok warns AI has delivered 'zero financial payoff' outside the tech sector itself, questioning broader ROI (return on investment) assumptions.
  • Goldman Sachs stands to earn large fees from Meta's offering and other AI mega-deals.
  • Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 27% of its workforce (~60,000 employees) across the US, Canada, India, and UK, effective December 31, amid continued tech layoffs.
  • SpaceX secured $40 billion in financing from Apollo Global Management to expand AI chip infrastructure.
