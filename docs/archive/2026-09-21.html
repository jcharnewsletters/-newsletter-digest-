Visa Closes Meme Coin Credit Card Loophole

  • Visa is shutting down a loophole where payment processors classified meme coin purchases under merchant category codes (MCCs, standardized codes identifying the type of business a merchant runs) for digital media or other non-crypto categories, allowing credit card funding and even cashback rewards on crypto buys.
  • The change forces payment processors and merchants to stop misclassifying cryptocurrency transactions, closing a workaround that let users buy speculative assets on credit and earn normal rewards.
  • This reflects broader regulatory and card-network pressure to enforce existing rules against credit-funded crypto purchases, tightening consumer access to speculative assets like meme coins through traditional payment rails.

TypeSafe AI Launches Jev, a Specialized Decision Model

  • TypeSafe AI released Jev, a model built for small categorical or probability-based decisions (choices, scores, yes/no questions) applied directly to structured input, without generating free text—positioned as cheaper and faster than full LLM (large language model) calls for routing, classification, and scoring.
  • Jev takes structured state (e.g., a support ticket and customer tier) plus pre-defined question types—Choice (pick one option), Score (ordered scale), and Noul (yes/no as a probability)—and returns probability distributions like {billing: 0.91, technical: 0.06} instead of text needing parsing.
  • Enables 'speculative fan-out': asking all questions in parallel against the same state, which batches faster and cheaper than chaining sequential LLM calls, while preserving confidence scores so high-confidence decisions can be automated and low-confidence ones escalated to humans.
  • Unlike typical pipelines that discard uncertainty, Jev keeps confidence scores as usable application state, letting teams set thresholds (e.g., auto-act above 0.9, confirm above 0.6, escalate below that) tuned to each decision's risk.
  • Early adopters use Jev for agent routing (filtering which requests need expensive reasoning models), safety-review layers grading whether LLM answers are supported, semantic database columns for SQL-based customer analytics (e.g., churn_risk=0.61), and browser tools like Chrome extensions filtering AI-generated content or skipping YouTube sponsor segments; Vercel reported Jev running 18x faster than the model it replaced in code-safety reviews.
