Anthropic Researcher Jacob Coxon Resigns, Sparks AI Extinction Debate

  • Jacob Coxon, who worked on pretraining at both OpenAI and Anthropic, resigned before his equity vested, publicly warning that AI labs are 'gambling with our lives.'
  • Coxon told NBC News that AI could achieve recursive self-improvement (RSI: AI using its own intelligence to help build even better AI) within 1-2 years, accelerating development cycles faster than labs can safety-test.
  • Coxon says competitive pressure between labs creates a Prisoner's Dilemma dynamic — if one lab slows down for safety while others race ahead, all labs are pushed toward riskier shortcuts.
  • He called for coordinated global action, including a possible temporary ban on improving model capabilities, to prevent catastrophic outcomes.
  • Anthropic's Alignment Science lead Evan Hubinger responded that the company 'earnestly believe[s] AI could kill all humans,' estimating over a 10% chance of AI-caused extinction within a decade.
  • Hubinger said current models pose low risk, but flagged self-improving systems as the critical danger point since control mechanisms for them don't yet exist.
  • Internally at Anthropic, this anticipated period is referred to as 'crunch time' or 'endgame'; the key threshold to watch is AGSI (artificial general superintelligence — a system applying superhuman intelligence across any domain to achieve goals), which is considered far riskier than narrow domain-expert AI.
