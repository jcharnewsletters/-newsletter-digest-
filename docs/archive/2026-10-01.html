PCE Inflation Cools More Than Expected, Fed Rate Path in Question

  • PCE inflation (Personal Consumption Expenditures index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge) rose 3.4% year-over-year in August, below forecasts of 3.7%.
  • Core PCE (excluding food and energy) also came in soft, around 3.0% vs. 3.3% expected.
  • ADP reported strong private-sector job additions—figures ranged from 90,000 to 990,000 depending on report cited—beating expectations and signaling labor market resilience.
  • Fed officials, including NY Fed President John Williams, signaled no urgency to hike rates, with markets now split between pricing a near-term hold and three hikes by mid-2027.
  • CME futures showed a 61% probability of a Fed rate hold in October following the data.
  • Friday's nonfarm payrolls report seen as the critical next catalyst for Fed policy direction.

Treasury Yields Surge to Multi-Decade Highs Amid Bond Market Turmoil

  • 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.34%, the highest since 2002, after rising 87.1 basis points (0.871 percentage points) in Q3—the sharpest quarterly jump since 1994.
  • 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.67%, a 24-year high.
  • Causes include strong economic data, energy-driven inflation concerns, heavy government and corporate borrowing (including for AI infrastructure), and unwinding of the Japanese yen carry trade (where investors borrowed cheap yen to invest in higher-yielding global bonds, now being forced to sell as Bank of Japan rates rise).
  • Years of central bank quantitative easing (mass bond-buying to suppress yields and inject money into the economy) had kept 'bond vigilantes' (investors who sell bonds to protest fiscal recklessness) at bay; that subsidy is now ending.
  • Despite the selloff, the S&P 500 remains near record highs and credit spreads stay tight, with investors reading higher yields as a sign of economic strength rather than distress.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Strategist Ed Yardeni coined 'Revenge of the Bond Vigilantes' as investors pressure governments over fiscal imbalances.

CNBC Morning Squawk Report described yields falling, not rising, as the bond market braces for softer economic data ahead—offering a contrasting near-term take.

Bloomberg Tied elevated global yields to persistent inflation alongside oil climbing above $100/barrel and diesel export restrictions straining farmers and voters.
