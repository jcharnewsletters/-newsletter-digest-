Tech selloff and Meta's massive spending spook markets, trigger flight to safety

  • Nasdaq fell 2.6% and S&P 500 dropped 1.2-1.3% Thursday as tech stocks broadly sold off, erasing much of the week's earnings-driven optimism.
  • Meta stock fell 7-8%, wiping out roughly $124 billion in market value, after disclosing plans for up to $825 billion in long-term spending and raising 2026 capex (capital expenditure, spending on long-term assets like equipment and infrastructure) guidance to $135-145 billion.
  • Meta's capex jumped 80% to $33.94 billion in the quarter, outpacing its $32.79 billion operating cash flow for the first time since 2012, despite record $64.6 billion revenue (up 26%).
  • To fund the AI buildout, Meta's board approved a new non-voting Class C share structure allowing stock sales without losing voting control; the cost of insuring Meta's debt hit record highs.
  • Investors rotated out of risky growth stocks into safer assets: Treasury bond yields fell sharply (bond prices rose) as demand surged, and mortgage rates dropped for the first time in months as yields slipped below 5%.
  • Tesla and SpaceX also revealed outsized future spending plans (including a ~$100 billion chip plant estimate for Tesla) for ventures like robotaxis, robot scaling, and reusable rockets, adding to investor unease about AI costs outrunning available cash industry-wide.
  • Apple, by contrast, is sitting on its cash rather than spending heavily on AI infrastructure, making it a preferred 'AI hedge' for investors seeking stability.

Oil prices surge to multi-month highs amid Middle East tensions

  • Brent crude hit roughly $113 per barrel, its highest level since May, posting its best close in months.
  • The price spike followed Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia, prompting Saudi Aramco to evacuate staff from its Dhahran headquarters.
  • Delayed U.S. military action against Iran (reportedly held off until after midterm elections) is adding further uncertainty, with markets pricing in risks like embassy evacuations and leaked Israeli strike plans even without an immediate attack.
  • Sustained high oil prices are feeding into inflation concerns just as the Federal Reserve's upcoming policy meeting approaches ahead of the midterms.

California's Proposition 40 billionaire wealth tax

  • The measure includes strong anti-evasion provisions targeting trusts and offshore wealth, and has prompted some billionaires—including Google co-founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin, PayPal's Peter Thiel, and former Uber CEO Travis Kalanick—to leave California before the Jan. 1, 2026 cutoff.
  • High-profile California billionaires facing the tax include Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and executives at Salesforce, Snap, Airbnb, and DoorDash.
