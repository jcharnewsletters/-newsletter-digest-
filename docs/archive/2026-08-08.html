July jobs report shocks: payrolls contract by 23,000

  • U.S. nonfarm payrolls (the monthly count of jobs added or lost outside farming) fell 23,000 in July versus consensus forecasts of +80K to +88K — a miss in direction, not just size.
  • The decline erased the prior month's roughly 20K gain, and May–June revisions cut a combined 103,000 jobs from earlier reports.
  • Unemployment ticked down to 4.1% from 4.2%, but only because 260,000 people left the labor force entirely, masking the weakness.
  • Biggest losses: local government education (-50K), retail (-20K), financial services (-14K); healthcare added just 22K.
  • Stocks rallied on the weak print (S&P 500 +0.6%, Nasdaq +1.3%) as traders read fewer jobs as less reason for the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh now has political cover to hold rates steady; Capital Economics says only a big inflation surprise next week would justify a September hike.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Richmond Fed's Tom Barkin called the labor market 'not loose, not tight' — employers neither hiring nor firing, with zero workforce growth from demographics and lower immigration.
  3. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Frames the weak data as clashing with strong corporate earnings, raising questions about economic momentum into Q4.

SpaceX stock swings after blowout Q2 and lockup overhang

  • Q2 revenue hit $7.8B, up 90% year-over-year and $980M above estimates; GAAP EPS of -$0.09 beat by $0.20.
  • Shares jumped 20% on the week, but $260B of the company's $1.77T valuation has been erased as of Thursday's close since its June IPO.

  • Swings reflect investors weighing rapid revenue growth and Elon Musk's ambitious spending plans against a very high starting valuation.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Reads the stock's advance despite earnings headwinds and the expiring lock-up as evidence of strong investor appetite for the aerospace name.

Techpresso 5

1. OpenAI slows Astra model over cyber risks

  • Internal tests flagged "critical" cyber capabilities in the unreleased Astra model, triggering OpenAI's 2023 preparedness framework (a formal safety protocol requiring extra safeguards when a model shows dangerous advanced abilities).
  • Stricter controls now include isolated testing environments and monitoring for agentic uses (tasks where an AI acts autonomously to reach a goal).
  • First public instance of a major AI lab slowing a model's development over cyber vulnerabilities; the model was not tied to the recent Hugging Face exploits.
  • Release timeline is uncertain until tighter security requirements are met.

2. Cloudflare launches Kitesurf, a browser built for AI agents

  • Kitesurf is a cloud-hosted browser designed for AI agents rather than humans, built in 12 weeks on Cloudflare's serverless Workers platform (code that runs on demand without managing servers).
  • Drops visual features like tabs and extensions, optimizing instead for context windows (how much text an AI can consider at once), token costs (charges based on input/output length), and prompt injection attacks (hidden commands smuggled into content to hijack an AI).
  • Uses less CPU and memory than Chromium for tasks like screenshots and HTML extraction, and has passed over 215,000 web platform compatibility tests.
  • Free during beta as part of Cloudflare's Browser Run service.

3. US sanctions Iran-linked crypto exchanges

  • Treasury sanctioned exchanges Shelbit and Aban Tether for moving money outside traditional banks and funding the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (an Iranian military entity).
  • IRGC-linked wallets sent over $1 million to Shelbit and received over $2 million back; Aban Tether processed millions with sanctioned Iranian exchanges Nobitex, Wallex, Bitpin, and Ramzinex.
  • Individual Siavash Kayvanpour and his firms in Georgia, Poland, and the UAE were also sanctioned; wallets he controls sent over $2 million to Nobitex, Iran's largest crypto exchange.

4. Flock pitched turning ride-share dashcams into a surveillance network

  • Plan would convert hundreds of thousands of Uber, Lyft, and delivery driver dashcams into mobile license-plate scanners as drivers work.
  • Relied on a partnership with dashcam maker Nexar covering roughly 350,000 devices; Flock says it pitched the idea to customers in August 2025 but never executed the deal.
  • Unlike Flock's stationary pole-mounted cameras, the mobile approach would greatly widen data collection, potentially without drivers' or ride-share companies' knowledge or consent.

5. Smaller items

  • Anthropic now lets separate Claude Code sessions message each other across macOS and Linux, so they can share findings and coordinate on shared projects.
  • Apple may raise iPhone 17 prices as soon as August 10, ahead of expected increases tied to next month's iPhone 18 Pro launch.

There's An AI For That (TAAFT) 5

1. AI designed 700,000 viruses — 16 survived

  • Stanford and the Arc Institute used AI to generate and synthesize 700,000 novel viral sequences from scratch; 16 proved infectious in lab tests.
  • Johns Hopkins researchers confirmed no regulatory framework currently exists to govern this capability.
  • Demonstrates AI can design biological threats far faster than traditional lab research, amplifying pandemic-risk concerns among biosecurity experts.

2. OpenAI slows Astra over autonomous hacking risk

  • Internal safety tests could not rule out Astra hitting the highest cyber risk tier — meaning it can independently write working zero-day exploits (attacks that abuse software flaws nobody has discovered or patched yet).
  • Development is slowed until safeguards can stop the model from finding and weaponizing new attack vectors without human approval.
  • First major OpenAI capability pause driven by concrete evidence of dangerous autonomous cyber behavior in testing.

3. SpaceX to build 100M-square-foot AI chip fab near Houston

  • The complex would be five times larger than Toyota's Georgetown, Texas plant, the current world record holder for building size.
  • Phase one costs $16.8 billion and creates 3,000 jobs.
  • Output targets custom AI chips for Tesla's Optimus humanoid robots and autonomous Cybercabs, signaling expectations that AI hardware demand will dwarf current capacity.

4. Roku launches first all-AI TV channel

  • Fairground AI streams AI-generated films 24/7 with synthetic ads in the commercial breaks, produced without human creators or production crews.
  • Founder pitched it as avoiding 'social feed slop,' claiming higher editorial standards than algorithmic content platforms.
  • First major broadcast rollout of fully synthetic media at scale, testing audience appetite for AI-made prime programming.

5. AI compressing the industrial-revolution timeline to 5 years

  • Chip industry insiders argue past industrial revolutions took roughly 40 years to reshape society; AI is doing it in about 5.
