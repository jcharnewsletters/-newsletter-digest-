Apple vs. Amazon earnings cap Big Tech week: cloud/AI rewarded, consumer hardware punished

  • Amazon stock surged (~4%) as AWS (Amazon Web Services, its cloud computing division renting servers and computing power to businesses) grew 37% year-over-year to $42.2B, its fastest pace in 18 quarters.
  • Amazon operating profit jumped 52% to $16.2B and the company raised full-year guidance, with advertising also growing double digits as a third revenue pillar beyond retail and cloud.
  • Amazon posted -$7.6B free cash flow (cash left after operating costs and investment) for a second straight negative quarter yet kept $197-$202B capex guidance — investors rewarded the AI spend anyway.
  • Apple beat on most metrics but fell ~4% initially: reported revenue up 15% year-over-year with iPhone sales $54.2B (+22%), while services growth and China sales disappointed and forward guidance was cut.
  • Consensus takeaway: markets now reward AI infrastructure monetization over pure profitability, leaving Apple as an 'AI-light' laggard despite being the best-performing Magnificent 7 name year-to-date (+22% vs Amazon's +4%).
  1. CNBC Morning Squawk: Puts Apple quarterly revenue at $93.7B with iPhone sales flat year-over-year, citing developed-market saturation and Chinese competitive pressure.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Flags Apple anxiety over rising chip costs and argues its lack of AI announcements pushes it into a back seat this earnings season.
  3. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Frames the split as markets explicitly paying for AI capex over cash generation, using Amazon's negative FCF versus Apple's beat.
  4. CNBC Morning Squawk: Notes analysts repositioning toward a 'growth in cloud/AI, caution elsewhere' stance for Q3.

Covered by: Investopedia Pre-Market, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

Q2 GDP growth slowed to 1.5%, missing forecasts

  • U.S. second-quarter GDP (gross domestic product, the total value of goods and services produced) grew 1.5% versus 2.1% consensus.
  • Cooling consumer spending and contracting government outlays drove the miss, signaling loss of economic momentum.
  • Slower growth pressures equity valuations and corporate earnings outlooks while raising recession chatter.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Pairs the miss with core PCE inflation (the Fed's preferred price gauge, excluding volatile food and energy) stuck at 3.3% year-over-year in June, limiting Fed room to cut rates.

Covered by: Investopedia Pre-Market, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

30-year Treasury yield hits multi-decade high as Fed credibility is questioned

  • The 30-year Treasury yield (interest rate the U.S. government pays on 30-year debt) reached its highest level in nearly 20 years.
  • Bond market is signaling skepticism that the Fed can bring inflation down, following Wednesday's Fed presentation.
  • Mortgage rates climbed to a one-year high as yield pressure spread across the curve, raising borrowing costs for households and businesses.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Fed governor Christopher Waller conceded 'staring at inflation until it melts before our withering gaze is not an option.'
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Reports the 10-year Treasury yield falling to 3.84% and a weaker dollar, framing bonds as pricing in rate cuts.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness hedge fund forced to unwind; Citadel steps in

  • The $1B+ AI-focused fund run by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner hit margin calls (lender demands for extra collateral to back borrowed-money trades) during July's tech selloff.

  • Ken Griffin's Citadel brokered a rescue/acquisition of the portfolio, averting a more acute blowup.

Prediction markets boom collides with regulators

  • Kalshi's monthly trading volume exploded from $226M in late 2024 to over $33B by July 2026, a roughly 2,500% year-over-year jump after becoming Robinhood's event-betting infrastructure.
  • New York Governor Hochul and Attorney General James sued Kalshi, alleging an illegal gambling business and seeking forfeiture of all illegal gains plus consumer restitution.
  • Core legal question: whether event contracts (tradable bets on real-world outcomes) are legitimate hedging instruments or plain gambling — most retail users appear to be speculating.
  • Massachusetts, Nevada and Washington have already restricted activity via litigation; Kalshi's defense is its status as a federally regulated commodity exchange under CFTC oversight.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Robinhood posted record $1.3B quarterly revenue, up 32% year-over-year, driven partly by surging event-contract and prediction-market revenue.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)
