Ethereum Scaling Proposal (RowDAS/EIP-8371) Cuts Node CPU Load by 99%

  • Currently, 2,000+ qualifying nodes each independently reconstruct 32 blobs (data chunks used for transaction batches) in 5.18 seconds per slot; RowDAS distributes this work across 128 row-based subnets, cutting individual reconstructor time to 40 milliseconds.
  • A two-dimensional cell matrix lets column subnets retain custody authority while row subnets pool reconstruction work, allowing ordinary nodes to reach the erasure-coding threshold (minimum data points needed to recover lost information) without high-stake infrastructure.
  • A Prysm prototype using real KZG cryptography (a mathematical proof system for verifying data) exists but lacks mainnet-scale testing; a duty-sharded fallback variant preserves 91% of CPU savings for a phased rollout.

AI-Designed Drug Rentosertib Shows Signs of Slowing Biological Aging

  • Insilico Medicine's rentosertib, an AI-designed drug originally developed to treat idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (a chronic lung-scarring disease), showed biological age reduction as a secondary effect in a 42-patient Phase 2a trial.
  • Six independent aging clocks (AI/biomarker-based systems that estimate biological age from blood or protein data) converged on the same direction of effect, with estimates ranging from about 2.7 up to 6 years of apparent age reduction depending on the clock and dose.
  • The dose producing the clearest anti-aging signal differed from the dose with the best lung-function results, suggesting the drug may affect multiple biological pathways beyond its original disease target.
  • Patients were compared against the broader UK Biobank database rather than a matched placebo-only cohort; scientists caution that aging clocks are not yet standardized and remain scientifically contested.
  • This is viewed as an early proof-of-concept that AI-driven drug design could yield genuine medical breakthroughs, with Anthropic's CEO suggesting such results may shift public sentiment on AI more than marketing ever could.

OpenAI's GPT-6 'Astra' Debuts With Viral Feats and Benchmark Controversy

  • Astra scored a near-perfect 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 (a benchmark testing abstract reasoning) and a perfect 450 on South Korea's CSAT exam while using fewer computational tokens than rivals.
  • When ARC Prize researchers tested the identical model with stripped-down support scaffolding, the score collapsed to 62.7%, showing published benchmarks can reflect testing setup as much as raw model capability.
  • Astra performed viral stunts—clearing all 48 levels of CAPTCHA tests (including 'wiggle' puzzles), autonomously mining Minecraft diamonds, and generating an interactive 3D anatomical atlas—though these are one-off feats requiring manual agent control, not reliably repeatable results.
  • Access is limited to ChatGPT Pro's paid tier, with viral capabilities requiring extra manual setup beyond typical subscriber reach; a head-to-head with Anthropic's Fable 5.1 found Fable more polished on everyday tasks like email writing.
  • The model reportedly uses 'recurrent depth' (also called opaque recurrence) training, which cycles queries through internal layers repeatedly, performing large amounts of reasoning in latent space invisible to users—raising safety concerns from researchers about reduced auditability, though OpenAI says reasoning depth is within 2x of GPT-4 and chain-of-thought remains largely readable.

The Neuron Details the specific benchmark collapse (99.9% to 62.7%) and recommends building custom real-task evaluations (correctness, fix-ups, time, cost) instead of trusting published scores.

Staying Ahead Direct head-to-head comparison with Fable 5.1 found Fable's professional email draft more polished, with an unprompted offer to shorten it, and noted Fable is more widely accessible via Claude.ai's default paid tier.

Superhuman (Zain Kahn) Safety researcher Ryan Greenblatt called the recurrent-depth training approach 'the single worst development for AI security/safety to date' due to reduced monitorability.
