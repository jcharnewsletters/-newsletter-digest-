1. The nickel now costs 13.31 cents to make

  • The US Mint spends 13.31 cents to produce and distribute each nickel worth 5 cents, a loss of more than 8 cents per coin.

TLDR Crypto 7

1. CLARITY Act's 2026 passage odds cut to 10%

  • Odds of passage in 2026 dropped to just 10% after the bill missed the August congressional recess window.
  • Three blockers remain: ethics controls on government officials' crypto holdings, community banking lobbying that eroded Republican support, and concerns that BRCA (a developer protection mechanism) enables illicit finance.
  • Senate reconvenes September 14 and adjourns October 2 for midterm activity, leaving a 2–3 week window in which the bill would have to dominate the entire session.
  • SEC delays compound the problem: 'Reg Crypto' (a primary issuance pathway for cryptoassets) and the 'Innovation Exemption' (secondary trading of tokenized securities in DeFi) were both pushed again.
  • Commissioner Peirce's planned November departure compresses the timeline before crypto loses a friendly SEC vote.

2. Bitcoin soft fork BIP-110 died after two blocks

  • The proposed soft fork (a backward-compatible rule change to the blockchain) targeting Ordinals (data inscriptions stored on Bitcoin) drew only 2.53% miner support.
  • It survived just two blocks, reinforcing that Bitcoin upgrades need broad consensus to stick.

3. Anatomy of a stablecoin card swipe: settlement modernization

  • Traditional card programs force issuers to prefund idle capital across bank-day windows — a $1M daily program needs $4M prefunded over a long weekend.
  • Visa's 2021 decision to accept USDC (a dollar-pegged stablecoin) for settlement replaced multi-day ACH and Fedwire cycles with 365-day clearance.
  • Rain pioneered the first weekend and Christmas Day Visa settlement over stablecoins and holds principal memberships with both Visa and Mastercard.
  • Rain now serves Western Union, Whop, and Latin American neobanks launching cross-border programs without local correspondent banking relationships.
  • Next target is ISO 8583, the 1987 card messaging protocol, as AI agent-to-agent commerce uses stablecoins to bridge on-chain liquidity to card rails.

4. Ethereum Foundation drops Poseidon for SHA2/BLAKE2s

  • EF abandoned Poseidon after an 8-year, 8-figure investment, pivoting to the SHA2 and BLAKE2s hash functions.
  • Enabler is binary field-based SNARK design (SNARK = a zero-knowledge proof that computation was done correctly without revealing the data).
  • Binary fields natively handle bit operations, cutting SNARK overhead for standard hashes to 100x versus native CPU boolean compute — matching Poseidon's efficiency.
  • Benchmarks show 1M hash proofs per second on a laptop; production-grade leanVM targeted for 2027, with consensus, data, and execution layer deployments in 2028.
  • Recent attacks on lattice-based HAWK and isogeny-based SQIsign cement SHA2 as the minimal-assumptions post-quantum primitive, skipping years of cryptanalysis Poseidon needed.

5. Solana came within 4.5 points of a finality halt

  • On August 12, 28.83% of staked SOL went delinquent — 4.5 percentage points from the 33.34% threshold that triggers a finality halt (validators can no longer agree on blockchain history); 90 validators affected, 333 SOL of rewards forfeited.
  • Root cause: a malformed BGP (Border Gateway Protocol, the internet's routing system) default route at Teraswitch's Miami site propagated to European and APAC nodes, taking 94% of AS20326 offline.
  • That single routing provider carried 118.89 million SOL, over 25% of total staked supply; engineers fixed the corrupted route in under 10 minutes.
  • The incident exposes systemic concentration risk from single-provider infrastructure dependence.

6. Tokenized T-bills pull in $367M in 30 days

  • Tokenized US Treasury bills saw $367M of inflows over 30 days: Superstate +$184.2M, Securitize +$182.8M, Franklin Templeton +$86.2M.
  • Institutions cite blockchain settlement to eliminate multi-day bank-day windows and correspondent banking, with 24/7 accessibility.
  • Avici expanded EUR-to-EURC on-ramps (fiat-to-stablecoin conversion) with €1 SEPA deposits, though card spending still settles in USD via Visa conversion with FX markup.

7. Early-stage token buybacks may signal weak reinvestment options

  • Hyperliquid routes 97%+ of fees to buybacks (~$2B) and Pump burns 50% of revenue — unusual for three-year-old startups with thin moats (defensible competitive advantages).
  • Contrast: Amazon reinvested for 20 years before returning capital, and Google is raising debt to fund growth despite huge cash flow.
  • Interpretation: either teams see no reinvestment opportunities, or fee pools are so contestable that tokens must pay holders their own revenue to retain them.

1. Anthropic explains Claude's text watermark

  • Code output stays largely unaffected because strict grammatical constraints leave little room for word substitution.

2. 1M-token context windows in Codex

  • An OpenAI engineer published a one-command/config edit to unlock 1M-token context windows (a token is roughly 4 characters; bigger windows let a model 'remember' more code and chat history) in Codex GPT-5.6 Sol.

3. Forward Deployed Engineers: value or services drag

  • If per-rollout cost plateaus instead of falling, the model drifts into services overhead; Andrew Ng argues the field needs far more general AI engineers than FDEs.
