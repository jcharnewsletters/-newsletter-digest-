July retail sales fall 0.6% as consumer sentiment sours

  • Retail sales dropped 0.6% month-over-month in July, the sharpest decline since May 2025, versus expectations of +0.1% growth.
  • Online sales fell 2.2% and auto dealership sales fell 2%, pointing to broad-based spending weakness.
  • University of Michigan consumer sentiment (a monthly survey of how households feel about the economy) fell sharply in August on war, higher bond yields and inflation worries.
  • Declines in sentiment were steepest among older, lower-income and non-college-educated consumers.
  • Only 8% of consumers expect their income to outpace inflation over the next year.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Frames the gap as 'stocks up, vibes down,' with the S&P 500 nearing a 7,800 record even as household confidence erodes.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 5

1. The nickel now costs 13.31 cents to make

  • The US Mint spends 13.31 cents to produce and distribute each nickel worth 5 cents, a loss of more than 8 cents per coin.
  • Penny production already stopped last year as copper, zinc and nickel prices climbed.
  • Dimes and quarters still earn positive seigniorage (the profit between production cost and face value) and have subsidized penny and nickel losses, but that model is breaking down.

2. Common Cents Act advances in the Senate

  • The bill formally ends penny production and sets standardized rounding rules so cash prices round to the nearest nickel.
  • It authorizes Treasury to reformulate the nickel with cheaper metals instead of a proposed 10-year production freeze; the House passed similar framework language.
  • Precedent: the half-cent was scrapped in 1857, silver was removed from coins in 1965, and the penny switched from copper to zinc in 1982.

3. Jane Street lost $15 billion in July

  • The trading firm's loss followed the 'Situational Awareness' AI-driven market disruption, exposing systemic risk in algorithmic (computer-run) trading strategies.

4. Stripe to buy OpenRouter for over $7 billion

  • The payments company is consolidating AI model routing and infrastructure (software that directs requests to different AI models) into its platform.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) 3

1. Bubble debate: CAPE at 1999 levels, but margins are records

  • The cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE, today's price divided by 10 years of inflation-adjusted earnings) has reached dot-com-bubble levels, fueling bear warnings.
  • S&P 500 net profit margins hit 16.9% in Q2, the highest since 2009, versus single-digit margins at the 2000 peak.
  • 86% of companies beat earnings estimates (five-year average: 78%), with 15% revenue growth and 50% earnings growth.
  • Forward price-to-earnings (based on expected future profits) is 20x, only modestly above the 19x 10-year average; the pandemic profit collapse still sits inside CAPE's 10-year average, inflating it.

2. Concentration risk in the rally

  • Excluding Alphabet and Amazon cuts Q2 earnings growth from 50% to about 30%.
  • Analyst price target dispersion on S&P 500 companies hit a 10-year high and market leverage has risen noticeably.

3. Movers and AI datapoints

  • Applied Materials posted record Q3 revenue of $9.12 billion, with semiconductor systems sales of $7.04 billion, beating estimates.
  • JPMorgan initiated coverage of Sandisk with a $2,250 price target, lifting the stock.
  • Anthropic Q2 revenue topped $11.5 billion; Alibaba's AI models passed 3 billion downloads, ahead of Google and Meta.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 1

1. Dollar caught between oil strength and a softer Fed

  • The historic inverse link between crude and the dollar broke in 2022 after Russia invaded Ukraine and the US became a major energy exporter.
  • Rising oil now supports the dollar via export strength and safe-haven demand, while fading rate-hike expectations pull the other way.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent recently sold euros rather than dollars to buy yen, signaling concern about dollar weakness.

1. Trump curbs joint drills with South Korea

  • Trump ordered the Pentagon to 'substantially reduce' joint military exercises with South Korea, citing frustration over limited Iran war support.
  • Using military commitments as leverage may unsettle other Asia-Pacific allies about the reliability of US security guarantees.

2. Americans hit beef fatigue

  • Beef sales volumes fell 0.3% in the 13 weeks ending mid-July year over year, the first major consumption decline after nearly two years of record prices.
  • Chicken consumption keeps rising as ample supply holds prices down.
  • High beef prices worry the administration before midterms because staples like eggs, ground beef and gasoline shape voters' inflation perceptions.

3. Deadly storms in Indiana and Hawaii

  • At least six died in Indiana storms and floods; Trump approved the state's federal emergency aid request.
  • Storm Lala, weakened from hurricane strength, killed one person in Hawaii and continues dumping heavy rain.
