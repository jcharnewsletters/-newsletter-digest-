Brooks Running Thrives Under Berkshire Hathaway Ownership

  • Brooks Running, owned by Berkshire Hathaway since 2006 (acquired via Fruit of the Loom subsidiary, made standalone in 2012), has grown revenue 12% year-to-date in 2026 while rivals Nike and Under Armour struggle.
  • Year-to-date regional sales growth: 9% in Americas and Asia-Pacific, 24% in Europe, Middle East, and Africa.
  • Brooks holds the No. 1 market share position in US specialty running retail, led by its Ghost and Adrenaline GTS shoe models.
  • Berkshire's decentralized ownership model gives Brooks patient capital (capital invested without pressure for immediate returns) and operational autonomy, enabling 5-10 year strategic planning instead of quarterly earnings focus.
  • Brooks CEO Dan Sheridan calls Berkshire ownership 'a competitive advantage... in every market,' contrasting with the quarterly pressure facing competitors.
  • Warren Buffett stepped down as Berkshire chairman in September 2026, but his long-term capital allocation philosophy continues to underpin Brooks' performance.
