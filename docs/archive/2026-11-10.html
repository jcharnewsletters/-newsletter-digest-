Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces espionage scandal and board investigation, stock drops amid succession fears

  • Huang admitted to an affair with a woman US intelligence links to China's Ministry of State Security, prompting Nvidia's board to open an independent investigation on Sunday.
  • Nvidia stock fell roughly 4.3% Monday even as the S&P 500 rose and AI peers like AMD, Micron, and Taiwan Semiconductor rallied—a divergence seen only a few times since 2001.
  • Nvidia is down about 21-25% from its October 6 record close; the company said it found no evidence company secrets were compromised, Huang pledged cooperation, and China's Foreign Ministry called the spy allegations groundless.
  • President Trump publicly stated the FBI is investigating Huang, adding federal scrutiny; a class action lawsuit was also filed against Nvidia following the disclosures.
  • Unlike Nvidia's three prior 20%+ drawdowns since 2024 (tied to market or AI-cycle concerns), this selloff centers on leadership/succession risk, as Huang has led the company for 33 years (700,000%+ stock return) with no publicly designated successor.
  • Analysts (e.g., Wedbush) say Huang's political credibility advocating for looser AI export controls with the US government may be impaired regardless of investigation outcome.
  • Despite the scandal, broader tech and AI-related indices have not seen major declines, suggesting investors are separating Nvidia's leadership issue from broader AI sector strength; Morgan Stanley advised clients to buy the dip, estimating tougher data-center politics would only delay (not derail) AI spending, pushing some projects from 2028 to 2029.

Anthropic pursues record-breaking IPO amid AI valuation jitters

  • Anthropic cleared its 15-day SEC waiting period and is beginning its marketing roadshow, targeting $80B-$100B (or up to $90.4B combined IPO and private placement per Bloomberg) at a $1.25T-$1.75T valuation.
  • The deal aims to surpass SpaceX's ~$200B/six-month-old record as the largest IPO in history, with trading expected to begin shortly after the roadshow.
  • Revenue jumped roughly 10-fold year-over-year to $16.3B, but net loss also ballooned to $80.7B as Anthropic invests heavily in data centers (facilities that train and run AI models) to compete with Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI.
  • Major tech firms including Nvidia, Alphabet, and Broadcom are expected to invest in the raise.
  • The IPO comes amid a broader AI sector selloff and deteriorating market conditions, creating a dilemma: delaying looks weak, but launching now risks a disappointing valuation (some reports suggest Anthropic may price lower than its earlier ~$2 trillion target).

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) CEO Dario Amodei has called for AI labs to slow development pace, but says pausing is economically unfeasible given intense competition; broader AI economics also depend on chipmakers, memory suppliers, foundries, and energy providers regardless of which model wins.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Anthropic may launch IPO marketing as soon as Tuesday at a lower valuation than previously targeted.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief A market strategist called Anthropic's IPO 'the only thing that matters the rest of this year' for overall AI market sentiment.

Bloomberg CEO Dario Amodei will discuss the IPO with JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon in a live-streamed event; filing documents reportedly had last-minute issues adding to market nervousness.
