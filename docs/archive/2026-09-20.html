T. Rex Body Temperature Redefined

  • New geochemistry technique analyzing fossilized teeth pinned the T. rex's body temperature at approximately 97°F (36°C), remarkably close to human body temperature
  • Previous belief that T. rex was reptilian and cold-blooded is incorrect—the dinosaur was warm-blooded (meaning it generated its own heat internally rather than relying on external warmth), enabling a more active lifestyle and wider geographic range since it didn't need sun exposure to regulate temperature

Human-Mouse Brain Hybrid Research Advances

  • Stanford team transplanted human brain organoids (lab-grown clusters of human brain cells) into mice engineered to lack their own cortex, creating animals whose brains contain millions of living human neurons

  • Creators are pushing for stronger ethical oversight before similar research expands to other animal species

01

Peter Sellis on Team Design, Talent, and Product Strategy

  • Peter Sellis, first PM at Snapchat and ex-Head of Product at Discord, shared unfiltered advice in a 97-minute podcast interview on team design, talent management, and product strategy.
  • Great PMs embody three oxymorons: rigid yet flexible (firm on direction, flexible on implementation), confident yet humble (inspire teams but admit mistakes), and ambitious yet patient (aggressive goals but patience for compound progress).
  • High-performing teams should be designed with radical autonomy and clear accountability like decentralized organizational units rather than traditional hierarchies with heavy approval layers.
  • Managers should 'ride their best people hard' since elite performers are motivated by intense pressure and challenging work, not light-touch management.
  • The Peter Principle (the idea that people get promoted until they reach a role they're incompetent at) is a real organizational risk; companies must distinguish strong individual contributors from those actually suited to management.
  • Snapchat's advertising business underperformed because it diverted focus from improving the core social product; growth mostly comes from strengthening core features, not adjacent revenue streams.
  • System dynamics thinking (a method for understanding how different parts of a system feed back into each other) helps product managers spot when new initiatives are draining energy from core product work.
