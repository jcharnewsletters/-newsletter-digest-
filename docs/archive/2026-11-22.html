What to evaluate in a vector database

  • Vector databases are an overlooked bottleneck in AI projects; most failures stem from poor data retrieval rather than weak models or prompts.
  • A vector database stores data so AI systems can retrieve information by semantic meaning (what something is about) rather than just keyword matching.
  • Three major AI patterns depend on vector databases: Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG, a technique where models pull relevant context from your own data to answer questions instead of relying only on training data), agentic memory (letting AI agents recall context across conversations without replaying entire histories), and semantic search.
  • AWS Marketplace released an evaluation guide covering hybrid search (combining semantic and keyword search), scaling and hosting trade-offs, and metadata filtering (filtering results by extra properties beyond similarity) to help teams choose a vector database.
  • Teams should also check embedding pipeline fit—how well a database integrates with their chosen embedding model (the AI component that converts data into vector form) and framework.
  • Free trials and developer-tier pricing on AWS Marketplace let teams prototype vector database solutions without upfront license fees.

What it takes to be a great product manager in the AI era

  • Sherif Mansour (Head of AI and Product Craft at Atlassian) argues PMs are thriving in the AI era because their core function—building a 'shared brain' (a shared understanding across engineers, designers, and executives of vision, constraints, and trade-offs)—becomes more critical as complexity increases.
  • This shared understanding is especially valuable when teams face novel, unclear problems requiring constant alignment; PMs skilled at communication see rising influence as uncertainty increases.
  • PMs fall into three archetypes: the Artist (intuitive, creative, strong at vision-setting but weaker on analytical rigor), the Scientist (data-driven, systematic, but prone to analysis paralysis), and the GM/General Manager (a balanced generalist operator, common among founders, but lacking deep expertise in any one area).
  • Recognizing your archetype helps you build complementary teams and delegate to cover your natural weaknesses.
  • Atlassian is hiring more junior PMs than ever because they lack preconceived notions about 'how things are done,' making them faster learners of new AI tools than experienced hires.
  • Pairing junior PMs with strong mentorship scales PM capacity: juniors handle execution while seniors focus on strategy and guidance.
  • The most common mistake new PM managers make is 'rowing the ship' themselves (continuing to do individual-contributor work) instead of 'directing it' (setting direction, trusting reports to make decisions, and measuring outcomes rather than micromanaging).
  • Atlassian offers dual-track career paths so senior PMs can advance via a deep specialist/craft track rather than only through people management, retaining expertise and preventing high performers from being pushed into management roles they don't want.
