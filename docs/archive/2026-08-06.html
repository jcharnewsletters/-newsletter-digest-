CoinDesk 3

1. Ether is the only major token whose holders are underwater

  • ETH trades near $1,900 against a realized cost basis (average price all holders paid) of $2,450, putting the aggregate holder base at a loss.
  • Bitcoin by contrast trades 17% above its realized price of $52,900.
  • ETH hit a similar below-cost-basis level in early 2025 before bottoming and recovering, making the metric the key signal to watch across BTC, ETH and XRP.

2. Bitcoin's low implied volatility masks hidden risk

  • Bitcoin's 30-day implied volatility (the market's expected price swing priced into options) has fallen to 36%.
  • Cheap volatility encourages large leveraged positions, so any move can trigger dealer hedging that amplifies swings.

3. Why bitcoin isn't following the stock market rally

  • The S&P 500 added $2.1 trillion in August — roughly crypto's entire market cap — while bitcoin gained just 2%.
  • The equity rally is concentrated in AI and semiconductor names, where bitcoin has minimal correlation or exposure.
  • Macro tailwinds hit equities first: falling oil cuts corporate costs immediately, but must shift inflation expectations and Fed policy before reaching bitcoin.
  • Real Treasury returns are the highest since 2008, making cash attractive, and most traders are waiting for an expected October bottom under the four-year halving cycle.

1. Circle's Arc mainnet launches September 16 with BlackRock, Visa and Mastercard as validators

  • Founding validators (entities that verify and confirm blockchain transactions) include BlackRock, DTCC, Galaxy, Mastercard, MoneyGram, Standard Chartered and Visa.
  • Over 100 ecosystem builders are already testing the private mainnet (the live production blockchain network).
  • Circle received a federal trust bank charter for Circle National Trust from the OCC, enabling regulated banking operations.

2. Crypto rails for AI agent payments

  • Card networks block autonomous agents via OTP, 3D Secure and chargebacks, and Stripe's $0.30 flat fee ate 97% of the typical ~$0.31 agent transaction in 2025.
  • Seven projects are building the stack: Kite AI (purpose-built EVM Layer-1, $35M raised), PayAI (35.8M transactions), Thirdweb (pay-per-call API), Eco (stablecoin orchestration), Crossmint (virtual cards), FluxA (co-wallet with zero-knowledge-batched settlement), PaymanAI ($13.8M from Visa/Circle/Coinbase).
  • The x402 agentic payment standard is gaining momentum with Cloudflare's new wallet/identity system and Monetization Gateway.

3. AO on Arweave targets trustless AI inference

  • AO is a compute layer on Arweave (a blockchain for permanent data storage) that runs AI agents without depending on centralized APIs.
  • It uses signed hashpaths (cryptographic proof chains) to make AI inference verifiable.
  • Permanent storage costs roughly $22 per gigabyte.

4. Blockspace launches as Ethereum staking and block-production infrastructure

  • The company self-caps at 15% of total stake to limit centralization risk.
  • It aims to capture ETH revenue from transaction fees via block production (assembling and proposing new blocks to the chain).

5. The economics of Layer-1 token value capture

  • Inflation-funded staking rewards dilute non-stakers without creating protocol revenue; only fee burns and staker distributions produce real tokenholder surplus.
  • Gas demand is elastic: a 10% fee increase cuts transaction volume 6–8% (short-term sensitivity 0.6–0.8).
  • Value-based fees (a percentage of transfer notional) capture willingness-to-pay better than flat compute pricing; Solana and Ethereum's network effects give fee streams more pricing power than valuations reflect.

Meta's Muse Spark 1.2 and Muse Code push Meta back into the frontier race

  • Meta launched Muse Code, its first coding agent, paired with the top-tier Muse Spark 1.2 model, going head-to-head with Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's dev tools.
  • Muse Spark 1.2 scored 82.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (a benchmark measuring how well a model completes real command-line tasks), landing between Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Terra.
  • It also hit 59.3% on the DeepSWE coding benchmark and 54 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 40 cents per task — about half the cost of Kimi K3 for equivalent results.
  • Muse Code introduces persistent background sub-agents (independent task runners that keep context across a session) and parallel isolated worktrees (separate copies of a codebase so multiple agents can work without conflicts).
  • Nearly all the performance gain comes from agentic scaffolding rather than base-model improvement.
  1. Everyday AI: Frames Muse Spark 1.2 as jumping into the highest model tier alongside Grok 4.5 and GPT-5.5 at lower cost.
  2. AI Daily Brief: Reads Meta's steady quarterly gains and efficiency as genuine competitive repositioning after a rough year.
