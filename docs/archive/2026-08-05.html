SpaceX's first quarterly earnings beat overshadowed by $18.4B capex surge

  • Revenue nearly doubled year-over-year to $7.81B, beating the $6.8B consensus estimate; net loss per share was $0.09 versus $0.29 expected.
  • Q2 capital expenditures (capex, spending on equipment and infrastructure) hit $18.4B, up from $10.1B in Q1 and $2.8B a year ago, with $15.8B going to SpaceX's AI business for computing capacity and cloud services.
  • Starlink subscribers doubled year-over-year to 12 million.
  • Stock reaction was mixed across reports — a 9.4% gain on earnings optimism in one account, declines in others — with investors focused on cash burn rather than the beat.
  • Shares trade at roughly 40x sales and over 100x adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization — a rough measure of operating profit), demanding near-flawless execution.
  • Company wants a bigger slice of the $600B U.S. wireless market, combining satellite direct-to-cell with ground-based terrestrial mobile infrastructure to challenge AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: The real test is Thursday, Aug 6, when a massive insider lockup period (a restriction preventing insiders and early investors from selling shares) expires and could flood the market with stock.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Analysts split: Agar Capital and Kenio Fontes flag no margin of safety, while Geneva Investor is bullish on SpaceX as a space-based AI infrastructure play with an Nvidia partnership for orbital data centers.
  3. CNBC Morning Squawk: Frames the spending as Starship manufacturing and orbital infrastructure buildout, with Musk prioritizing scale over near-term returns and record launch cadence masking financial-model questions.

Iran deal hopes near, with a 60-day Strait of Hormuz shipping agreement reportedly imminent

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC a US-Iran nuclear deal could be announced as soon as Wednesday, or 'today or tomorrow.'
  • US, Iran and Oman are preparing to announce an interim 60-day deal to normalize shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a vital energy conduit, per Axios.
  • Donald Trump said talks with Iran are 'moving along very nicely.'
  • WTI crude fell to its lowest level in a week on the deal hopes; Treasury yields also fell as geopolitical risk eased.
  • Yemen's Houthis issued a fresh threat against Middle East shipping, tempering optimism and nudging oil slightly higher.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: A deal would lower oil prices, boost consumer spending power and reduce pressure on the Fed to hike rates.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Counterpoint: oil edged higher on geopolitical tensions, pressuring energy-consuming stocks while helping traditional producers.
  3. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Separately, U.S. long-range missile stockpiles are reported running critically low after the Iran campaign, though the White House disputes the urgency.

Palantir surges 29% on 93% revenue growth as S&P 500 and Dow hit records

  • Palantir jumped 29% after quarterly revenue rose 93%, driven by AI and government demand for its data-analytics platform; the CEO called it an 'otherworldly' quarter.
  • S&P 500 hit a record 7,736.52 and the Dow also closed at a record; futures point to modest further gains Wednesday.
  • Deutsche Bank said Palantir 'looks like a time traveler' already living in the AI future, uniquely positioned to help federal and private clients implement AI.
  • Takeaway: the AI pie may be big enough for hyperscalers (large cloud computing providers), chipmakers and specialized software firms to all win.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Reads the surge as evidence the AI software sector isn't dead despite fears that proliferating AI models would commoditize software.
