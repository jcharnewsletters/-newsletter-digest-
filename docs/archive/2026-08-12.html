July CPI inflation report lands today — the week's key Fed catalyst

  • The July Consumer Price Index (CPI — the government's main gauge of what households pay for goods and services) is out at 8:30 a.m. ET.
  • Consensus looks for a 0.1% monthly rise and headline inflation cooling to 3.4% year over year.
  • A soft print would ease pressure on the Federal Reserve and, per Yahoo Finance, could rule out a September rate hike; a sticky print pushes bond yields up and pressures stock valuations.
  • Traders are split between 'sticky' (persistent) inflation that delays cuts and cooling prices that justify aggressive reductions, so S&P 500 and bond markets are unusually sensitive to a surprise.
  • A Producer Price Index (PPI — wholesale prices charged by producers) report follows Thursday.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Argues the report may pass without major volatility yet still reveal why interest rates are headed even higher.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Notes investors have been hedging (buying downside protection) into the release, with inflation still above the Fed's 2% target.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg, Investopedia Pre-Market

AI earnings from CoreWeave and Super Micro revive the AI trade

  • CoreWeave (a cloud provider that rents out GPU computing power for AI) more than doubled quarterly revenue to $2.58 billion and disclosed a $104 billion revenue backlog.
  • CoreWeave added a fresh $21 billion Meta contract on top of an existing $14 billion commitment running through 2032; the stock jumped about 13% after hours.
  • Super Micro Computer surged on strong guidance, with both names riding AI data-center demand.
  • S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100 futures rose 0.3% and 0.7% after two down days, as tech beats lifted sentiment.
  • Micron and TSMC echoed the message that demand for AI-related memory and chips remains astronomical; South Korean shares rallied on Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: About one-third of tech companies have raised full-year guidance and CoreWeave narrowed losses with 'expanding operating leverage' (profits improving faster than revenue), prompting the question of whether tech earnings ended the selloff.
  2. Investopedia Pre-Market: Cautions the tech sector stays volatile despite the beats, reflecting doubts about valuations and growth sustainability.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg, Investopedia Pre-Market

Strait of Hormuz standoff keeps oil traders guessing

  • Trump claims the US has 'total control' over the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint between Iran and Oman that carries roughly 20% of global oil shipments.
  • Iran says the strait stays closed until its conditions are met, while US Energy Secretary Wright claims Persian Gulf oil exports are near pre-war levels.
  • Oil prices wavered as traders doubted a near-term restoration of flows, with Washington–Tehran talks deadlocked.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Data centers push into US neighborhoods

  • The share of US home sales within 5 miles of a large data center doubled since 2018 to 1.5% of 2024 sales, with no measurable hit to home values yet.
  • 53% of Americans oppose nearby data center construction over electricity and water use, noise and aesthetics; New York, Texas and other states have enacted temporary moratoriums.
  • Wall Street Breakfast notes data center growth is also driving job creation and rising home values in tech-heavy counties.

Covered by: Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha)

Market breadth widens beyond mega-cap tech

  • The equal-weight S&P 500 (which gives each company the same weight rather than sizing by market value) is outperforming the market-cap weighted index, showing gains spread across segments.
  • Investopedia flags sector rotation as tech retreats from highs and other sectors gain ground, raising the question of whether the market is structurally stronger.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Investopedia Pre-Market

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 2

1. New York City targets Amazon's subcontracting model

  • The Delivery Protection Act, from Council member Tiffany Cabán and backed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani, would force last-mile delivery operators to directly employ workers instead of using third-party subcontractors.
  • It creates a licensing system for warehouses and distribution facilities, sets minimum safety and training standards, and makes companies liable for worker protections at their own sites.
  • Amazon says it would have to relocate delivery operations outside the city; Amazon and allies spent over $5 million campaigning against the bill.
  • If passed it could be a template for other cities regulating Amazon, the second-largest US private employer, and would likely face legal challenges like NYC's blocked pied-à-terre tax.

2. Other corporate and policy moves

  • Oracle is planning another round of layoffs this month while spending billions on AI infrastructure.
  • Google's Gemini app passed 1 billion users as competition with ChatGPT intensifies.
  • The Pentagon opened the Golden Dome portal to broaden bids on the $185 billion program; Northrop unveiled its Raid Hunter air defense system and Lockheed pushed hypersonic missiles.
  • Senator Sanders called for freezing AI development; Oracle's Ellison threatened to pull Paramount out of California absent a settlement; CME will launch index-based hockey futures in September.
CNBC Morning Squawk 1

1. Consumer spending is cracking

  • Recent retail sales and consumer spending data came in unexpectedly weak, contradicting expectations of steady growth.
  • Consumption is about 70% of US economic activity, so slower spending typically foreshadows weaker corporate profits.
  • That clashes with still-resilient earnings reports, raising doubts about whether current profit margins are sustainable.
Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) 4

1. The S&P 500 is getting cheaper while setting records

  • The index has notched 26 record highs in 2026 even as its price-to-earnings multiple (share price divided by annual earnings per share) compressed by 7 points.
  • Earnings contributed 20.3 percentage points of the index's 14% total return — profit growth, not investors paying up, drove the gains, the reverse of 2023 when multiple expansion supplied three-quarters of returns.

2. Two mega-caps supply a third of all profit growth

  • Q2 profit growth is 50% across the S&P 500, the highest since 2021, but falls to 32% excluding Alphabet and Amazon.
  • Alphabet earned $9.11 per share versus a $2.88 estimate; Amazon earned $5.75 versus $1.82.
  • Neither stock has returned in proportion: Alphabet still lags the index and Amazon trails modestly.

3. Alphabet's capex binge dings the stock

  • Alphabet raised its 2026 capital spending forecast to as much as $205 billion and closed a $25 billion debt offering for AI data centers.
  • The stock fell 3% despite the earnings blowout.

4. Housing warning signs

  • A collapse in lumber futures preceded July's weak home sales, suggesting leading indicators are flagging cooling housing demand.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 1

1. 'Chipflation' is reshaping enterprise IT budgets

  • A structural memory chip shortage could persist another two years, per JPMorgan, driving 'Fear of Missing Procurement' buying.
  • Companies are pulling forward PC, server and storage-array purchases to lock in prices ahead of further increases.
  • Morgan Stanley names HPE, Everpure and Lenovo as beneficiaries; price pressure is spreading to consumer electronics, with Apple and others raising prices.
Bloomberg 4

1. Two-thirds of requested AI data-center power may be 'phantom'

  • Wood Mackenzie estimates utilities will commit to only 28% of the 1,066 gigawatts requested by data center developers — capacity equal to over 1,000 traditional nuclear reactors.
  • Developers' 'shotgun approach' to pitching projects inflates forecasts and complicates grid planning.
  • Cost overruns on grid infrastructure are typically passed to customers via higher utility bills.

2. Trump defends secret flight out of Turkey

  • The White House indicated Trump was aboard Air Force One while he was secretly flown out of Turkey last month on a different aircraft.
  • Trump said the Secret Service and military demanded the plan over unspecified safety threats, raising transparency questions.

3. Moderate wins Wisconsin governor primary

  • Moderate Democrat David Crowley upset Democratic Socialist Francesca Hong, signaling an ideological shift in the party.

4. Also in Bloomberg

  • A feature on how 'romantasy' publishing (romance-fantasy fiction) spawned a billion-dollar cottage economy.
