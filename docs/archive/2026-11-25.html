October PCE Inflation Reading Expected to Stay Hot, Cementing December Rate Hike

  • Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Price Index—the Fed's preferred inflation gauge—expected to rise to 3.8% year-over-year in October, up from 3.6% in September.
  • Core PCE (excludes volatile food and energy prices) forecast at 3.2% year-over-year versus 3.1% prior; even the 0.3% month-over-month consensus is seen as 'too hot'.
  • Market pricing shows 97.5% probability of a 25-basis-point (0.25 percentage point) Fed rate hike in December; sticky core inflation also fueling talk of a larger 50-basis-point move.
  • Q3 GDP second estimate also due; a hot PCE paired with strong GDP would likely force continued Fed tightening and risk an equity selloff, while a cooler reading would be market-favorable.
  • 10-year Treasury yields hit multidecade highs around 5% (5.34% in October, highest since 2002) before retreating to near 4.9%–5%, driven by sticky inflation and heavy government borrowing.
