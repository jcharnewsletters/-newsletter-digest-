South Korea Moves to End Its 7-Year ICO Ban

  • South Korea implemented a broad ICO (Initial Coin Offering—a fundraising method where companies sell new cryptocurrencies to investors) ban in 2017 after high-profile fraud cases; the government now plans to lift restrictions and create a regulated framework.
  • New regulations will likely include investor protections, disclosure requirements, and registration processes for projects seeking domestic token fundraising.
  • The reversal reflects growing recognition that blanket bans push legitimate projects overseas while hindering domestic blockchain innovation.
  • South Korea's move could influence other Asian markets to adopt clearer ICO frameworks rather than outright prohibitions, signaling a broader shift from early regulatory overreach toward more nuanced, risk-based crypto oversight.

CFTC Allows Crypto to Count Toward Derivatives Platform Capital Requirements

  • CFTC staff advisory clarifies that bitcoin, ether, and payment stablecoins (cryptocurrencies pegged to stable assets like the US dollar, used for transfers) can count toward the one-year operating cost coverage component of financial resources requirements for derivatives platforms.
  • Firms must still maintain separate cash or highly liquid securities to satisfy liquidity requirements—crypto cannot fully replace traditional capital reserves.
  • The advisory provides regulatory clarity on native asset holdings without requiring a full rulemaking cycle.
02

Ethereum Foundation Doubles Security Team for Post-Quantum Upgrade

  • EF doubled its protocol security team, citing AI as a greater near-term risk than quantum computing since AI models now find production bugs faster than humans can review them.
  • Targeting December 2028 (community goal) with a 2029 internal deadline and January review, to replace vulnerable elliptic curve encryption with hash-based signatures (a quantum-resistant cryptographic method).
  • Three planned changes: hash-based validator keys for consensus, a migration path to hash-based wallet signatures, and hash-based blob commitments; EF warns the upgrade is harder than the Merge and will require updates to BLS tooling, wallets, multisigs, and staking pools.

  • Stellar's Protocol 30 ('Bell') introduces muxed contract addresses using 64-bit IDs to consolidate customer funds into a single smart account instead of one contract per customer.

Epochal: xAI Founders Launch AI Startup for Time-Traveling Predictions

  • Three former xAI founders launched Epochal, a startup whose Time Portal lets AI agents browse archived internet snapshots from any specific past date.
  • Time Portal offers three APIs: Search (browse the web as of a past date), Fetch (retrieve historical page versions), and Sandbox (run time-gated agents).
  • Core thesis: by training on simulated historical internet states, AI models can learn patterns enabling superhuman predictions about future events.
  • Use cases include financial strategy backtesting, historical counterfactual analysis, and agent evaluation without leaking future data into training (a common pitfall in forecasting models), targeting enterprise decision-making.

The Code Explicitly framed as solving the ML problem of future-data leakage in reinforcement learning (training models by rewarding good decisions) environments and forecasting evaluations.

Major AI-Driven Mathematics Breakthroughs: Riemann Hypothesis and Matrix Multiplication

  • Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge, with AI assistance, claims to have solved the Riemann Hypothesis, one of math's most famous unsolved problems, via a 17-million-line proof.
  • The Riemann proof has already been independently verified by two research groups, exceeding typical journal publication standards, though some mathematicians remain resistant to accepting AI-generated proofs.
  • Separately, OpenAI published a preprint claiming to have pinpointed the exact exponent (2.25) for fast square matrix multiplication, a decades-old open problem, with matching upper and lower bounds across all mathematical fields.
  • OpenAI's matrix multiplication paper includes a Lean formalization (a machine-checkable proof framework) but has not yet been peer-reviewed by the mathematical community.

TLDR (Tech) If the Riemann proof withstands scrutiny, Alpöge would become one of history's most prominent mathematicians, underscoring AI's accelerating ability to crack decades-old problems.

Getting Data Ready for AI: Start with the Business Question

  • Problem: when agents access scattered definitions across spreadsheets and people's heads, they draw wrong conclusions even from accurate numbers (e.g., one system books revenue at contract signing, another at payment).
  • Solution: pick one business question (e.g., 'why did margins fall?'), then work backward to identify the data and definitions it depends on.
  • A semantic layer (a shared set of agreed definitions and calculations) tells agents what numbers mean; a data dictionary (a catalog of every table and field) helps them find and read data correctly.
  • Each fix becomes reusable for the next project; agents approving payments need a higher accuracy bar than internal dashboards.

01

What Makes an AI Harness Work: The Four Layers

  • An AI harness (the software and setup that wraps around an underlying model to make it useful) matters more for real-world results than the model itself, even when two systems share the same engine.
  • Context: everything the model knows about you, your business, and the task — a purpose-built harness can access your full CRM (customer relationship management system) versus a general-purpose harness receiving only pre-selected snippets.
  • Tools: capabilities beyond text generation, like web search or file creation/reading, letting the AI act on information rather than just describe it.
  • Skills: procedural instructions the model can follow, turning general intelligence into task-specific execution; Guardrails: permission boundaries defining what the model can and cannot do.
02

Purpose-Built vs. Horizontal Harnesses: The Tractor Analogy

  • General-purpose harnesses (ChatGPT, Claude) work like family cars — optimized for anyone, anywhere, but lacking domain-specific knowledge about your business.
  • Purpose-built harnesses are custom-built for specific jobs, like a tractor designed for farming; same underlying engine, entirely different context/tools/skills wrapped around it.
  • Key difference: a CRM with AI inside sends only selected context to the model; an AI harness with a CRM inside lets the model choose from your entire data store for richer answers.
  • Managing context in a horizontal harness requires ongoing 'context engineering' — connecting tools, keeping data fresh, flushing stale information — which purpose-built harnesses handle automatically.
03

Why The Same Model Produces Different Results

  • Access to context is the primary variable: even identical AI models generate better answers when equipped with comprehensive background information versus fragmentary requests.
  • Example: a purpose-built personal CRM harness called YouSpot accesses every contact, email thread, and meeting note, while a generic CRM only sends the AI whatever the CRM developer decided to include.
  • This gap persists even when both systems use the same underlying LLM (Large Language Model, the core AI engine), proving harness architecture — not raw model power — determines practical utility.
04

Integration Strategy: Both Harnesses at Once

  • Users can run general-purpose harnesses (ChatGPT/Claude) and purpose-built ones (like YouSpot/Breeze) simultaneously, connected through MCP (Model Context Protocol, a standard that lets AI apps communicate with each other).
  • HubSpot's Breeze is a purpose-built harness for marketing/sales/growth, while HubSpot also opens its infrastructure to third-party harnesses, letting 300,000+ customers route Breeze capabilities into Claude or ChatGPT.
  • This layered approach mirrors real life: most people own both a car and a tractor because each solves different problems — software is evolving to support AI agents as first-class users, not just people with keyboards and mice.
