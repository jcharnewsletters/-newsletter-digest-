  • Oracle invoked a force majeure clause (a legal escape clause claiming unforeseeable circumstances prevent contract performance) on its 2.45 GW, $165B New Mexico data center (Project Jupiter), citing natural gas pipeline delays that could push completion past the 2028 deadline.

  • Oracle stock fell 3% on the news; its credit default swaps (insurance against Oracle defaulting on debt) hit record highs, its 2056 bond yield crossed 8% (vs. 7.5% average for B-rated bonds), and the $18B in project loans are trading below 90 cents on the dollar, signaling market distress.
  • Developer Blue Owl Capital's Stack Infrastructure has a history of pulling out of Oracle deals, having previously abandoned a $10B Michigan data center financing.

Regulatory Scrutiny Intensifies on Prediction Markets

  • New York filed a civil lawsuit against Polymarket, alleging it operates as an unlicensed gambling exchange and unregistered securities exchange, and is seeking to shut down its operations in the state.
  • New York claims Polymarket (a platform where users bet on real-world event outcomes using crypto-based financial contracts) lacked required licenses and consumer protections for user funds.
  • Separately, the CFTC's Division of Market Oversight warned that prediction contracts on event 'mentions' (bets on whether specific words or attendance occur) carry heightened manipulation risk under the Commodity Exchange Act.
  • CFTC has already charged a former White House teleprompter operator and ex-Rep. George Santos over Kalshi 'mention market' trades made using advance knowledge of outcomes.
  • CFTC instructed exchanges to assess whether people who control a contract's outcome face independent obligations that deter manipulation, setting a new compliance baseline.
