Oracle's Project Jupiter data center hits financing trouble via force majeure notice

  • Oracle invoked a force majeure clause (a legal escape clause claiming unforeseeable circumstances prevent contract performance) on its 2.45 GW, $165B New Mexico data center (Project Jupiter), citing natural gas pipeline delays that could push completion past the 2028 deadline.
  • Project Jupiter is backed by $18B in bank financing from roughly 20 lenders and is part of the broader $500B+ Stargate initiative with OpenAI and SoftBank.
  • Oracle stock fell 3% on the news; its credit default swaps (insurance against Oracle defaulting on debt) hit record highs, its 2056 bond yield crossed 8% (vs. 7.5% average for B-rated bonds), and the $18B in project loans are trading below 90 cents on the dollar, signaling market distress.
  • Developer Blue Owl Capital's Stack Infrastructure has a history of pulling out of Oracle deals, having previously abandoned a $10B Michigan data center financing.
  • Oracle has posted negative free cash flow (spending more cash than it generates) for five straight quarters, burning $29.1B total, as $28.5B in last-quarter capex outpaced $23.1B in operating cash flow.
  • If Oracle were downgraded from Baa2/BBB- to junk status, $120B of its bonds would be automatically removed from investment-grade indexes, forcing index-fund sales.
  • Bloom Energy, which supplies fuel cells for the project, fell 3% on the news, though analysts note its $1.2B-$2.8B Oracle contract could be redirected to other projects.
