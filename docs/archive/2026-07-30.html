Fed holds rates but three officials dissent for a hike; Warsh scraps forward guidance

  • The FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee, the Fed panel that sets US interest rates) held its benchmark rate at 3.5–3.75% in a 9–3 vote.
  • Three dissents in favor of a rate hike (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) marked the first time since 2016 that three members dissented in the same direction, up from zero dissents previously.
  • New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is eliminating forward guidance (advance signals about future rate moves) and the dot plot (each official's individual rate projection), pushing forecasting responsibility onto markets.
  • Long-term Treasury yields jumped despite no rate change: the 10-year up 7 basis points to 4.68% and the 30-year up 12 basis points to 5.22%, a level last seen during the 2008 financial crisis.
  • Bond traders are effectively pricing in fewer rate cuts than the Fed has signaled, a warning sign that often precedes equity moves.
  • Stocks sold off into the close: S&P 500 -1.5%, Dow -2.2% (over 1,000 points), the Dow's worst day since April 2025.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Warsh called the internal split 'a good family fight' and argues less Fed information will make investors sharper, at the cost of more short-term volatility.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Warsh's press conference was so unclear that multiple journalists opened with 'I'm confused' or 'I need help here'; he claimed fighting inflation and supporting the labor market are not contradictory.
  3. Bloomberg: Some investors rotated capital to higher-yielding markets like Australia and Europe, depressing US Treasury prices, while metals traders benefited.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

Microsoft soars, Meta slides as AI capex spooks investors

  • Microsoft posted $90 billion in revenue versus $87.4 billion consensus, with earnings up 31%.
  • Azure (Microsoft's cloud-computing division) crossed $100 billion in annualized revenue for the first time, growing 43% year-over-year, while capex stayed under budget at roughly $42 billion.
  • Microsoft's remaining performance obligations (revenue already under contract but not yet booked) swelled 84% to $678 billion.
  • Meta beat on revenue at $60.8 billion versus $60.2 billion expected, but earnings of $6.18 per share badly missed the $7.14 consensus; shares fell about 9%.
  • Meta raised full-year capital expenditure guidance again, by $5 billion to $130 billion annually, and its free cash flow (cash left after operating costs and investment) nearly evaporated — similar to Google's near-zero cash generation.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Frames it as a strategy divergence: Meta gambling on AI returns while Microsoft proves profitability through cloud efficiency.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Positions the Meta reaction as one of three market headwinds, alongside rate fears and Middle East war, with the Nasdaq 100 entering correction territory.

Covered by: Investopedia Pre-Market, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

Iran conflict spreads across the Middle East, pushing Brent above $90

  • Brent crude (the global oil benchmark) climbed over 7% back above $90 per barrel as Iran-backed militias struck Saudi facilities for a second straight day.
  • The US ran a two-hour retaliatory operation against forces attacking American bases, hitting multiple regional targets including in Egypt.
  • Drones struck two LNG (liquefied natural gas — fuel used for power and shipping) tankers off Egypt's Mediterranean coast, the first such incident in Egyptian waters since fighting began in February.
  • Saudi Arabia and Iraq have been drawn into the flareup, widening the conflict beyond the original Iran-Israel theater.
  • US crude stockpiles are rapidly depleting on elevated demand, amplifying the price move.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Iran is negotiating for up to 400 Chinese air-defense missiles, signaling a prolonged conflict with no clear resolution.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

1. Chip rout deepens, and a lesson in leveraged ETFs

  • The semiconductor sector has fallen 25% since June 22 as SK Hynix disappointed and Nvidia and Micron declined.
  • The 3X leveraged Direxion Daily Semiconductor Bull ETF (SOXL — a fund that aims to triple the sector's daily move) lost 63% over that stretch because daily rebalancing compounds losses.
  • The plain SOXX semiconductor ETF is still up 51% year to date, illustrating the cost of leverage.
  • Broader market breadth is strong: 72% of S&P 500 companies trade above their 200-day moving averages, the best since December 2024.
