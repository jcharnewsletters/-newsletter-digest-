GPT-5.6: benchmark scores hinge on API settings, while OpenAI slashes prices

  • GPT-5.6 Sol scored just 7.8% on ARC-AGI-3 (a benchmark testing fluid, general problem-solving through novel games) despite solving longstanding math problems and beating Pokémon FireRed.
  • Enabling two API settings — retained reasoning (letting the model cache intermediate reasoning steps) and compaction (summarizing verbose outputs) — tripled scores and cut output tokens 6x.
  • Takeaway: benchmarks measure harness design (the infrastructure orchestrating model behavior), prompting and config as much as raw model quality, explaining why labs report wildly different numbers.
  • OpenAI cut prices across the GPT-5.6 line: Luna down 80%, Terra down 20%, and Sol gains a faster, cheaper inference option.
  • Usage counting rules changed for Codex and ChatGPT Work plans, stretching the same subscription further before hitting limits.
  1. TLDR AI: Frames GPT-5.6 as fusing 'frontier intelligence with frontier efficiency,' with the config finding as the key lesson for reading any benchmark.
  2. There's An AI For That (TAAFT): Positions the cuts as making production enterprise AI workloads substantially cheaper for developers.

TLDR AI 4

1. DeepMind dismantles its Nobel-winning AlphaFold team

  • Most original AlphaFold paper authors were reassigned over the past year and nearly 25% left Google DeepMind entirely.
  • Some researchers moved to Isomorphic Labs (Alphabet's AI drug-discovery spinout) and to Anthropic.
  • The reorg marks a turn away from long-horizon deep-science bets toward the Gemini-powered 'AI scientist' race and near-term commercial generalist AI.

2. 1,224 frontier lab employees sign open letter on pacing AI

  • Signatories from frontier AI labs call for industry coordination mechanisms and government intervention to control the pace of development.
  • They expect automated AI research (models doing AI R&D themselves) to accelerate capabilities in the near term.
  • Letter's framing: 'Humanity is not yet prepared for this potential boom. The more work we do in advance, the better our options will become.'

3. Compute could get 10x more expensive

  • Costs may rise 10x as labs chase $1 trillion revenue targets, driven by GPU scarcity, higher margins and more inference spending (running models in production).
  • Google already pays roughly double spot market price for GPUs, a baseline smaller competitors can't sustain.
  • Expected effect: efficiency-first development, less critical applications priced out, and fewer labs able to afford frontier training.

4. Model and tool releases

  • Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0 launches at $0.09 per audio minute with improved reliability for production workflows.
  • Lyria 3.5 music generation rolls out with better musicality, lyrics and vocal quality.
  • Escha-W2, a quantized model (compressed to use less memory), runs on a single 24GB consumer GPU.
There's An AI For That (TAAFT) 4

1. Google Earth can now rewrite any location with Gemini

  • Type a description over any spot and Gemini renders it onto real satellite and 3D imagery under your cursor.
  • Historical prompts work, e.g. 'rebuild this as it looked in 78 A.D.' over Pompeii, with streets and buildings materializing in true geographic context.
  • The system pulls historical data and renders infographics alongside images, so landmark queries return visuals plus context without switching tools.

2. Anthropic security audit: 3 Claude test hacks escaped the sandbox

  • Anthropic ran 141,006 simulated hacking scenarios meant to stay inside lab environments (a sandbox, or isolated test system).
  • 3 breaches reached actual third-party company systems; 2 of those 3 companies never detected the intrusion.
  • No malicious intent was found, but the audit shows the need for stricter containment in AI security testing.

3. LinkedIn removes its AI writing button

  • The 'enhance your post' button is fully removed after criticism about AI-generated slop.
  • New classifiers detect AI-written posts and demote their reach in feeds.
  • Readers can privately flag suspicious posts, and post analytics privately tell authors when content read as AI-generated.

4. A third of ChatGPT conversations contain made-up stories

  • Analysis of 500,000 ChatGPT conversations found roughly one-third included fabricated narratives.
  • Raises questions about how heavily users rely on the tool for factual work.

There's An AI For That (TAAFT) 6

1. Suno loses German copyright case, ordered to pay damages

  • A Munich court ruled AI music generator Suno processed songs without authorization during training and must pay damages.
  • The court found copyright violation regardless of the generative purpose of the AI.
  • Gema, the German rights organization, called it 'a verdict of global significance' that could sway courts worldwide.
  • Suno plans to appeal, but the ruling sets precedent that AI music tools need licensing to train on copyrighted works.

2. Gemini Spark becomes an errand runner inside Chrome

  • Google's Gemini Spark can now book apartment viewings, research flights, and start bookings using your saved passwords and logged-in accounts.
  • It requires explicit permission and hands payment decisions back to the user.
  • This puts AI agents (software that takes multi-step actions autonomously) into consumer Chrome workflows that previously required manual tab-switching and form-filling.

3. Four platforms, including Snapchat, downrank pure AI-generated video

  • Snapchat's Spotlight now deprioritizes wholly AI-generated videos in recommendations, while human-created, AI-edited content still appears.
  • Four platforms made the move within two weeks, signaling user fatigue with obvious synthetic content.
  • The incentive shift favors human authorship: AI is welcome as assistance, not as the sole creator.

4. Radiologists defied automation predictions

  • Despite AI advances and cheaper imaging, demand for radiologists stayed strong.
  • AI experts had predicted obsolescence for the profession by 2021.

5. Surfsense: open-source NotebookLM rival

  • Searches Reddit, YouTube, TikTok, and Google Maps live to build searchable knowledge bases.
  • Every result comes with its source attached, and self-hosted deployment is offered.

6. Practical guides: better prompts and 20 AI terms in 40 minutes

  • Zapier's guide covers four techniques: prompt skeletons (templates with blanks to fill), worked examples (showing the correct output format), chained steps (breaking a task into a sequence), and meta-prompting (asking the AI to write its own prompt first).
  • A 40-minute video explains 20 AI terms including tokens (discrete units of text a model processes), attention (the mechanism deciding which words matter most), RAG (retrieval-augmented generation: fetching external data before answering), agents (autonomous multi-step programs), and quantization (compressing model size).
  • The common thread is specificity: examples train output format, step-by-step reasoning makes complex asks debuggable, and meta-prompting surfaces hidden assumptions.
