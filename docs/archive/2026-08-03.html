US and Japan stage rare joint yen-buying intervention

  • Japan and the US conducted their first joint yen-buying operation in 15 years (central banks selling dollars to buy yen to prop up the currency) after the dollar-yen rate weakened past 160.
  • Japan holds $1.14 trillion of US Treasury debt; a collapsing yen pressured Japanese institutions to sell Treasuries, which would push US yields — and mortgage and credit costs — higher.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent telegraphed the operation at a Camp David cabinet meeting; Trump called it 'a signal of friendship' with Japan and good for the global economy.
  • Both governments say they will not hesitate to intervene again; the yen surged on reports of further action beyond last week's move.
  • Treasury also published new criteria to identify currency manipulation, including off-balance-sheet transactions and disguised commercial activity by major trading partners.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Frames the move as a shift away from Treasury's historically hands-off currency stance toward an active intervention policy, with the pace of major interventions accelerating.
  2. Bloomberg: Notes the coordination stands out because currency interventions have become far less common globally in recent decades.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Trump calls off Iran strike; oil slides about 6%

  • Trump cancelled planned military strikes on Iran and signaled fresh US-Iran talks starting Monday, after Saudi Arabia and other Middle East allies urged restraint.
  • WTI crude fell 6.2% to $79.43 (Seeking Alpha cited crude futures down 5.9% to $79.72) on the de-escalation.
  • Iran's foreign minister said talks with Oman are in final stages on a new shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global oil flows.
  • OPEC+ completed its planned output increase and is weighing even larger supply hikes following the Iran conflict.
  • Exxon and Chevron executives said high fuel prices are likely to persist whether or not the Iran conflict continues, pointing to structural supply constraints.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Cautions this is another pause in a pattern of stop-start deals that have repeatedly fallen through, keeping the conflict a live volatility driver.

Covered by: Bloomberg, Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)

AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb held merger talks

  • The two drugmakers held early-stage talks on a tie-up that would create a roughly $400 billion pharmaceutical company, per Financial Times and Bloomberg reporting.
  • AstraZeneca shares fell on the news; any deal would need board sign-offs and regulatory approval and may never be completed.
  • A combination would consolidate major cancer, immunology and cardiovascular drug portfolios and reshape global pharma.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Situational Awareness hedge fund blows up on leveraged AI bets

  • Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund, which grew from hundreds of millions to $45 billion in under two years, made concentrated, highly leveraged bets on semiconductors, memory chips and AI infrastructure.
  • A sector rotation (investors rapidly shifting money out of one industry into another) wiped out the positions overnight Wednesday.
  • The fund lost 67% in July yet remains up roughly 80% year-to-date; Citadel acquired its public assets in fire-sale conditions.
  • The fund was named after Aschenbrenner's 165-page 2024 white paper on artificial general intelligence (AI systems more capable than humans at most tasks); he is 25.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Reads the collapse as a test of what happens when a concentrated thesis on AI's inevitability meets a market that stops agreeing.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Busy earnings week: SpaceX debut and Palantir

  • SpaceX reports its first results as a public company Tuesday, with an Aug. 6 lockup expiration that could free 20% of shares for sale.
  • SpaceX stock is down 30% from its $150 June IPO price, trading near $105 versus an all-time high of $225.64.
  • Palantir reports after the close Monday, with Wall Street expecting revenue growth above 80%.
  • AMD and Disney also report this week; stock futures pointed to a higher open Monday as August trading began.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) 2

1. S&P 500 enters its weakest seasonal stretch

  • August and September are the only months averaging negative returns since 1990, the year's weakest window.
  • ProCap Insights found that in the 17 of the last 36 years when Aug-Sept were negative, the S&P 500 rose in Q4 16 times — a 94% hit rate, with 2008 the lone exception.
  • The setup is unusual: the index is up 9% year-to-date at record highs and recovered from a 9% intra-year drawdown faster than prior midterm years, which historically post the weakest returns of the four-year presidential cycle.
  • The Nasdaq 100 just had its worst month since March 2025, suggesting seasonal churn may already be underway in tech, the market's most crowded corner for three years.
  • Fed uncertainty plus the stop-start Iran conflict are set to amplify volatility into the weak months.

2. Corporate and AI datapoints

  • Amazon topped $200 billion in single-quarter revenue for the first time in company history.
  • Chinese AI labs have matched or exceeded US peers on enterprise-relevant benchmarks this year.

  • An Apollo Global study found workers in high AI-exposure fields saw slower wage growth with no measurable job losses — wage compression (suppressed pay growth) rather than layoffs.
  • The burden falls hardest on the least economically secure workers.
