US and Japan stage rare joint yen-buying intervention

  • Japan and the US conducted their first joint yen-buying operation in 15 years (central banks selling dollars to buy yen to prop up the currency) after the dollar-yen rate weakened past 160.
  • Japan holds $1.14 trillion of US Treasury debt; a collapsing yen pressured Japanese institutions to sell Treasuries, which would push US yields — and mortgage and credit costs — higher.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent telegraphed the operation at a Camp David cabinet meeting; Trump called it 'a signal of friendship' with Japan and good for the global economy.
  • Both governments say they will not hesitate to intervene again; the yen surged on reports of further action beyond last week's move.
  • Treasury also published new criteria to identify currency manipulation, including off-balance-sheet transactions and disguised commercial activity by major trading partners.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Frames the move as a shift away from Treasury's historically hands-off currency stance toward an active intervention policy, with the pace of major interventions accelerating.

Bloomberg Notes the coordination stands out because currency interventions have become far less common globally in recent decades.

Coldcard firmware flaw drains ~1,082 BTC (~$70M) from Bitcoin self-custody users

  • A seed-generation vulnerability (seed = the master secret from which a wallet's private keys, the credentials that authorize Bitcoin transfers, are derived) let attackers reconstruct keys and empty wallets.
  • TLDR Crypto reports 1,082 BTC (~$70.2M) taken from nearly 1,200 addresses; CMC Spotlight puts confirmed losses at over $50M.
  • Affected devices span Coldcard Mk3/Mk4/Mk5 firmware versions — Coldcard is a popular hardware wallet, a dedicated offline device meant to keep keys away from internet-connected computers.
  • Maker Coinkite shipped emergency patched firmware and urges all users to update, generate fresh seeds, and test with a small transaction before moving full balances.
  • Incident revives the tension between keeping wallets 'air-gapped' (physically isolated from networks) and safely delivering firmware updates.

TLDR Crypto Coinkite's CEO suggested the bug may have been found via AI-assisted code review, implying a new attack surface for hardware wallet makers.

TLDR Crypto Links to a Galaxy Research technical analysis of the vulnerability.

CMC Spotlight Says exploitation generally required physical or USB access, though some reports hint at remote vectors.

CMC Spotlight Notes Coldcard also patched vulnerabilities in 2023 and 2024, and that rival hardware wallet makers are now auditing their own code.

CMC Spotlight Coldcard is working directly with affected users on fund recovery and probing whether the flaw was exploited before disclosure.

OpenAI's unreleased 'Astra' model solves 10 long-standing open math problems for ~$2,000

  • Astra cracked 10 previously open problems across mathematics, quantum complexity and theoretical computer science, several untouched for a decade or more (one open since 1999).
  • Total cost was roughly $2,000 in token/compute charges at current API rates, putting open research problems within reach of individual mathematicians' budgets.
  • Results are documented in a 249-page peer-reviewed paper.
  • Named results include proving non-sofic groups exist (a question about symmetry structures open since 1999), Alain Connes's rigidity conjecture, and three problems from Paul Erdős's list.
  • All 10 proofs were verified in Lean (a formal verification language that machine-checks whether a mathematical proof is logically correct).
  • Academics are debating whether machine-derived proofs deserve human-style recognition such as Fields Medal status, a question that will grow as AI attacks drug discovery and materials science.

The Rundown AI Anthropic's Levent Alpoge independently reproduced 5 of the 10 proofs using a different model (Fable) with only generic prompts and no internet access, suggesting the method generalizes.

  • DeepSeek V4 Flash costs $0.14 per million tokens vs. $5 for Claude Opus 5 — 36x less — and now pulls more OpenRouter traffic than Google and OpenAI combined, despite Anthropic's public worries about distillation (training a cheap model on a stronger model's outputs).
