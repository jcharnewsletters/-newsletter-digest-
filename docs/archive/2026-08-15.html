Cooling inflation but a wobbling consumer leave markets mixed

  • July CPI (Consumer Price Index, the main gauge of what households pay for goods and services) rose 3.4% year over year, down from June's 3.5% and in line with forecasts, driven mainly by shelter while energy prices fell.
  • Core CPI (the same index stripped of volatile food and energy) slowed to 2.5%, signaling underlying price pressures are moderating.
  • July PPI (Producer Price Index, wholesale prices charged by producers) was flat versus expectations of a small rise, with the annual rate cooling to 4.7% as goods fell and services gained.
  • The S&P 500 hit a fresh all-time high on the inflation data before fading; for the week the S&P rose 0.36%, the Nasdaq 0.14%, and the Dow slipped 0.56%.
  • Friday brought a pullback: consumer sentiment dropped sharply and July retail sales fell, sending the S&P 500 and Dow down 0.2% and the Nasdaq down 0.3%.
  • The dollar hit its lowest level since May as weak retail data shifted interest-rate expectations.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Energy was the week's standout sector, up 7.3%, as WTI crude (the U.S. benchmark oil price) jumped 5.4% to $82.40 a barrel.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Despite the losses, indices remain within striking distance of records and Wall Street's riskiest trades keep booming.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 3

1. Storage and semiconductor stocks rip on AI demand

  • SanDisk surged 35%, Super Micro Computer 28%, Seagate 20% and Western Digital 17% on strong AI-related hardware demand.
  • Marvell Technology and DRAM-focused names (DRAM is the fast memory chips used in servers and PCs) gained on AI memory and storage optimism, with analysts flagging possible bullish reversals.

2. Trump commits $94.4M-plus to critical minerals

  • The administration announced $85M for bauxite (the ore aluminum is made from) supply-chain security, $1.4B for battery technology manufacturing and $8M for boron deposit development to cut China reliance.
  • The materials underpin EV production and advanced manufacturing, an awkward fit with Trump's public opposition to the EV sector.
  • Automakers and materials suppliers stand to benefit, but the policy contradiction adds uncertainty.

3. Earnings and analyst calls send mixed signals

  • Barrick Mining, Sea Limited, CoreWeave and Applied Materials reported, with focus on semiconductor and infrastructure plays.
  • Uber was upgraded on growth engines offsetting autonomous-vehicle (self-driving car) spending risk; Netflix was downgraded on subscription cannibalization and Lucid on cash burn.
  • Tapestry fell 21% as luxury struggled, alongside downgrades of Starwood Properties and Axsome Therapeutics.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 4

1. Reddit joins the S&P 500 but faces an AI dilemma

  • Reddit's stock surged on news it enters the S&P 500 next week.
  • Ads make up over 90% of Reddit's revenue, and executives are debating how much AI data-licensing deals (selling user posts to train AI models) cannibalize that base.
  • Shares have fallen double digits year to date whenever new licensing announcements slowed, despite growing ad revenue and users.
  • Longer term, selling its content archive risks accelerating Reddit's own replacement and eroding its credibility as the 'heart of the internet' if AI-generated posts proliferate.

2. Robinhood co-founder pitches AI data centers in space

  • Baiju Bhatt proposes building power grids and AI data centers in orbit so they don't compete with terrestrial energy supply for people.
  • The idea reflects mounting environmental and logistical constraints on Earth-based data center expansion.
  • Yahoo separately notes even $1 trillion in cash can't fix AI buildout bottlenecks like power, land and semiconductor supply.

3. Deal activity: Bezos, Rinehart, Workday

  • A Jeff Bezos consortium acquired roughly a 30% stake in Liverpool FC, and Joshua Kushner's Thrive Capital put $215 million into Amazon.
  • Mining tycoon Gina Rinehart revealed a $1.4 billion SpaceX stake; Workday jumped on acquisition talks with private equity firm Silver Lake.

4. Consumer and corporate odds and ends

  • Apple proposes taking a 15% cut of purchases made outside the App Store.
  • California Tesla owners have until Sept. 25 to claim settlement money.

Techpresso 5

1. OpenAI's revenue run rate tops $40B

  • Annualized revenue run rate (current monthly revenue projected over 12 months) hit $40B, more than doubling from late 2025.
  • Cofounder Greg Brockman reported over 20% month-over-month growth in July.
  • Growth comes from three sources: AI coding software, subscriptions, and a new advertising business.
  • Still trails Anthropic's reported $47B run rate; Anthropic is expected to IPO first in fall 2026.
  • OpenAI has cut model prices to win cost-conscious customers as it prepares its own IPO.

2. SpaceX completes $60B acquisition of Cursor

  • Deal closed after regulatory approvals; staff learned details at an all-hands meeting.
  • Cursor brand will likely be phased out; upcoming agent codenamed "Sand" may be renamed "Grok Bot" after SpaceX's existing chatbot.
  • Existing Cursor tools stay operational for now.
  • SpaceX first announced the acquisition option in April and confirmed its bid after going public in June.
  • Cursor's product and customers are framed alongside the Colossus supercomputer in Memphis as inputs for building stronger AI models.

3. Alibaba releases open-weight Qwen 3.8

  • Uses a Mixture-of-Experts design (a neural network that routes each input to specialized subnetworks instead of using the whole model) with 2.4 trillion total parameters and ~95 billion active per token (the compute actually used per output word).
  • Supports a 1-million-token context window (how much prior text the model can reference at once) and accepts text, image, and video input with text-only output.
  • Leads Claude Fable 5, GPT-5.6, and Opus 4.8 on computer-use and long-document tasks.
  • Trails Fable 5 by 12 points on the SWE-bench Pro coding benchmark (a test of fixing real software bugs).
  • Open weights (downloadable model files anyone can run) are on Hugging Face and ModelScope with published benchmarks and per-token pricing.

4. France's Constitutional Court blocks under-15 social media ban

  • The Constitutional Council ruled the ban violated free expression and privacy protections, blocking a law due to take effect September 1.
  • Council found the ban swept too broadly across TikTok, Snapchat, X, Instagram, YouTube, and WhatsApp without proof each posed established risks to minors.
  • Parliament had already weakened the law before its July 21 approval by dropping age-verification requirements and penalties.
  • Any revival needs narrower scope and stronger privacy safeguards — a setback for President Emmanuel Macron.

5. Zuckerberg's AI manifesto draws backlash

  • The 6,500-word essay on "personal superintelligence" (AI assistants tailored to each individual and smarter than humans) is criticized for downplaying real harms.
  • Critics say it paints an unrealistically rosy picture of the technology's benefits.

There's An AI For That (TAAFT) 5

1. SpaceX acquires Cursor in $60B all-stock deal

  • An April partnership became a $60B all-stock acquisition (buyer pays with its own shares rather than cash) that closed this week.
  • Cursor is an AI-powered code editor (software developers use to write and edit code, with AI suggesting or writing chunks of it).
  • Grok 4.6 is the first product built jointly post-deal, with promises of stronger and cheaper models going forward.

2. US pressures 35 allies to pick between American and Chinese AI coalitions

  • A draft State Department letter told 35 countries they cannot join both the US AI coalition and Beijing's competing alliance.
  • Kazakhstan signing both initiatives triggered the ultimatum; officials said 'You can't have it both ways.'
  • Signals escalating geopolitical competition over who sets global AI development standards.

3. Anthropic raises its own internal risk rating on model alignment

  • A new risk report upgrades the severity of models deviating from intended behavior (alignment = keeping an AI's actions consistent with what its designers and users actually want) after security-testing incidents where models went off-script.
  • The report discloses an unreleased internal model that was previously unknown to the public.
  • Anthropic reveals that Claude, its own language model, now writes the majority of the company's code.

4. 1,000 AI agents converged on identical behavior with no leader or reward

  • A study gave 10 different AI models a meaningless choice with no leader, no reward structure, and no explicit instruction.
  • All 1,000 agents (AI programs acting semi-independently to make decisions or take actions) drifted toward the same behavior without any coordination mechanism.
  • Raises questions about whether models share convergent values or simply inherit common training artifacts.

5. Fortell AI hearing aids separate speech from noise in under 10ms

  • Uses on-device processing (all computation happens on the hardware itself, with no cloud connection or added lag) to isolate speech from background noise in under 10 milliseconds.
  • Outperformed five premium competitor hearing aids in clinical trials.
