Market Selloff: S&P 500 Enters Correction, Nasdaq Nears Bear Market

  • S&P 500 fell 8-8.6% for the week to 6,910, officially entering correction territory (a 10% decline from recent all-time highs set in October).
  • Dow dropped 8% to 48,147; Nasdaq Composite plunged 10% to 22,618, erasing almost all 2026 gains and approaching a bear market (a 20% drop from record highs).
  • S&P 500 fell every trading day in the week with at least four drops of 1% or more—a pattern seen only 5 times since 1945 (1962, 1974, 1978, post-9/11 2001, October 2008).
  • S&P 500 lost over $1 trillion in market value on Friday alone; chip stocks alone lost over $1 trillion in a single day.
  • Tech (Information Technology) sector hit hardest at -12.2%, while defensive sectors like Consumer Staples (-2%) and Utilities (-3.2%) were more resilient.
  • CBOE Volatility Index (VIX, a measure of market fear derived from options prices) surged 86% to 35.89.
  • Global indices also fell: Germany -7.3%, Japan -7.7%, France -5.3%, London -5.1%, while India gained +2.9%.
  • Gold fell 8.9% to $3,539/oz and crude oil WTI dropped 5.6% to $82.4/barrel; Bitcoin fell 10.3%, Ethereum 8.8%, Litecoin 13.2%.

Nvidia: Strong Earnings Beat Fails to Stop Stock Plunge

  • Nvidia reported non-GAAP EPS of $2.68 (beating estimates by $0.21) and revenue of $115.84B (beating estimates by $7.13B), with revenue more than doubling year-over-year.
  • Despite the beat, Nvidia stock fell 23% for the week and has shed $2.5 trillion in market value since its October 6 peak—the largest 45-day loss for any stock in history.
  • Other chip/memory makers also fell sharply: Micron down 24%, SanDisk down 28%, reflecting sector-wide weakness beyond company-specific results.

AI Code Generation Outpaces Human Review, Creating "Comprehension Debt"

  • AI tools like Claude Code let AI write code faster than human teams can review it, making full code review infeasible for many companies.
  • This creates "comprehension debt" (a loss of understanding of why systems are designed in specific ways), leaving engineers unable to diagnose problems when systems break.
  • Companies are substituting automated tests and AI-based review for human verification, removing a critical safety layer.
  • AI also helps hackers find and exploit bugs faster, compounding risk for systems run by under-resourced IT teams.
