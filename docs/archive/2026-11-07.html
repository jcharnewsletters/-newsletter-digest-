Market Reversal: Tech Selloff Overwhelms Post-Election Rally

  • Nasdaq down 3.4% for the week; semiconductor giants Micron, Intel, and AMD lost roughly 25% on AI policy fears following Democrats' midterm sweep of Congress.
  • Nasdaq Composite (a market index weighted toward technology companies) entered correction territory, down 10%+ from recent highs.
  • S&P 500, Nasdaq, and Dow all posted worst single-day losses since April 2025, erasing post-election rally gains.
  • Investors cited fears of AI regulation (artificial intelligence oversight by Congress) as the primary driver of tech weakness.

Economic Data: Jobs Strong but Services Growth Slows

  • October nonfarm payrolls (total U.S. private and government jobs added) rose by 165,000, more than double forecasts, signaling a resilient labor market.
  • ISM Services growth index ticked down in October as new order growth slowed, hinting at softening in the broader services economy.
  • Mixed data complicates Fed outlook: strong jobs support further tightening while services slowdown suggests spreading weakness.
  • CME FedWatch Tool now prices a 77.1% probability of a 25 basis point (0.25%) rate hike at the December FOMC meeting.

Sector-Specific Carnage: Hardware and Energy Hit Hardest

  • Semiconductors (computer chips) fell sharply: Micron, Lam Research, Intel, and AMD all lost about 25% on AI-related demand concerns.
  • Energy sector dropped 11.9% for the week as WTI crude fell 13.2% to $90.50/barrel and Brent fell 13.7% to $97.94/barrel.
  • Information Technology was the worst-performing S&P 500 sector, down 6.9% for the week, while Consumer Staples and Utilities posted small gains.
  • Storage/memory makers Seagate, Marvell, Western Digital, and Sandisk all fell 27-28% on weakening demand outlook.

Global Market Impact and Volatility Surge

  • International markets diverged: Japan fell 9.3%, while Hong Kong gained 5.1% and India surged 8.8%.
  • CBOE Volatility Index (VIX), a measure of expected stock market swings, spiked 32% to 25.64, indicating elevated investor fear.
  • Relative U.S. economic strength versus global weakness produced uneven sector impacts, hitting tech hardest while sparing some defensive names.

Individual Stock Moves: Winners and Losers

  • Apple, Palantir, and Qualcomm reported earnings during the selloff but faced sector-wide repricing headwinds.

Week's Bottom Line
