Anthropic's Record-Breaking IPO

  • AI company Anthropic (maker of the Claude chatbot) began trading on Nasdaq under ticker ANT, in the largest tech IPO on record.
  • Reported pricing and raise figures varied widely by outlet: CNBC cited $98/share raising $81.2B (~$980B valuation); Seeking Alpha cited $83/share raising $81.3B plus a $26B private placement for a $107.3B total deal (~$1.5T fully diluted valuation); Opening Bell cited $83/share raising ~$68.5B (~$1.08T valuation); Bloomberg cited a $107.3B combined public/private raise surpassing SpaceX's prior record.
  • The deal surpasses previous record IPOs (variously cited as Saudi Aramco's 2019 listing or SpaceX's private raise five months earlier).
  • Revenue is growing extremely fast (10x year-over-year to ~$16.3B per Seeking Alpha, or $17B per Opening Bell, roughly 12x prior year) but the company posted a massive net loss (~$80.7B per Seeking Alpha, ~$104B per Opening Bell), driven mainly by non-cash valuation/convertible-note charges rather than operating losses.
  • News of the IPO lifted Nasdaq futures (+2.1%) and broader tech and S&P 500 futures (+1.2%), reflecting investor enthusiasm for AI infrastructure plays.
  • The listing intensifies competition with OpenAI, Google, Meta, and Microsoft in the AI race, and Anthropic will be added to broad tech indices and potentially the S&P 500, giving institutional investors direct access.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Analysts flagged that Anthropic has $900.9B in compute (computing power/data center) commitments against only $35.3B trailing revenue—a fixed cost that doesn't shrink if sales disappoint.

Anthropic's record-breaking IPO

  • Anthropic priced its IPO at $83/share, raising $81-107 billion, making it the biggest IPO in history and a major inflection point for the AI industry.
02

OpenAI's Foundation & Microsoft Partnership (Dec 2015 & July 2019)

  • OpenAI founded as a nonprofit in Dec 2015 with Elon Musk and Sam Altman as co-chairs, primarily to prevent Google from reaching AGI (artificial general intelligence—AI systems matching or exceeding human capability across all domains).
  • In July 2019, OpenAI added a for-profit arm and took $1 billion from Microsoft, a structural shift that enabled scaling but seeded future internal conflicts.
03

The Transformer Breakthrough (June 2017)

  • Google Brain published 'Attention Is All You Need,' introducing the transformer architecture (a neural network design using attention mechanisms—methods letting a model focus on the most relevant parts of its input) that underlies all modern large language models.
  • Google had early access but underutilized the breakthrough; OpenAI recognized its potential and built GPT models on it, turning Google's missed opportunity into competitive advantage.
04

ChatGPT's Consumer Explosion (Nov 2022)

  • OpenAI released ChatGPT expecting tens of thousands of users; it reached 1 million users in 5 days and 100 million within 2 months, becoming the fastest-growing application ever.
  • Anthropic had held back its own chatbot since early 2022 over arms-race fears; a rumor of Anthropic's imminent release paradoxically pushed OpenAI to ship first, triggering the broader generative AI wave.
05

Sam Altman Ousted and Reinstated (Nov 2023)

  • OpenAI's board fired Altman citing lack of consistent candor but gave no detailed explanation; he was reinstated within 5 days after near-unanimous employee support and a Microsoft offer to hire him separately.
  • Microsoft's access to OpenAI models was capped at AGI declaration, motivating Microsoft to hire Mustafa Suleyman and build independent AI capability, fragmenting the former OpenAI-Microsoft duopoly.
06

Claude 3.5 Sonnet and the Rise of Agentic AI (June 2024 - Nov 2025)

  • Claude 3.5 Sonnet (June 2024) established Anthropic as the coding leader among AI labs.
  • Agentic capabilities (AI that can autonomously complete multi-step tasks rather than just respond to single prompts) later unlocked Claude Opus 4.5 (Nov 2025), shifting AI's pricing model from $20-30/seat to hundreds of dollars per user monthly.
07

DeepSeek's Market Shock (Jan 2025)

  • Chinese startup DeepSeek released its R1 reasoning model (trained via chain-of-thought inference, which requires the model to show step-by-step reasoning), claiming a $5.5 million training cost—a figure later seen as overstated.
  • The release sparked a $589 billion single-day market cap loss for Nvidia (a 17% drop), the largest one-day loss for any company ever.
  • Most Western users first encountered reasoning models through DeepSeek, since OpenAI's o1 had only been in limited preview and paid tiers; the event catalyzed US-vs-China framing of AI competition and changed labs' release strategies away from staged previews.
