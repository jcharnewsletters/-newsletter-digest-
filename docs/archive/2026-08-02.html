AI capital-spending fears become the market's main swing factor

  • Meta declined to give 2027 capital expenditure (capex — money spent on data centers, chips and other long-lived assets) guidance, triggering another post-earnings sell-off on worries about runaway AI infrastructure costs.
  • AMD's Q2 report Tuesday is the week's key read on AI accelerator (GPU — the specialized chips that train and run AI models) sales, data center demand, gross margins and forward guidance.
  • SK hynix analyst coverage begins this week as the post-IPO quiet period ends, making memory-chip demand from hyperscalers (giant cloud operators like Amazon, Microsoft and Google) a live indicator of the AI capex cycle.
  • Risk case: if major chipmakers signal hyperscalers are pulling back on AI spending, a broad equity selloff could follow.
  • Money is rotating out of compute-heavy tech toward large caps with scale, operational leverage and clear shareholder-return plans and no AI infrastructure bets.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Multiple semiconductor reports in the same week give a thematic read on whether AI infrastructure spending stays robust or moderates.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Frames it as an investing lesson: valuations are being destroyed by heavy AI spending with uncertain returns, and investors should hunt overlooked large caps with durable advantages instead.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 3

1. July jobs report (Friday) tests Fed rate-cut odds

  • Economists forecast 88,000 jobs added in July, up 54% from June's 57,000, suggesting labor-market stabilization.
  • Unemployment rate is expected to hold steady at 4.2%.
  • Weak data could pull forward Federal Reserve rate cuts; strong data could delay them.
  • Also due: Fed Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey (bank lending standards) Monday and NY Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations Friday.

2. SpaceX posts first public quarterly earnings (Tuesday)

  • Inaugural report as a public company, with focus on Starlink (its satellite internet service) subscriber growth and revenue trajectory.
  • Starship launch timeline updates are seen as critical to the valuation.
  • Capital spending plans will show how aggressively it funds Starlink, Starship and commercial services at once.
  • Elon Musk is expected on the call, a likely source of market-moving headlines.

3. Heavy earnings calendar across sectors

  • Monday: Palantir (AI/data software) and Snap (social advertising).
  • Tuesday–Wednesday: Merck, Pfizer, Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk (GLP-1 obesity drugs), Uber and Disney.
  • Thursday–Friday: ConocoPhillips, Airbnb, Shopify, plus Cloudflare and Warner Bros. Discovery.
  • Take-Two Interactive details the November launch of Grand Theft Auto VI on Friday; the Ai4 conference in Las Vegas Tuesday features OpenAI, Waymo and Cisco speakers.
Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 2

1. Starbucks turnaround accelerates under new leadership

  • Global comparable-store sales (sales at locations open at least a year) rose 7.9%, a fourth straight quarter of growth and well above expectations.
  • Adjusted earnings of $0.85 per share beat the $0.66 consensus on cost cuts and better store operations.
  • Operating margin expanded to 14.4% from 10.1%.
  • Full-year guidance was raised; the CEO credited restaurant basics and afternoon service expansion, with the stock near its July 2021 record high.

2. Coca-Cola grows on brand strength and volume

  • Net sales rose 7% year over year to $13.4 billion; comparable EPS jumped 11% to $0.97.
  • Global unit case volume rose 5%, with namesake Coca-Cola volume up 5% and Coca-Cola Zero Sugar up 16%.
  • Powerade volume rose 8% and Diet Coke plus Diet Coke Light rose 7% combined.
  • CFO said Diet Coke is 'having a moment,' pointing to multiple simultaneous growth drivers.
