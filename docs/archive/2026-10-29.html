Alphabet shares tumble after rare earnings miss tied to AI spending and ad cannibalization

  • Alphabet stock fell roughly 10% after Q3 EPS of $2.12 missed the $3.01 estimate, with services revenue of $82B below the $83.5B forecast.
  • Capital expenditure hit $51.4B for the quarter, and free cash flow swung from a $24.5B surplus a year ago to a $9.1B deficit (free cash flow = cash generated after operating and capital expenses, a key profitability gauge).
  • Google Cloud grew over 100%, but that growth hasn't offset weakness in core search advertising, the company's primary revenue engine.
  • Gemini AI app reached 1 billion users, and AI Mode in Search has gained traction, but both remain harder to monetize than traditional search ads.
  • The decline risks being Alphabet's largest single-day post-earnings loss and dragged down Nasdaq futures given its heavy index weighting.
  • Investors are increasingly worried that AI-powered search alternatives are structurally eating into Google's historically dominant ad business, not just causing temporary softness.

CNBC Morning Squawk Framed the move as Alphabet risking its largest-ever single-day post-earnings loss, with Nasdaq futures contagion highlighted as the lead concern.

Bloomberg Alphabet explicitly disclosed that its own AI search product is cannibalizing its core advertising business, rather than merely facing external AI competition.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Positioned Alphabet's miss within a broader 'Goldilocks' investor framework where tech firms spending too little or too much on AI both get punished, while balanced capex/monetization wins favor (as seen with Oracle and Microsoft).

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief Noted free cash flow deteriorated by a further $3B versus the prior quarter, intensifying pressure on Alphabet to justify AI spending.
