July inflation cools to 3.4%, clearing the way for a Fed hold

  • Consumer Price Index (CPI, the government's main gauge of what households pay for goods and services) rose 3.4% year-over-year in July, matching forecasts and down from 3.5% in June.
  • Core inflation (which excludes volatile food and energy prices) fell to 2.5%, a third straight monthly decline.
  • Prediction-market odds of no Fed rate change in September rose to 60% from 45% a week earlier, while rate-hike odds slipped to 34% from 40%.
  • Bond markets now price roughly one rate cut by year-end, down from three anticipated six weeks ago.
  • Services inflation remains elevated near 3.2%, keeping Fed officials divided on how fast prices are truly normalizing.
  • Weak July hiring data added to the case for standing pat, giving policymakers dual signals of cooling prices and softening jobs.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Fed Chair Kevin Warsh stayed hawkish, insisting the central bank won't let its 2% inflation target drift to 3%, though investors largely shrugged at the rhetoric.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Lettuce prices hit record lows (down over 40% year-over-year on bumper harvests), a concrete example of food-price deflation undercutting the two-year inflation narrative.
  3. Investopedia Pre-Market: Stock index futures (contracts letting traders bet on where stock prices will be later) traded slightly higher into the print.
  4. Bloomberg: Softer inflation plus AI momentum pushed global markets toward record highs.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg, Investopedia Pre-Market

AI earnings, not macro data, are driving the market

  • Nebius and CoreWeave each jumped about 19% on strong quarterly results, while the S&P 500 rose just 0.26% on the cooler CPI print.
  • Nebius revenue surged 454% to $582 million, with its AI cloud business growing more than 5x.
  • CoreWeave doubled revenue to $2.58 billion (up 112% year-over-year) and booked $25 billion in additional commitments.
  • Super Micro Computer raised fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $65–72 billion versus Wall Street's $52.5 billion estimate.
  • Mega-cap tech (the 'Magnificent Seven': Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Tesla, Nvidia, Meta) gained 2–3% as money rotated out of defensive positions.
  • Semiconductor and hardware names rallied on expectations of sustained AI data-center spending.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Tech giants have committed roughly $5 trillion of AI capital expenditure over five years, making a 0.25% shift in the federal funds rate (the interest rate banks charge each other overnight) economically immaterial to their plans.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Market breadth improved as mid-cap growth stocks joined the rally for the first time in three weeks.

Covered by: CNBC Morning Squawk, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

Paramount Skydance dangles CNN sale and a California exit to save $110B Warner Bros. deal

  • Chief legal officer Makan Delrahim said selling CNN is 'on the table' to settle the antitrust suit blocking the $110 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery.
  • The deal already has clearance from US federal regulators, the UK, EU and China; only the state lawsuit remains.
  • California AG Rob Bonta and 11 other states argue the combined company — Paramount's studios and CBS News plus HBO, DC Studios and TNT — would raise prices and cut competition.
  • Paramount faces a $7 million-per-day termination fee if the transaction doesn't close by October 1.
  • CEO David Ellison told leadership he would move the company out of California unless the state drops its claims, echoing Elon Musk's Tesla/SpaceX shift to Texas.
  • A relocation to Tennessee, Texas or Georgia could save about $500 million a year in taxes plus billions from selling studio lots.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Governor Gavin Newsom, worried about losing Hollywood, is reportedly pushing Bonta's office toward an out-of-court settlement; Paramount has also floated a board to guarantee CNN's editorial independence.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: A federal court has set a March trial date on the merger's legality, implying months of costly delay and risk the deal unravels; the states counter that a bigger Paramount would dominate TV and film.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief

LA Lakers sold for a record $12.5 billion

  • The purchase, at a record price for a sports franchise, was struck in roughly 24 hours with minimal investment-banker involvement.
  • Buyers were Thrive Capital founder Joshua Kushner and former Disney CEO Bob Iger, with Mark Walter selling.
  • Madison Square Garden Sports (MSGS) shares rallied on the read-through to other franchise valuations.
  1. Bloomberg: The sale helps Walter's TWG Global cut related-party loans at its insurance units, which had drawn Justice Department scrutiny; Iger quipped that avoiding 'hordes, investment bankers and the like' kept talks secret.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Frames the price as evidence wealthy investors treat teams as prestige assets with embedded real-estate and media value.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Anthropic in talks to buy AI startup Decart for ~$6 billion

  • The AI lab is negotiating a roughly $6 billion acquisition of Decart, one of the sector's larger consolidation moves.
  • The talks underscore continued investor appetite for artificial-intelligence assets and specialized capabilities.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

South Korea's Kospi flips from bear to bull market in two weeks

  • The Kospi index moved from bear-market territory (down 20%+ from a recent peak) into a technical bull market (up 20%+ from its low) in about two weeks.
  • The whipsaw reflects short-covering and bargain hunting off oversold levels amid the broader global AI-led rally.

Covered by: Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), Bloomberg

Energy: oil steady near $75 as Iran pressure builds, US pump prices set an August record

  • Crude held around $75 a barrel, with OPEC production cuts offsetting demand worries.
  • Oil wavered as traders waited on negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz after the Trump administration reverted to economic pressure on Iran following a failed military campaign.
  • US average gasoline topped $4 a gallon after August 12 for the first time on record for that date, per GasBuddy.
  • July gasoline prices fell 2.9% month-over-month but are still up 25% year-over-year.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Analyst Tom Kloza expects more supply interruptions and says the final third of the year will be 'considerably more expensive' than prior years.

Covered by: CNBC Morning Squawk, Bloomberg, Yahoo Finance Morning Brief
