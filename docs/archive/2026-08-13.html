July inflation cools to 3.4%, clearing the way for a Fed hold

  • Consumer Price Index (CPI, the government's main gauge of what households pay for goods and services) rose 3.4% year-over-year in July, matching forecasts and down from 3.5% in June.
  • Core inflation (which excludes volatile food and energy prices) fell to 2.5%, a third straight monthly decline.
  • Prediction-market odds of no Fed rate change in September rose to 60% from 45% a week earlier, while rate-hike odds slipped to 34% from 40%.
  • Bond markets now price roughly one rate cut by year-end, down from three anticipated six weeks ago.
  • Services inflation remains elevated near 3.2%, keeping Fed officials divided on how fast prices are truly normalizing.
  • Weak July hiring data added to the case for standing pat, giving policymakers dual signals of cooling prices and softening jobs.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Fed Chair Kevin Warsh stayed hawkish, insisting the central bank won't let its 2% inflation target drift to 3%, though investors largely shrugged at the rhetoric.
  2. CNBC Morning Squawk: Lettuce prices hit record lows (down over 40% year-over-year on bumper harvests), a concrete example of food-price deflation undercutting the two-year inflation narrative.
  3. Investopedia Pre-Market: Stock index futures (contracts letting traders bet on where stock prices will be later) traded slightly higher into the print.
  4. Bloomberg: Softer inflation plus AI momentum pushed global markets toward record highs.

TLDR Crypto 5

1. CFTC and SEC sue Goliath Ventures over $397M DeFi Ponzi scheme

  • The scheme ran November 2022–February 2026 across 1,600 customers, promising 36% annual yields on Bitcoin and Ethereum via DEX liquidity pools (automated trading protocols where users deposit assets to earn a share of trading fees).
  • No capital was ever deposited into any DeFi protocol; account statements were fabricated.
  • Funds were diverted as $174M in staff recruiting commissions, $87M in Ponzi payments to existing customers, and $48M to CEO Christopher Delgado for real estate, vehicles, and jewelry.
  • Delgado pleaded guilty in June to wire fraud conspiracy and money laundering; a bankruptcy receiver was appointed in March 2026.
  • The same-day dual civil filing shows CFTC Chairman Michael Selig asserting parallel jurisdiction alongside the SEC, signaling a turf expansion in crypto fraud enforcement.

2. Centralized crypto firms capture 8.5x more revenue than on-chain protocols

  • On-chain protocols generated $8B of revenue in 2025 versus $60–100B for centralized players such as exchanges and stablecoin issuers.
  • That gap implies an on-chain market cap of roughly $168B against ~$840B for centralized crypto — about the valuation of a single AI lab.
  • On-chain revenue is led by Layer 1/Layer 2 blockchains (L1 is the base chain that settles transactions; L2 bundles transactions off the base chain to cut fees) at 49%.
  • Off-chain revenue is dominated by exchanges and brokers (66%) and stablecoin issuers (19%).
  • Centralized players win on proximity to users and abstraction of complexity, pushing adoption to Coinbase, Robinhood, and Binance over self-custodial alternatives.

3. Tokenized stocks become the next battleground as traditional exchanges pile in

  • The tokenized stock market hit $1.9B, up 6.5x since January 2025, while tokenized Treasuries stalled at $15B.
  • The Linked Security structure (DeFi-composable tokens with no voting rights, sold only to non-US investors) dominates via Ondo (45.9%, $851M) and xStocks (26%, $482M).
  • DTCC launched limited QQQ/SPY tokenization on July 15 after SEC approval; Nasdaq won a rule-change approval in March 2026 and is building a permissionless gateway with Kraken parent Payward; NYSE filed a rule change plus an MOU with Securitize.
  • Coinbase announced a 1:1 stock-backed token for non-US customers but has not launched it.
  • A January 2026 SEC taxonomy of four structural models gave the sector its regulatory framework and triggered incumbent competition.

4. Neutrl's carry trade earns yield without the crypto cycle

  • Neutrl buys locked private company shares at a 15% discount and shorts an equity perpetual (a derivative contract with no expiry, where longs and shorts exchange periodic 'funding' payments) on the same name, hedging direction from day one.
  • Funding on the short leg averaged 10.9% annualized from May–July 2026, contributing to roughly 20.5 percentage points of total return over a six-month hold.
  • Traditional delta-neutral books (offsetting long and short positions with no net price exposure) earned least as altcoin volume fell 30% in H1 2026.
  • Boros protocol turns funding rates into standalone assets via Yield Units; a 4.03-point gap between HYPE fixed rates on Hyperliquid (7.93%) and Bybit (3.89%) locks in 40.30% APR at 10x leverage with no directional risk.

5. FlightAware drops Kalshi lawsuit one day after filing

  • The suit targeted prediction market Kalshi over unauthorized use of flight data in flight-cancellation markets.
  • The one-day withdrawal suggests a private settlement was reached.

  • Most users hand AI the final polished step instead of the research and ideation stages where it performs best.
  • Reframe tasks so AI does prep — research, brainstorming, wide exploration — while you handle cutting, editing and judging fit.
  • Tell the model upfront what format you work in (spreadsheet, Word doc, slide deck) so output lands where you finish work.
