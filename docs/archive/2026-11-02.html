Tech selloff: Alphabet, Intel lead mega-cap declines on AI spending fears

  • Alphabet lost roughly $500-530 billion in market cap in one session despite beating on search growth, as investors worried about AI eroding search ad monetization.
  • Intel shares plunged 28-29%, its worst trading day since 1974, erasing about $140 billion in value, even though its data-center/AI segment grew 78% year-on-year to $7.3 billion, beating estimates.
  • Analysts (Evercore ISI's Mark Mahaney) describe the rout as an 'expectations correction, not a fundamentals correction'—Amazon's AWS also beat estimates (40% growth to $45.9B) yet its stock sold off too.
  • Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta are each spending $100B-$200B annually on AI infrastructure (capex), pushing free cash flow negative and forcing bond sales (Microsoft $25B) and stock offerings (Meta $95B) to fund the buildout.
  • Oaktree's Howard Marks said the stumbles have deflated AI's 'halo of invincibility,' while Morgan Stanley's Michael Wilson told investors to prepare to add risk, framing the selloff as a possible buying opportunity.
  • Apple stands apart, keeping capex near $10B/year versus rivals' much larger spend, relying instead on third-party AI models and partners; new CEO John Ternus holds his first earnings call with hardware launches (touchscreen MacBooks, HomeView hub, foldable iPhone Duo) in focus.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Soaring memory chip costs (needed for AI hardware) are adding further pressure to already strained capex budgets across the industry.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Sector rotation accompanied the selloff: energy and consumer staples each gained over 10% in October while tech fell 8.2% and communication services dropped 9.9%; the equal-weight S&P 500 (an index weighting all 500 stocks equally rather than by size) beat the cap-weighted index by 5.8 points in October, the second-best such month since 1990.

Binance suffers record-breaking crypto hack, sparking withdrawal rush

  • Binance, a major cryptocurrency exchange, suffered the largest crypto heist in history, with reported losses ranging from $3.6B to $6.4B depending on the source.
  • The exchange briefly suspended withdrawals but pledged to 'cover all losses,' stating user funds are safe.
  • Despite assurances, customers pulled a net $3.6-4.7 billion from the platform after withdrawals resumed, and Bitcoin-linked tokens like Ethereum and Binance's own BNB token fell in response (ether down 4% to $2,200, BNB down 5%).
  • The FBI attributed the theft to North Korean hackers, who are reportedly converting stolen funds into Ethereum and plan to launder them via DeFi (decentralized finance: peer-to-peer financial services built on blockchain) and instant-swap services, possibly aided by AI tools.

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) Framed alongside Fitch's downgrade of France's credit outlook, citing high borrowing costs and a widening budget deficit, as part of a broader day of risk-off headlines.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief Clear Street's Owen Lau warned the industry lacks a regulatory framework to protect investors, and noted the crypto industry is pursuing legislation to align with banking-style standards.

Bloomberg Experts believe North Korean hackers have incorporated powerful AI tools into their operations, raising concern about more sophisticated future attacks.
