Big Tech rally: Mag 7 comeback powered by cloud earnings

  • The Magnificent 7 (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta) jumped 4%+ Monday via the Roundhill MAGS ETF, its best day since March, after being nearly flat for seven months.
  • S&P 500 rose 1.5% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 1.3% to a record high; six of seven mega-caps rose 3%+ while Apple fell ~1.8%.
  • Microsoft +4.9%, Amazon +4.6%, Alphabet +4.4%, Meta +6.1%, Oracle +9.6% on the session.
  • Microsoft Azure grew 43% to a $100B annual run rate, Amazon Web Services grew 37% to $42.2B, and Google Cloud surged 82% to $24.8B.
  • The three cloud giants added nearly $1.5 trillion in combined market value in a week; hyperscaler (large-scale AI data center operator) capex is projected up 70% this year to over $700 billion.
  • Mag 7 valuations had compressed to their cheapest level versus the market in years, setting up a 'catch-up trade'.
  1. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Cites a $2.3 trillion compute-demand backlog across the top four cloud providers and Iran conflict de-escalation as rally drivers; Goldman Sachs frames multiple compression as a major break from the dot-com era.
  2. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Nvidia reclaimed the title of world's most valuable company at a $5 trillion valuation.
  3. Bloomberg: S&P 500 futures up 0.1% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.7% premarket as focus shifts to earnings.
  4. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Alphabet posted its first-ever quarter of negative free cash flow, which investors shrugged off.

Covered by: Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, CNBC Morning Squawk, Bloomberg

Free cash flow becomes the key Big Tech metric as AI debt looms

  • Heavy data center spending has eroded the cash-generation profile that made Big Tech a safe haven; Alphabet and others are now showing negative free cash flow (cash left after capital expenditures).
  • Citadel Securities (Ken Griffin's firm) forecasts a $500 billion wave of new Big Tech borrowing for data center chips.
  • Analysts call the potential scale 'unprecedented,' possibly forming one of the largest new investment-grade credit segments (debt rated as high quality by ratings agencies).
  • Price-to-earnings multiples are compressing across mega-cap tech despite revenue growth, as investors broaden bets beyond the top five names.
  1. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Frames the market's shift from treating huge capex as a red flag to treating it as a growth catalyst.

Covered by: Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen)

Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha) 4

1. Missouri votes on eliminating its income tax

  • Amendment 5 would phase down Missouri's individual income tax rate with full elimination targeted for 2032.
  • Individual income taxes bring in over $9 billion a year, 63% of state general revenue in FY2025.
  • Lawmakers would get authority to expand and raise other levies, including the 4.225% state sales tax, to offset the loss.
  • Supporters cite competitiveness for business investment; opponents warn of cost-of-living hits and cuts to education, law enforcement, healthcare, and infrastructure.
  • Nine states have no income tax today: Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Texas, Washington, Wyoming, Alaska (1980), Tennessee (2021), New Hampshire (2025).
  • Wisconsin created the first modern state income tax in 1911; most states adopted them after the Great Depression and again after WWII.

2. Trump targets Exxon and Chevron profits

  • Trump called out Exxon (XOM) and Chevron (CVX) for 'making too much money,' signaling political pressure on oil profits.
  • The criticism lands amid elevated geopolitical tension affecting global oil supply.

1. China's AI companies close the gap with Silicon Valley

  • Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max and Moonshot's Kimi K3 have matched top American models; ByteDance and DeepSeek are advancing in video generation, pricing, reasoning, and coding.
  • The flood of low-cost launches is creating a 'death zone' for rivals lacking cutting-edge tech or market-breaking pricing.
  • The US threatened sanctions over alleged intellectual property theft and China vowed to respond, ahead of a planned summit.
  • New US limits on foreign-made robots and power inverters (devices that convert DC electricity to AC, a China-dominated sector) widen the dispute into core manufacturing.

2. Yen intervention seen as self-serving

  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent framed Friday's US intervention to support the yen as helping an ally.
  • The real concern is climbing US borrowing costs and potential forced selling of Treasuries by Japan, the largest overseas holder.
  • The yen is unwinding intervention gains, with traders questioning whether it can strengthen past 155 per dollar.
  • Tokyo–Washington coordination is at a level unseen in decades and may add leverage in trade talks.

3. Investors rotate toward European debt

  • Money is increasingly favoring European government bonds over US Treasuries (IOUs the federal government issues to borrow money).
