OpenAI's unreleased Astra model solves 10 open math problems — with big caveats

  • Astra produced the first improvement since 1978 to sphere-packing efficiency in high dimensions and built a "non-sofic group" that resolved Connes's rigidity conjecture.
  • Solutions were converted into Lean (a formal verification system where every logical step is machine-checked), with humans preparing the initial papers; each solution costs roughly $2,000 in tokens at standard API rates.
  • OpenAI published a 249-page paper, the Lean files, and model-written reasoning reconstructions, but did not disclose failed attempts, human guidance, or total compute.
  • Anthropic's Levent Alpöge showed Claude Fable reproduced about half of Astra's results within 24 hours using generic prompts, no web access, and full autonomy.
  • Gary Marcus cautions that checkable math (right-or-wrong feedback plus unlimited synthetic practice) does not prove general scientific reasoning for messy domains like cancer research or strategy.
  • Credibility would require pre-committed open-problem sets where every failure counts, rather than curated success stories.

The Neuron Frames it as a reusable research loop: humans pick verifiable problems, models search huge idea spaces with extreme compute, proof software checks answers — compressing years of trial-and-error into days.

TLDR AI Argues formally verifiable problems enable automated feedback loops, and whichever lab first achieves AI R&D self-improvement (AI advancing its own training) gains dominant advantage.

Careers work by emergence, not by long-term plans
