1. Tokenized stock volume up 288% in July, but one Binance token drove it

  • Tokenized stock trading (blockchain tokens that track the price of real equities) hit $11.3B in July, up 288% month-over-month.
  • Binance's QQQB token alone accounted for $9.27B (82%), helped by zero maker fees through August 31 and a VIP multiplier counting trades at 3x value.
  • Excluding QQQB, the sector shrank 30% versus June: xStocks fell from $1.55B to $335M, Ondo posted $792M, Backpack $479M.
  • The underlying QQQ ETF (tracks the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq companies) fell 6.6% in July, suggesting volume reflected fee incentives rather than real demand.

2. Uniswap v4 fee switch triples protocol revenue, UNI up 12%

  • The July 27 fee activation lifted daily protocol revenue from ~$114K to ~$325K within three days, with proceeds funding UNI token burns (permanently removing tokens from supply) — ~$28M since December.
  • Robinhood Chain produced over half of 24-hour revenue at $170K, ahead of Ethereum $81K (+82% week-over-week), Base $48K and Arbitrum $16K.
  • v4 charges traders 5 basis points (0.05%) on top of fees paid to liquidity providers (users who deposit tokens so others can trade), instead of taking a cut of LP earnings as earlier versions did.
  • Critics warn trade-routing aggregators will avoid pricier pools, pressuring LPs to cut their own fees and effectively absorb the protocol charge.
  • Governance approved the change with 46.6M votes across seven chains; UNI rose 12% in 24 hours to $4.40 and 16% on the week.

3. MetaDAO uses prediction markets to police treasury deals

  • Governance runs on conditional markets where traders forecast whether a proposal raises token value over a 3-day TWAP (time-weighted average price), with the first 24 hours excluded to deter manipulation.
  • Markets rejected discounted OTC (over-the-counter, privately negotiated) deals from Theia Research and DBA/Variant, forcing Theia to re-propose at a 38% premium before passage; a memecoin launchpad bid was also rejected.
  • Jito, Flash and Sanctum have adopted the framework, including Jito's fee switch and Flash's vote on sharing revenue with stakers (holders who lock tokens to earn protocol rewards).
  • Unlike Uniswap, Aave and ENS — where frontend fees accrue to labs entities — instructions baked onchain give token holders binding control over treasury flows.

4. Tether's excess reserves halve to $4.1B as Bitcoin and gold slide

  • Excess reserves (assets held above what is needed to back USDT one-for-one) fell 51% from $8.2B to $4.1B in Q2 2026.
  • Bitcoin and gold each depreciated more than 10%, cutting mark-to-market values (current market prices rather than purchase prices) on $5.8B of Bitcoin and $18.8B of precious metals.
  • Headline $1.5B net operating profit excludes unrealized asset value changes; including them, losses exceeded $4B, with H1 2026 net financial result at negative $3.2B.
  • USDT's user base hit a record 650M and the CEO called it a 'great quarter.'

5. Prediction markets are Robinhood's most lucrative trades

  • Prediction markets (bets on real-world event outcomes) generate ~$23 per $1,000 traded, versus $2.50 for crypto, $1.47 for options and $0.13 for stocks.
  • They are now Robinhood's largest transaction revenue source, a roughly 9x spread over crypto that shows unusually high fee tolerance among participants.

OpenAI's unreleased Astra model solves 10 open math problems — with big caveats

  • Astra produced the first improvement since 1978 to sphere-packing efficiency in high dimensions and built a "non-sofic group" that resolved Connes's rigidity conjecture.
  • Solutions were converted into Lean (a formal verification system where every logical step is machine-checked), with humans preparing the initial papers; each solution costs roughly $2,000 in tokens at standard API rates.
  • OpenAI published a 249-page paper, the Lean files, and model-written reasoning reconstructions, but did not disclose failed attempts, human guidance, or total compute.
  • Anthropic's Levent Alpöge showed Claude Fable reproduced about half of Astra's results within 24 hours using generic prompts, no web access, and full autonomy.
  • Gary Marcus cautions that checkable math (right-or-wrong feedback plus unlimited synthetic practice) does not prove general scientific reasoning for messy domains like cancer research or strategy.
  • Credibility would require pre-committed open-problem sets where every failure counts, rather than curated success stories.
  1. The Neuron: Frames it as a reusable research loop: humans pick verifiable problems, models search huge idea spaces with extreme compute, proof software checks answers — compressing years of trial-and-error into days.
  2. TLDR AI: Argues formally verifiable problems enable automated feedback loops, and whichever lab first achieves AI R&D self-improvement (AI advancing its own training) gains dominant advantage.

Covered by: The Neuron, TLDR AI

White House finalizes voluntary pre-release testing framework for frontier AI models

  • Framework gives the government access to frontier models (advanced systems with broad capabilities) up to 30 days before public release, designed under Trump's June 2 executive order.
  • OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Google were invited to a Tuesday meeting to review the finished framework and its classified benchmark.
  • Open implementation questions include what counts as frontier AI, whether open-source models are covered, and who leads testing.
  • Effort follows OpenAI and Anthropic agents breaching other companies; it echoes the EU AI Act's mandatory model reviews as 1,200+ AI staff call for slower frontier development.
  • Because participation is voluntary and standards are classified, outsiders won't know which companies showed up or what the tests found.
  1. The Neuron: Notes the framework explicitly treats frontier models as potential national-security or safety risks before deployment.
  2. Superhuman (Zain Kahn): Ties the meeting's timing to disclosures that ChatGPT accounts for nearly 90% of US House members' AI spending, with Claude second.

Covered by: The Rundown AI, The Neuron, Superhuman (Zain Kahn)

Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Max goes wide as open-weight frontier rival

DeepSeek V4-Flash resets AI inference pricing

  • V4-Flash matches Claude Opus 4.8-level performance at 28 cents per million output tokens versus $25 — roughly an 89x price gap.
  • Research firms peg it at 105x cheaper to operate than Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.
  • Gains came purely from stronger retraining — no increase in model size, architecture changes, or price — released under an MIT license.
  • US labs are responding: OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna and Terra prices last week and Google shipped an efficient model family this month.
  1. Superhuman (Zain Kahn): 'Diminishing model returns': Coinbase held AI spend flat while raising usage via model routing (sending routine tasks to cheaper models like GLM 5.2 and Kimi 2.7), and Microsoft is weighing DeepSeek V4 for Copilot Cowork.

Open-source video generation catches up: MiniMax H3, Seedance 2.5, Grok video

  • MiniMax H3 is an open-weight multimodal model that generates and edits 2K video with stereo audio from text, image, video, or audio inputs.
  • H3 topped Artificial Analysis video rankings — first in editing, second in text-to-video, third in image-to-video — as the first open-source model to do so.
  • Seedance 2.5 produces native 4K video up to 30 seconds with synchronized audio and up to 50 reference assets (free daily credits, $18/mo tier).
  • xAI's Grok added text-to-video that locks a character's face and voice across scenes using reference clips, at native 1080p.
  1. TLDR AI: Notes open models matching proprietary quality could democratize video generation for startups and researchers.

GPT-Live: OpenAI's full-duplex voice assistant

  • Rebuilt voice stack listens and speaks simultaneously via full-duplex (two-way at once) communication, removing turn-taking delays.
  • Architecture combines stateful inference (keeping context across interactions), asynchronous task delegation, dynamic context-window management (adjusting how much prior conversation is retained), and low-latency media routing.
  • It can search the web, use memory, and keep talking while background tasks run; priced at $8/mo after a free mini tier.
