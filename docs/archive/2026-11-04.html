Democrats sweep midterm elections, flipping Senate and House control

  • Democrats flipped at least 7 Senate seats in historically Republican strongholds, including Texas (James Talarico, first Democrat since 1988), Maine (Troy Jackson defeated Susan Collins), Kansas (Adam Hamilton, first since 1932), Iowa, South Carolina, North Carolina, and Ohio.
  • Party also retained House majority, giving Democrats full control of Congress and ending unified Republican rule in Washington.
  • Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa defeated incumbent Greg Abbott, a stunning upset seen as a blow to Elon Musk's political influence in the state.
  • Results are widely interpreted as a voter rebuke of President Trump's economic record, policy agenda, and the Iran war.
  • Trump told the New York Times he would accept the results and work with Democrats, congratulating them despite deflecting blame for the defeat.
  • Corporate donors who spent heavily backing Trump-aligned candidates — including Tesla, SpaceX, Meta, Amazon, and Apple — could face Democratic scrutiny now that Senate control gives subpoena power.

Democratic political gains threaten AI data center expansion

AMD posts strong Q3 earnings driven by AI chip demand

  • Q3 revenue hit a record $13.4B, up 45% year-over-year, beating the $13.09B estimate, with adjusted EPS of $2.09 versus $1.95 expected.
  • Data center segment (AI chips and server processors) revenue nearly doubled to $8.5B, now 64% of total revenue, up from 47% a year earlier.
