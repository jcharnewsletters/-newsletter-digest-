01

Molly Graham Updates 'Give Away Your Legos' Career Advice for the AI Era

  • Molly Graham's famous 'give away your Legos' framework (leaders delegating favorite tasks to develop team members) assumed delegation builds relationships and teaches skills through mentorship.
  • Delegating to AI is fundamentally different from delegating to humans because there's no one on the other end to grow, learn, or build connection with—removing the relationship-building benefit of traditional delegation.
  • Best managers now distinguish between tasks suited for AI automation versus those requiring human delegation for team-building and development.
02

Tech Industry Grief, Loneliness, and Burnout Amid AI Disruption

  • Tech workers in 2026 report grief and loneliness tied to rapid, uncertain change rather than simple job losses, with the industry described as 'splitting in two' between those adapting and those struggling.
  • Burnout stems less from job displacement and more from the psychological burden of constantly re-evaluating which skills matter and where humans remain irreplaceable.
  • Managers are advised to explicitly acknowledge this grief rather than push purely technical adaptation strategies.
03

Skills That Should Never Be Delegated to AI

  • Judgment calls requiring deep domain expertise, ethical decision-making, and accountability should remain with humans and not be delegated to AI systems.
  • Work that builds trust, mentorship, and organizational culture requires human connection and can't be effectively outsourced to AI regardless of its capability.
  • Senior leaders' roles are shifting toward curating which human capabilities remain irreplaceable rather than maximizing automation.
  • AI job displacement fears are overstated when framed as simple replacement; the real tension is between workers who adapt to leverage AI and those who resist without a clear path forward.

01

AI Helps Identify Genes to Bring Back the Woolly Mammoth

  • Asian elephants share 99.6% of DNA with woolly mammoths; AI pinpointed the remaining 0.4% of differences by comparing fragmented ancient mammoth DNA against elephant genomes.
  • Analysis across 121 mammoth and elephant genomes expanded the target gene list from 60 to about 150, covering traits like thicker coats, smaller ears, and shorter tails.
  • Editing these genes into mice produced 38 'woolly mice' with the predicted mammoth traits, validating the AI's gene picks; de-extinction could occur as early as the early 2030s.
02

AI Can De-Anonymize Forum Users From Public Posts Alone

  • ETH Zurich researchers stripped names and links from 338 forum users' posts, then used AI web searches to identify 226 of them (67%), reaching ~90% accuracy on confident matches.
  • Users who mentioned just one detail (e.g., one film) were hard to match, but those mentioning 10+ similar details were identified about 50% of the time.
  • No hacking was involved—every clue came from publicly posted information, showing how small details can combine to reveal identity.
03

AI Agent Designs and Runs Its Own Physics Lab

  • An MIT professor gave an AI agent five images of layered materials and one question about breaking behavior; the agent built its own shape generator, physics simulator, and crack tester in a browser app.
  • The agent planned and predicted experiment outcomes before running them; three independent runs gave different conclusions, but consistently found that layer arrangement and load-sharing matter more than thickness alone.
  • Hundreds of AI agents kept experimenting autonomously; results are simulation-based and unpublished, not yet tested on real materials.
04

Museums Adopting AI Without Policies or Training

  • A UNESCO/ICOM survey of 400+ museums in 90 countries found 57% use AI for administration (70%), communication (62%), and research (43%), but 55% have no AI policy or guidelines.
  • Only 17% of museums offer staff AI training, leaving most staff to adopt tools informally through trial and error.
  • Top shared concerns are factual errors, copyright violations, data protection breaches, and embedded bias, with museums lacking practical guidance and peer knowledge-sharing.
