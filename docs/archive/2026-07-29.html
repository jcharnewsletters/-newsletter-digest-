Fed decision day: hold expected, but a surprise hike is live

  • The FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee, the 12-official Fed panel that votes on U.S. interest rates) announces its decision at 2 p.m. ET, with consensus for no change to the 3.50%–3.75% target range.
  • Futures markets put roughly a 70% chance on a hold, while CME FedWatch (a tool that infers rate odds from futures prices) shows hike odds up to 32% from 25.7% a week ago.
  • Citadel Securities is calling outright for a surprise hike, an unusual break from consensus that has amplified market nervousness.
  • The interest rate swap market (contracts pegged to future rates) prices zero probability of a cut, despite President Trump's repeated demands for the world's lowest rates.
  • It is only the second meeting chaired by Kevin Warsh, who has deliberately avoided signaling the future rate path; at least two hawkish dissents are anticipated.
  • Because both a hike and a hold are seen as consequential, volatility is likely either way — and the risk lands hardest on high-multiple tech stocks reporting the same day.
  1. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Warsh frames internal disagreement as a 'good family fight'; Dallas Fed's Lorie Logan and Cleveland's Beth Hammack are the likeliest hike dissenters, with Neel Kashkari possibly leaning that way, while Evercore ISI expects Warsh to build inflation credibility first, then pivot to institutional reform and the balance sheet.
  2. Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha): Citi's Andrew Hollenhorst cites flat June core CPI (consumer price index excluding food and energy) implying just 0.18% month-over-month core PCE as evidence the economy isn't overheating; Truflation sees rates unchanged through the rest of 2026 and Seeking Alpha's Lawrence Fuller sees no hike Wednesday or in September ahead of midterms.
  3. Yahoo Finance Morning Brief: Argues rate policy may no longer 'work' — a 4.6% Treasury yield no longer rattles equity investors and high real rates (interest minus inflation, the true cost of borrowing) point to an economy adapted to expensive money; Thursday's June PCE is expected at +3.6% year-over-year (core +3.3%), down from +4.1%.
  4. Bloomberg: Speculative positioning in rate futures has hit a record high, so any surprise could force a violent unwind.
  5. CNBC Morning Squawk: Powell's post-decision press conference is flagged as the bigger market mover, since it signals the path of rates for the rest of 2026; rate changes reach consumers within weeks via credit card APRs and variable loans.

Covered by: Investopedia Pre-Market, Wall Street Breakfast (Seeking Alpha), CNBC Morning Squawk, Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen), Yahoo Finance Morning Brief, Bloomberg

Microsoft and Meta earnings: the AI capex test

  • Both report after Wednesday's close, the biggest test yet of AI capital spending versus actual cloud and advertising revenue growth.
  • Options markets (contracts whose prices imply how big a move traders expect) are pricing sizable one-day post-earnings swings for each.
  • Meta has committed $145 billion to AI investment this year with no clear payoff yet; Zuckerberg says it may sell surplus compute (unused AI data-center processing power), effectively becoming a discount cloud provider.
  • Microsoft faces the opposite squeeze — insufficient data-center capacity to meet AI demand — while investors worry about spending levels, with talk capex could slow next year.
  • Alphabet's disappointing capital-spending figures last week set a negative tone for the pair.
  • Reports land the same day as the Fed decision, compounding index-level volatility.
  1. Bloomberg: Frames the central question as whether debt-funded data-center buildouts can generate adequate returns.
  2. Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen): Magnificent 7 cash flows have collapsed, pushing Big Tech from self-funding cash cows to issuing equity and bonds — a trend expected to last two more years.

CoinDesk

1. $5B of bitcoin call options cluster at $70K–$72K

  • Nearly $5 billion in bitcoin call options (contracts betting the price rises) sits at the $70,000 and $72,000 strikes on Deribit — 18% of all BTC options open interest there.
  • Calls outnumber puts more than 10-to-1: ~39,000 calls vs 3,800 puts at $70,000, and ~37,900 calls vs 1,200 puts at $72,000.
  • The dominant trade is a bull call spread — buying the $70,000 call and selling the $72,000 call — making up ~49–50% of call open interest at those strikes.
  • One trader or group paid $3.4 million in premium for outright $70,000 calls.
  • Positioning rests on hopes the CLARITY Act (U.S. crypto market-structure bill) passes this month, but Polymarket odds fell from 51% to 38% after Senate Majority Leader Thune said it will miss the August recess.

2. EU's 21st Russia sanctions package hits crypto

  • Targets the A7 cross-border payments network, which Chainalysis says has processed ~$120 billion and was built for sanctions evasion, plus its A7A5 stablecoin.
  • Fourteen crypto service platforms in Georgia, Panama, the UAE, the Marshall Islands, Kyrgyzstan and Belarus are barred from transacting with EU entities.
  • A new instrument allows a blanket ban on transactions between any EU crypto provider and any crypto service used by Russia — the EU's first full third-country crypto ban option.
  • Also freezes assets for 94 banks, extends bans to 33 more Russian credit institutions, and hits 40+ shadow-fleet vessels.
  • Lands three days after Russia's Duma passed its own crypto framework, effective September 1.

3. Memecoins fade as institutions move in

  • DOGE and SHIB combined are now worth just 1.02% of bitcoin's market cap, down from 7% at the 2021 peak.

CMC Spotlight

1. Morgan Stanley launches spot Ethereum and Solana ETFs

  • Morgan Stanley launched spot Ethereum and Solana ETFs (exchange-traded funds: stock-market-listed baskets that hold the asset directly, so buyers get price exposure without holding crypto), giving wealth-management clients access via ordinary brokerage accounts.
  • As one of the largest U.S. wealth managers, its approval opens ETH and SOL to advisers who cannot buy tokens on crypto exchanges.
  • Spot ETFs create structural buying because the fund must purchase and hold the underlying token for every share issued.
  • Ethereum and Solana are the two most-used smart-contract blockchains (networks that run programmable applications, not just payments), making them the natural next listings after Bitcoin.
  • The issue carried only the headline and CoinMarketCap boilerplate — no tickers, fee levels or launch-day inflows were disclosed.

TLDR Crypto

1. SK Hynix perps flash-crash on Hyperliquid an hour before the stock did

  • SK Hynix perpetual futures (crypto derivatives that track an asset's price with no expiry date) fell 20% to $900 between 23:00 and 23:01 UTC on July 28, recovering above $1,000 within a minute; analysts blamed thin overnight liquidity.
  • One hour later SK Hynix stock opened down 15% in Korea, with the Kospi off 11% and Samsung and Hyundai also falling — the perps market priced the drop first.
  • Root cause: Nvidia fell 5% on reports of a $250 billion data-center commitment to an OpenAI-affiliated venture, dragging SK Hynix (a top supplier of high-bandwidth memory chips for AI processors) to a ~48% decline from its June 26 peak.

2. Visa launches Stablecoin Platform with Open USD

  • The Visa Stablecoin Platform lets banks and fintechs mint, burn and manage their own stablecoins (crypto tokens pegged 1:1 to a currency like the dollar) in one Visa-run environment, with maker/checker approvals, passkey signing and wallet-as-a-service.
  • Launch partner is bank-governed Open USD rather than incumbents Circle (USDC) or Tether (USDT).
  • EY Parthenon found 63% of corporates want stablecoin access through their own bank.
  • Supported chains are Ethereum, Solana and Tempo; the model mirrors Visa DPS, which lets thousands of card issuers process debit without building infrastructure.

3. Bitmine nears 5% of all Ether

  • Bitmine Immersion holds 5.79 million ETH (~4.8% of circulating supply), worth ~$11.3 billion, making it the largest corporate Ether holder among public companies.
  • Weekly buying accelerated to 9,946 ETH from 7,430 the prior week.
  • About 85% (4.9 million ETH) is staked via its MAVAN validator network (staking = locking coins to help secure the network in exchange for interest-like rewards), projected at $299 million in annualized rewards at full deployment.
  • Total assets are $11.8 billion including 208 BTC, $268 million in cash and securities, and stakes in Beast Industries and Eightco; it has repurchased 11.6 million shares since July 1 under a $4 billion buyback.

4. Base ships Base Verify Onchain

  • Coinbase's Base blockchain launched Base Verify Onchain, letting apps enforce one-claim-per-real-person for airdrops and voting without exposing users' personal data.

5. Neobank audit: 368 firms, 1.46 billion users

  • Asia holds 817 million of 1.46 billion neobank customers; WeBank alone exceeds 400 million and Nubank's 131 million Latin American users outnumber the entire U.S. neobank base, while Revolut's 50 million European accounts look small by comparison.
  • Only 127 of 368 hold a full banking license; the rest rely on sponsor banks and banking-as-a-service middlemen, whose failures have pushed customers into bankruptcy proceedings instead of deposit insurance when the ledger of whose money was whose broke.
  • 30% of neobanks founded in the 2020s are web3-native self-custodial apps (users hold their own keys) versus 4% of the 2010s cohort.
  • Only 67 of 368 (18%) have verified production AI deployments, led by thin-file lenders in Nigeria, the Philippines and Mexico.
