Weak September Jobs Report Shifts Fed Rate Hike Expectations

  • September nonfarm payrolls (total new jobs added to the economy) came in at just 29K, far below the 85K consensus forecast, down sharply from August's revised 133K.
  • Unemployment rate rose to 4.2% as labor market cooled.
  • Odds of an October 28 Fed rate hike (an increase in the central bank's benchmark interest rate) collapsed from roughly 64% a week earlier to about 18-20%, with markets now pricing ~80% probability the Fed holds rates steady.
  • PCE inflation data also cooled alongside the jobs report, reinforcing the case against a near-term hike.
  • Major indexes rallied on the weak data: Dow up 1.2-1.3%, S&P 500 up 0.3-0.7% (sources differ), Nasdaq up 0.5%, as investors interpreted slower hiring as easing inflation pressure.
  • Despite the jobs miss, long-term Treasury yields stayed elevated — the 10-year yield rebounded to 5.3% after an initial dip, and the 30-year yield hit its highest level since 2002 — signaling persistent bond market pressure from debt and inflation concerns.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief Economist Mohamed El-Erian argues the Fed cannot fix the economy alone: fiscal policy and deficit reduction are equally necessary, since the U.S. is running a 6% of GDP deficit (government spending exceeding revenue by 6% of total economic output) during strong economic times, which he calls 'ridiculous' and a problem monetary policy can't solve.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief El-Erian warns reliance on rate policy alone creates a 'K-shaped' recovery, where wealthy households benefit while higher rates disproportionately hurt lower-income Americans via housing and auto loan costs.
