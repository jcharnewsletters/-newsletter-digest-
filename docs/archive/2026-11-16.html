Nvidia Leadership Crisis: Jensen Huang's Exit and Calls for Shakeup

  • Jensen Huang resigned/was removed as CEO after revelations of affairs, including with a Chinese national suspected of espionage and two company employees; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly called for a 'dramatic shakeup' of Nvidia's leadership and demanded the company clarify its compliance with export controls (government restrictions limiting sales of advanced chips to certain countries) on AI chips.
  • Nvidia reports quarterly earnings Tuesday; analysts have cut price targets citing risks including further management departures, H200 chip write-downs, channel revenue adjustments, litigation, and potential 2029 market-share loss as customers seek alternative suppliers.
  • Elon Musk alleged many of Nvidia's critical supplier deals are informal handshake agreements tied to Huang's personal relationships rather than enforceable written contracts, raising counterparty risk (the chance a business partner fails to honor a deal) concerns.

  • Nvidia still committed $10 billion to Anthropic's pre-IPO funding round despite the leadership turmoil.

Anthropic IPO Debut

  • Anthropic, the first pure-play generative AI (artificial intelligence that creates new text, images, or other content) company to go public, priced its IPO at $70–$78 per share.
  • Valuation reached roughly $1.3–$1.45 trillion, below the earlier-hoped $2 trillion figure, with trading under ticker 'ANT' set to begin Thursday.
  • Nine-month revenue grew 1,206% to $33.2 billion, but the company disclosed $895.4 billion in aggregate committed spending, mainly for compute and infrastructure.
  • A $26 billion private placement round included $10 billion each from Nvidia and Alphabet, plus $6 billion combined from Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft.
  • Retail investor demand pushed the offering to six times oversubscribed, with investors using tactics honed during SpaceX's IPO; the company may reserve up to 20% of shares for retail buyers, though heavy institutional demand could shrink individual allocations.

Yahoo Finance Morning Brief 825 million shares are being sold in the offering, and Nvidia's investment is seen as providing institutional credibility despite the Huang scandal.

FAA Nationwide Ground Stop and Air Traffic Control Crisis

  • A nationwide ground stop was triggered Friday after the aging air traffic control system was overwhelmed by a surge of connection requests (roughly 1,000 attempts every 4–5 seconds), causing radar feeds and plane communications to glitch.
  • Over 12,460 flights were canceled Friday, with more than 6,700 additional cancellations Saturday and 1,400+ Sunday, per FlightAware; over 10,000 flights remained delayed as of late Sunday.
  • Investigators traced the data surge to two sites in Texas and Arkansas (near El Dorado) where contractor L3Harris had just deployed new networking routers from Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE); the system stabilized once both locations were blocked and a broadcasting function was disabled.
  • The FAA found no evidence of a cyberattack; officials are still investigating whether a faulty installation, configuration error, or hardware defect caused the surge, while HPE maintains its equipment did not fail.
  • Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy is requesting $50 billion in emergency funding from Congress to modernize air traffic control infrastructure, up from his earlier $30 billion ask and last year's $12.5 billion appropriation, targeting software and systems upgrades beyond last year's hardware-focused funding.
  • The outage follows a similar 2023 ground stop and years of ongoing copper-to-modern-network transition work, adding political pressure for a comprehensive overhaul.

Opening Bell Daily (Phil Rosen) Widespread flight disruptions are expected to ripple through supply chains, tourism, and business travel, creating near-term economic drag.

Bloomberg The FAA expected full operational recovery by Monday after the multi-day disruption.

Berkshire Hathaway's Record Run Echoes Dot-Com Era

  • Berkshire Hathaway shares are up 18% in 2026, nearly double the S&P 500's 10.5% gain, and the stock has closed at record highs four consecutive days, up 17.3% since the October 6 market peak while the S&P 500 fell 3.3%.
  • The pattern mirrors 2000: Berkshire fell 19.9% in 1999 while the S&P 500 gained 20%, then Berkshire's outperformance began March 10, 2000 (the exact day the Nasdaq peaked at 5,048.62), returning 29.7% cumulatively from 2000–2002 versus the S&P 500's -37.6%.
  • A key difference this time: Berkshire trailed the index by only 7 percentage points in 2025 versus 40 points in 1999, suggesting a less extreme reversal setup than the dot-com crash; for comparison, Berkshire beat the S&P 500 by 22.1 points in 2022, yet the index still returned 26.3% in 2023.
  • Capital flowing into Berkshire typically signals investors seeking defensive positioning, but historical patterns don't reliably predict downturns.
